## 4. Exact interpretation of F13

F13's registered text is: *"all worlds are non-discriminating"* (protocol §7;
plan `falsifiers.F13`). The committed computation (`exp_v30_o14.py`) is: a
world is *discriminating* iff at either registered timestep

- the A-versus-B absolute capability cost exceeds the numerical tolerance, or
- the D-versus-B post-burn-in service ratio differs from 1 beyond tolerance.

F13 fires iff the discriminating-world list is empty. It fired with evidence
exactly `discriminating_worlds = []`.

**What F13 means:** the registered *world family* failed to make the
registered *outcome-level* discriminator respond to the capability
restriction or to quote-driven destination choice. It is a statement about
the sensitivity of the (worlds × outcome-metric) instrument, reported
honestly as a falsifier of the study design's discriminating power.

**What F13 does not mean:** it does not mean every arm behaved identically.
Choice-level discrimination demonstrably occurred (W2 t0 divergence, W5
switching, D's EBU differing from C's), and arm S under-served in W1. F13 is
silent about S by construction: the registered discriminator reads only the
A-versus-B cost and the D-versus-B ratio.

### Why S's W1 result does not contradict F13

Arm S enters only the registered *secondary attribution* comparison
(`S_vs_B_and_D_secondary`, marked "informational only" in the committed
summary); it is not part of the F13 predicate. There is no tension: F13
asserts non-discrimination of the primary comparisons, and S's under-service
is a fact about a different, registered, secondary comparison.

Scientifically, S's W1 result *strengthens* the interpretation of F13 rather
than undermining it: it is a committed, in-run demonstration that the outcome
functional was **not vacuously saturated** in these worlds. A policy that
fails to track destination buffers (S is stock-blind by declared limitation)
did exit the buffered regime and was correctly detected and classified
(`distributive_or_policy_under_service`). The primary arms B and D stayed
inside the buffered regime because both are deficit-seeking (§7.3), not
because the metric could not move.

## 7. Buffering diagnosis

All statements below are derived from the committed bounded-service semantics
(`service_v30.bounded_step`, steps 1–10, unmodified in O14) and the committed
O14 artifacts. No new dynamics were run.

### 7.1 The mechanism

Per tick, for each destination cell `j` (destinations have no drive and no
out-edges in the registered worlds):

```
avail_j(t)   = x_j(t) + δ_j(t)              δ_j(t) = dt · Σ_e η_e q_e(t)   (deliveries)
dem_j(t)     = dt · d_j                      (fresh each tick; not carried over)
service_j(t) = min(dem_j(t), avail_j(t))
x_j(t+1)     = avail_j(t) − service_j(t)
```

The destination stock `x_j` is an **integrating reservoir**: deliveries are
banked, demand is drawn from the bank, and the service read-out saturates at
the demand cap. While `avail_j(t) ≥ dem_j(t)` at every tick of the
measurement window `W` (the *buffered regime*), service equals the demand cap
identically and cumulative service is

```
S_j(W) = |W| · dt · d_j        — a world constant, independent of the policy.
```

Service depends on the delivery schedule **only through the event
`avail_j(t) < dem_j(t)`**, i.e. only through exhaustion of the running
buffer. Equivalently: the delivery schedule `{δ_j(t)}` produces full service
iff the worst running shortfall of cumulative deliveries against cumulative
demand never exceeds the entering buffer,

```
x_j(t₀) + Σ_{s≤t} (δ_j(s) − dt·d_j) ≥ 0     for every t in the window.
```

The outcome functional is therefore a **plateau**: it has exactly zero
sensitivity to the delivery pattern everywhere in the interior of this
constraint set, and positive sensitivity only on its boundary. Different
delivery patterns produce identical bounded service precisely when (i)
destinations enter the window with sufficient stored stock, (ii) demand is
capped per tick, (iii) service is drawn from banked destination stock, and
(iv) the post-burn-in window gives the running sums time to absorb pattern
differences — the four conditions this task names, all present by
construction in the registered worlds (50-tick burn-in filled the buffers
before measurement began).

### 7.3 Why B and D never left the plateau — and why the restriction cost nothing

Two committed observations complete the mechanism:

1. **Temporal multiplexing.** The one-action restriction limits
   *simultaneity*, not time-averaged throughput. The committed trace shows
   arm B alternating destinations continuously: in W1 (conservative) B used
   edge 0 for 79 ticks and edge 1 for 121 with 151 switches; in W4 B served
   the four destinations in an exact 50/50/50/50 round-robin (199 switches).
   A destination served every k-th tick, with per-selected-tick delivery
   ≥ k × per-tick demand and buffer ≥ the multiplexing ripple, is
   outcome-indistinguishable from one served every tick. In W4 the initial
   per-selection delivery rate η·J = 0.9 × 2.675 = 2.4075 exceeds the total
   demand rate 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 — time-division covers all four destinations
   even though arm A's aggregate budget genuinely binds (σ_A < 1). This is
   why H3's zero is *correct* and not an artifact: capability cost at the
   outcome level requires cumulative infeasibility of multiplexed delivery,
   not merely instantaneous binding.
2. **Deficit-seeking feedback keeps buffers alive.** Both B's force rule and
   D's exact-quote rule increase monotonically in the destination's deficit:
   an unserved destination's stock drains, its force and its quote rise, and
   it eventually wins selection before its buffer empties — a self-stabilizing
   longest-deficit-first polling loop, stable whenever deliverable capacity
   exceeds total demand (true in every registered feasible world by the
   protocol's static feasibility bounds). The committed margins show the
   plateau was thin, not vacuous: B's worst post-burn-in destination stock in
   W1 was **0.368** stock units above empty. Arm S — whose score is
   stock-blind — mis-allocated (110/90 selections vs B's 79/121), drove that
   same buffer to exactly **0.0**, and accrued the 4.315 unmet. The plateau
   boundary is reachable in these very worlds; B and D avoided it *because*
   they track deficits, not because it is unreachable.

### 7.4 Analytical conditions under which destination choice becomes outcome-discriminating

Directly from the running-buffer inequality, outcome-level discrimination
requires making buffer-exhaustion events attainable and policy-dependent.
Candidate dimensions (**for a future preregistration only** — no numerical
parameter is chosen or endorsed here, and none was explored by trajectory):

1. **Insufficient destination buffers** — entering stock (initial value
   and/or burn-in accumulation) below the multiplexing ripple, on the order
   of (k−1) ticks of demand for k-way alternation, so that serving
   destinations in the wrong order exhausts a buffer.
2. **Per-edge deliverable rate near or below multiplexed demand** — when a
   single selection per tick cannot bank k ticks of demand, the one-action
   restriction becomes cumulatively binding and A-versus-B must separate.
3. **Perishable or leaking destination stock** — a destination-side decay
   term makes banked deliveries depreciate; the running-sum equivalence
   breaks and delivery *timing* (early vs just-in-time) becomes
   outcome-relevant.
4. **Service drawn directly from delivered flow** — removing (or sharply
   limiting) the stock-banking channel makes service read the instantaneous
   allocation, so every selection difference is visible at the outcome.
5. **Temporally concentrated demand** — demand pulses larger than
   buffer-plus-one-tick-inflow make pre-positioning decisions discriminating;
   the registered constant-rate demand is maximally forgiving.
6. **Inability to defer service across ticks** — in the current semantics
   demand is already per-tick fresh; the forgiving element is delivery
   banking. Tightening means constraining the bank (1–4) or making demand
   lumpy relative to it (5), not changing the demand-freshness rule alone.

Any world built along these dimensions is a **new preregistered study** with
its own protocol, plan, hashes and falsifiers — never a correction or
extension of the completed O14 execution. Changes to service semantics
(3, 4) are semantics extensions and must be registered as such.

## 9. Gate 1E and Gate 2 implications

**Gate 1E (registered robust-quote programme, Gate 1D diagnosis §10).** Its
*accounting-theoretic* wing — quote epochs, expiry conditions, robust
allowance `Q_max^rob`, interval quotes, and the proof obligations against
silent shrinkage — does not depend on outcome discrimination and is
unaffected by F13. Its *empirical* wing (does robust quoting preserve service
under latency/uncertainty?) would inherit the same outcome instrument that
O14 has just shown to be saturated for deficit-seeking policies in buffered
worlds. Executing Gate 1E's empirical comparisons first would risk a second
F13-class null, now confounded with latency and uncertainty dimensions.
**Verdict: Gate 1E remains registered and next-in-line, but is not ready to
execute its empirical service comparisons until an outcome instrument with
demonstrated discriminating power exists** (§10–§11).

**Gate 2 (actor economy) — remains PAUSED, unchanged.** The Gate 1D pause
condition is not met: whether exact-quote maximization can reward
under-service is precisely the question the saturated instrument could not
answer (C3 remains unresolved outside the buffered regime). No O14 outcome
authorizes wallets, health, prices, markets or learning; cumulative signed
EBU remains an evaluation variable, not a wallet.

## 10. The three sequencing options

**Option 1 — proceed directly to the registered Gate 1E robust-quote
programme.**

- *Scientific benefit:* advances O5 (robust quoting) and the
  accounting-theoretic obligations, which are real and F13-independent.
- *Confounding risk:* high for its empirical wing — latency/noise effects on
  service would be measured with an instrument known to be flat over the
  relevant policy region; a null would be uninterpretable (robustness vs
  saturation), repeating F13 with more moving parts.
- *Preserves O14's meaning:* yes (untouched).
- *Post-result tuning risk:* low.
- *Open problem addressed:* O5; not the O11 power gap.
- *Roadmap compatibility:* full — it is the registered next gate.

**Option 2 — first preregister a separate deterministic
outcome-discrimination follow-up to Gate 1D-B.**

- *Scientific benefit:* directly repairs the only thing O14 lacked —
  outcome-level sensitivity. Converts §7.4 into falsifiable analytic design
  requirements; gives O11 its first test with nonzero ex-ante power; lets the
  O10 predicate be validated in the fire regime. Small, deterministic, cheap,
  reuses the O14 arm structure and discipline unchanged.
- *Confounding risk:* low — no new layers, no noise, no latency; one
  mechanism (buffer adequacy) varied by analytic construction.
- *Preserves O14's meaning:* yes, by explicit framing: a **new** study with
  its own protocol, plan and hashes; O14's record stands final.
- *Post-result tuning risk:* the design is *informed* by O14's result — that
  is legitimate sequential science, not post-result tuning of O14, provided
  (i) every parameter is fixed by static analysis before execution (as O14's
  were), (ii) a registered positive control (an S-style stock-blind arm that
  must discriminate) verifies instrument sensitivity in-run, and (iii) an
  F13-symmetric falsifier plus an instrument-failure falsifier are
  registered. The prohibition that matters — adjusting O14 or presenting the
  follow-up as an unbiased test of "quote-greedy misaligns nowhere" — is
  explicit in §6 and §7.4.
- *Open problem addressed:* O11 (primary), O10 (validation in fire regime).
- *Roadmap compatibility:* inserts one small gate before 1E; 1E's
  registration is untouched and inherits a validated instrument.

**Option 3 — fold an outcome-discriminating control family into the future
Gate 1E preregistration.**

- *Scientific benefit:* one fewer gate; the control family eventually exists.
- *Confounding risk:* highest — two orthogonal questions (instrument
  sensitivity; robustness under latency/uncertainty) inside one
  preregistration; if a null recurs, attribution between saturation, noise
  and latency is undecidable within the study.
- *Preserves O14's meaning:* yes.
- *Post-result tuning risk:* elevated — every predicate and threshold in the
  merged design serves two masters, and the temptation to adjust the control
  family "for the latency setting" after partial results is structural.
- *Open problem addressed:* O5 and O11, each partially and neither cleanly.
- *Roadmap compatibility:* nominal, but it enlarges Gate 1E well beyond its
  registered §10 scope.

## 11. Recommendation: Option 2 — one deterministic outcome-discrimination gate before Gate 1E

**Recommended next gate: a new, separately preregistered, deterministic
outcome-discrimination study (working name "Gate 1D-C"; targets existing
registrations O11 and O10 — no new O-number is required or created).**

Rationale, in one sentence: O11 is the project's central alignment question
and currently rests on a measurement instrument whose discriminating power
was, ex post, zero over the tested policy region (F13 + §7.2); Gate 1E's
empirical wing builds on the same instrument, so restoring instrument power
first is the cheapest step that makes every subsequent gate interpretable.

**Exact proposed scope (qualitative; nothing frozen here):**

1. A protocol and locked plan for a small deterministic world family whose
   discriminating power is established **analytically before execution**
   along the §7.4 dimensions that require no semantics change (insufficient
   buffers; per-edge rate near multiplexed demand; temporally concentrated
   demand). Semantics-changing dimensions (perishable stock; flow-drawn
   service) are admitted only if the protocol registers them explicitly as
   bounded-service semantic variants with their own conformance tests.
2. The O14 arm structure reused (A / B / C / D and the S-style positive
   control); the same information boundary, certificate discipline,
   request-shaping identity, tolerance discipline (O15) and
   one-execution rule.
3. Primary discriminator of the same registered form (A-versus-B capability
   cost; D-versus-B post-burn-in service ratio), plus **a registered
   positive-control requirement**: at least one (world, comparator) pair must
   be analytically guaranteed to discriminate, so that instrument sensitivity
   is itself falsifiable in-run.
4. Falsifiers to include an F13-symmetric "all worlds non-discriminating"
   falsifier **and** an instrument-failure falsifier ("the analytically
   guaranteed positive control failed to discriminate").
5. Worlds constructed to avoid shared endpoints in restricted arms so O3
   contamination cannot enter the primary comparisons (as in O14 §2.C).

**Explicit exclusions:** no migration, diffusion, convection, dynamic
topology, mobile actors, wallets, markets, prices, health, learning or actor
economy; no stochastic layouts; τ = 0, exact observations; no modification of
any O14 artifact, plan, protocol or result; no reopening of the O14
execution; Gate 1E remains registered and untouched until this gate reports;
Gate 2 remains paused.

**Stop condition:** if the Gate 1D-C preregistration cannot construct an
analytically guaranteed discriminating world family by static algebra alone —
i.e. if any candidate world would require trajectory-based tuning or
parameter search to establish its discriminating power — the gate must **stop
without execution**, record that finding, and the project reverts to Option 1
(proceed to the registered Gate 1E programme), leaving O11 explicitly open
with the §7 caveat as its permanent boundary. Likewise, if during any future
execution the positive control fails to discriminate, the study stops at its
falsifier and no alignment conclusion of any kind is drawn from it.
