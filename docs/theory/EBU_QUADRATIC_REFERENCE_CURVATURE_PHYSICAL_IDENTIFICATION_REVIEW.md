\tag{8}
\]

The energy kind is established by electrostatics and work, the absolute energy scale by SI realizations, and thermal normalization by k_B and measured T. No free c_source is introduced. Measurement uncertainty in those inputs is not permission to choose H within a range to favor an outcome.

## 13. Coordinate and unit treatment

Keep electrical voltage u distinct from canonical V. For fixed C_e, changing from Q to u=Q/C_e is an affine coordinate change. The same scalar is

\[
V_S(u)=\frac{C_e u^2}{2k_BT},\qquad H_u=\frac{C_e}{k_BT}.
\tag{9}
\]

H_Q decreases as C_e grows; H_u increases. There is no physical contradiction: the same numerical Q and the same numerical u do not specify the same comparison when C_e changes. All changing-H statements must name the coordinate convention and what endpoints are held fixed.

If the numerical charge coordinate is expressed in nanocoulombs, Q_nC=10^9 Q_C, then H_nC=10^-18 H_C. Likewise u_mV=10^3 u_V implies H_mV=10^-6 H_V. Substituting consistently leaves V and E invariant. A unilateral H rescaling does not. Under a nonlinear sensor map first recover calibrated physical coordinates; do not treat an instrument polynomial as a new physical potential.

Temperature must be absolute kelvin in k_BT, with any Celsius indication converted correctly. Phi and work in joules, Q in coulombs, u in volts and C_e in farads satisfy joule=coulomb×volt. The elementary charge e is exact in SI, but counting efficiency, missed events, current integration and instrument transfer remain uncertain [X01], [X05].

## 14. Interaction and hidden-state issues

### 14.1 Energy capacitance versus measured mutual capacitance

A physical multi-conductor system has an electrostatic energy form Phi=one-half v^T C_M v after removal of the common-potential gauge. C_M is the relevant Maxwell capacitance matrix with all boundary conditions stated. If actual terminals are bonded into one controlled mode v=b u, then

\[
\Phi=\tfrac12 C_e u^2,\qquad C_e=b^{\mathsf T}C_M b.
\tag{10}
\]

The conjugate mode charge is Q=C_e u. A three-terminal bridge can measure a mutual capacitance, not automatically this contraction. For example, a high electrode with capacitances C_hl to the low electrode and C_hg to the guard has C_e=C_hl+C_hg when low and guard are bonded as the return and other couplings are absent. Using C_hl alone omits stored energy. The published three-terminal standards are evidence for measurement methods; their reported C is not silently inserted into (8) for another connection. Their terminal matrix must be mapped or the actual energy capacitance calibrated directly. This is a qualification requirement, not an unresolved new physical law.

Two independent voltage sources or a floating unmeasured conductor require additional coordinates or a proved reduction. Eliminating a floating conductor by static minimization can give a Schur complement but does not establish time-domain sufficiency or remove changes of its trapped charge. At fixed external voltage, a source-inclusive thermodynamic potential can differ from field energy by work/Legendre terms. None is interchangeable with (6) without its boundary specification.

### 14.2 Memory and changing context

The risk inventory includes surface-film polarization, dielectric supports, charge trapping, leakage paths, thermal gradients, mechanical creep, residual inductive currents, changing guard potentials, detector input state and controller/switch memory. Vacuum removes bulk gap dielectric; it does not prove these other effects vanish. Published work directly documents surface-dispersion and conditioning effects [X06], [X07], [X09].

A scalar C_e measured now need not summarize future relaxation. Two devices/histories can have equal present Q, u and apparent C_e but different surface polarization or mechanical stress. Such a difference can alter future charge loss, energy or permitted actions. Either retain the relevant variables, restrict to a physically justified prepared manifold, or establish finite-horizon bounds. Unexplained history dependence refuses a Q-only recursive model immediately.

## 15. Action and passive-response model

The primary action family is **controlled charge transfer into or out of the storage mode**, with fixed geometry and thermal context during the event. Let a denote the intended signed amount. Ideal charge conservation gives

\[
A_a(Q)=Q+a,\qquad
E_S(Q,a)=-\frac{Qa+a^2/2}{C_e k_BT}.
\tag{11}
\]

This response comes from the physical port balance, not from the potential gradient. Before a future event, a controller/charge-delivery contract must independently predict the delivered extent a_hat from its commanded input and calibrated delivery law. Actual delivery need not equal a_hat exactly. A current command I_cmd and duration tau do not constitute a proved extent unless the actual current-transfer and timing model is qualified. The prospective physical model is

\[
Q_{\rm post}=Q_{\rm pre}+\widehat a+\epsilon_a-\ell,
\tag{12}
\]

where epsilon_a is delivery error and ell is signed uncommanded charge loss across specified ports during that event. Deterministic enclosures or a conditional joint law must be supplied. The endpoints are independently observed afterward; inserting their observed difference into a and calling it a prior prediction would be circular.

Passive processes **between** registered actions include leakage, relaxation and environmental changes. Ideally P_tau(Q)=Q for an isolated lossless mode. Under an independently qualified fixed ohmic leakage law, the deterministic mean-field map is Q exp[−tau/(R_l C_e)]; thermal fluctuations require their own noise law. Neither that exponential nor zero leakage is inferred from the energy polynomial. Published high resistance bounds are evidence of feasibility in their own conditions, not a license to set ell=0 in a new apparatus [X09].

Leakage occurring **during** an event is not excluded by relabeling it passive. It belongs in (12), and the actual source endpoint difference includes its effect. Allocation between intended action and simultaneous leakage needs additional physical registration/attribution information. The report supplies no actor-allocation rule. Mechanical work from changing geometry, charge injection by switching and thermal drift during the event must likewise be included or bounded.

## 16. Finite-domain quadratic validity

The ideal linear, fixed-boundary electrostatic model is exactly quadratic. A real device has a bounded candidate domain

\[
D=\{ |Q|\le Q_{\max},\ T_{\min}\le T\le T_{\max},\
\theta\in\Theta_{\rm qualified},\ 0\le\tau\le\tau_{\max}\},
\tag{13}
\]

with T_min>0 and fixed context within each action. The charge interval, time horizon, field-emission/breakdown exclusions, mechanical-stability region and instrument range must later receive measured or physically justified numerical limits. This review does not invent a device certificate by assigning convenient limits. The **form and physical meaning of the finite domain are identified; its numerical realization is unqualified**.

A particularly direct remainder certificate uses the corrected reversible conjugate voltage. If, relative to the physically identified reference r,

\[
\frac{d\Phi}{dQ}=\frac{Q-r}{C_e}+\delta u(Q),\qquad
|\delta u(Q)|\le b_u(Q)
\tag{14}
\]

on every segment needed for the compared endpoints, then

\[
\left|E_{\rm physical}-E_{\rm quadratic}\right|
\le\frac{1}{k_BT}\left|\int_{Q_{\rm post}}^{Q_{\rm pre}}
b_u(q)\,dq\right|.
\tag{15}
\]

The absolute integral means integration over the unordered interval; b_u is nonnegative. This bound can be sharper than summing separate endpoint remainders. It requires a single-valued scalar and a uniform envelope, not just small residuals at selected observations. Reference error is propagated separately unless already contained in delta u; it must not be double counted.

Alternatively, if Phi is C³, Phi'(r)=0 and |Phi'''|≤M on the relevant segments, the standard bound is |R(Q)|≤M|Q−r|³/6 and the EBU error is at most M(|Q_pre−r|³+|Q_post−r|³)/(6k_BT). A nonzero first derivative at the selected reference adds a linear term; Taylor's theorem does not remove it.

For a nonlinear but conservative capacitor, use **differential** capacitance C_d=dQ/du and Phi(Q)−Phi(r)=integral u(q)dq. In general Phi is not Q²/[2C_d(Q)] and its Hessian is 1/C_d(Q). Substituting a state-dependent “H(Q)” into one-half Q²H(Q) gives extra derivatives. Resolved voltage dependence can refuse the constant-H domain while leaving a nonquadratic EBU field possible under a later contract. Hysteretic work needs additional state; it cannot be repaired by fitting a single curve.

Add the independently accounted endpoint/reference/calibration and constitutive terms from §21. This quantifies the difference between a prospective quote and the realized endpoint value. It does not select B_a or a passing tolerance.

## 18. Changing scarcity and changing H

### 18.1 Effect A: position in a fixed physical field

For Q≥0 and a positive charging increment a, the negative value in (11) becomes more negative as Q grows:

\[
\frac{\partial E_S(Q,a)}{\partial Q}=-\frac{a}{C_e k_BT}<0.
\tag{17}
\]

This is real increasing work cost for increasing charge separation. Its field counterpart is higher voltage. It is a physically meaningful position effect, but calling charge separation “scarcity” would add a semantic claim not established here.

For removing charge d with 0<d≤Q,

\[
E_S(Q,-d)=\frac{Qd-d^2/2}{C_e k_BT}>0.
\tag{18}
\]

As the store becomes less charged, the same permitted removal delivers a smaller positive storage-energy drop. This **does not implement “a more depleted useful store makes further depletion more negative.”** The physically preferred zero-field reference and a useful charged operating target differ. No shifted engineering target or sign reversal is introduced to conceal that result. It is useful for EBU to know this limitation before interpreting a calibrated number as a social signal.

### 18.2 Effect B: an actual change of curvature

At fixed charge coordinates, H_Q=1/(C_e k_BT). A true reduction in C_e at fixed T increases curvature; a rise in T at fixed C_e decreases normalized curvature. Geometry change is a possible **physical cause** of changed C_e, not necessarily a competing error explanation. A geometric change in nature must be distinguished from merely changing the coordinate units used to describe unchanged geometry.

| Observed change | Discriminating requirement |
|---|---|
| Static dQ/du changes | Independent capacitance/charge/voltage comparisons at matched context; finite-domain and frequency-transfer checks |
| Only response time changes | Independently resolve resistance/mobility and extra modes; do not infer H from rate |
| Electrode geometry changes | Measure physical dimensions/terminal matrix; treat resulting C_e change as a context event; include mechanical work if during action |
| Thermometer or electrical standard drifts | Check independent references and shared calibration covariance; revise estimate/provenance rather than infer a physical event |
| Unit/coordinate representation changes | Transform H by congruence; V/E must agree for matching physical endpoints |
| Different history at equal apparent state | Test memory variables, surface relaxation, mechanical conditioning and observation loading; refuse the reduced state if unresolved |
| Residual within measurement uncertainty | Inconclusive evidence for a true H change; no automatic field update justified |

For smooth physical context variation in the unbiased branch,

\[
dV_S=\frac{Q}{C_e k_BT}\,dQ
-\frac{Q^2}{2C_e^2 k_BT}\,dC_e
-\frac{Q^2}{2C_e k_BT^2}\,dT.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus a changing capacitance or temperature during an action cannot be handled by one fixed H. For a changing reference r, an additional −(Q−r)dr/(C_e k_BT) appears in the corresponding centered formula. At fixed charge the physical energy differential is dPhi=u dQ−Q² dC_e/(2C_e²); the capacitance term can represent mechanical work. These are context terms in the balance, not extra actor credits or retrospective repricing.

## 19. Recursive-sufficiency requirements

RF §8 and M §8 require equal retained states to have the same available actions, action-value/next-state joint law and passive law for matched inputs. The ideal model with Q, fixed theta, deterministic charge increments and identity passive map satisfies this criterion: Q_{n+1}=Q_n+a_n, and (11) gives the value from the current Q alone. On a finite domain the action set is restricted by |Q+a|≤Q_max and by the stated physical delivery limits. Nothing in the model authorizes arbitrary charging beyond those bounds.

For a real capacitor, **Q alone is not accepted as sufficient**. A simple passive-memory counterexample shows the issue without running a trajectory. Let a main capacitor have terminal voltage u and let an internal dielectric-relaxation branch have a capacitor voltage w coupled to u through resistance R_d. With the external terminal open, its initial main-capacitor rate contains

\[
C_0\dot u=-(u-w)/R_d.
\tag{20}
\]

Two histories with the same measured u and main charge C_0 u but different w have different subsequent rates and internal stored energies. Including the dielectric branch can restore a larger physical state model; pretending its w is zero does not. This is a conditional circuit witness, not an assertion that every vacuum-gap device has exactly one such branch. The published surface-film and conditioning observations make the broader memory concern physical [X06], [X07].

The objection also holds if Q is correctly defined as total port-delivered charge rather than main-capacitor charge. In this two-capacitor model, Q_total=C_0 u+C_d w and Phi_total=(C_0 u²+C_d w²)/2. For C_0=C_d=C and fixed Q_total=q, the states (u,w)=(q/C,0) and (q/(2C),q/(2C)) have energies q²/(2C) and q²/(4C), respectively, and different initial rates under (20). Thus even the exact total-charge coordinate cannot recover the stored scalar or response when this hidden distribution is unresolved. These are two prescribed states, not a simulated relaxation or an empirical finding.

Another information loss occurs if only V is retained: +Q and −Q have the same energy, but the same positive charge increment has different values and may have different domain availability. A current scalar value is not a sufficient state.

A real candidate must therefore identify the required m or bound the effect of omitted state over a stated horizon and range of histories. For a projection pi, the relevant conditions are availability on each fiber, descent of action/passive laws, and fiber invariance of the required value. Approximate reduction can use RF/M finite-horizon state and value bounds. Measurement uncertainty, source remainder and reduction error are distinct; correlated reuse does not average away a common bias. The present status is **requirements identified; ideal model passes in principle; real retained-state sufficiency unqualified**.

## 20. Optional probability/statistical bridge

An independently constructed capacitor field need not be sampled thermally to define its storage contrast. A prepared isolated charge state or an actively driven capacitor is not expected to sample an equilibrium Gaussian merely because its energy is quadratic.

A possible **separate** classical equilibrium RC branch has a resistor at the same independently measured T, no imposed drive, correct thermal fluctuation–dissipation relation, fixed C_e and adequate state/bandwidth description. In its ideal white-noise limit,

\[
dQ=-\frac{Q}{R C_e}\,dt+\sqrt{\frac{2k_BT}{R}}\,dW_t.
\tag{21}
\]

The stationary variance balance gives Var(Q)=C_e k_BT. With full real support, the stationary density is proportional to exp[−Q²/(2C_e k_BT)], so beta_bridge=1 and Var(u)=k_BT/C_e. Equation (21) is an explicitly added classical model, not new EBU dynamics. Finite bandwidth, quantum charge discreteness, inductive modes, frequency-dependent loss, nonlinear support and feedback can change the observed law. A continuum Gaussian requires charge spacing small relative to the occupied fluctuation scale and the classical thermal regime; a bounded certified charge interval does not literally supply full Gaussian support.

The construction branch measures C_e, reference, charge/voltage scale, boundary and T. The observation branch estimates the state law and detector/noise transfer without using H to impose it. Inferring C_e from the same thermal variance used to test Var(Q)=C_e k_BT, or inferring T from that same capacitor-noise record, is circular for this comparison. A separately calibrated thermometer remains permissible even if its realization ultimately uses other established thermal physics; shared standards and assumptions are disclosed.

Thermal electrical noise and its thermodynamic theory are established [X11]. Published driven electrical fluctuation experiments also exist [X12]; they are not evidence that a driven source obeys the zero-drive Gaussian or P4 account. The possible statistical comparison here is metrological qualification of an established relation, not a new physical prediction. Its failure, without verified equilibrium/observation premises, does not refuse the independently identified storage scalar.

## 21. Metrology and uncertainty

### 21.1 Measurement model and correlation

Use a joint measurement model for pre/post charge, physical reference r, C_e, T, charge-delivery extent, port corrections and model remainder. For the fixed-field quadratic contrast,

\[
E=\frac{(Q_0-r)^2-(Q_1-r)^2}{2C_e k_BT},
\]
\[
dE=\frac{Q_0-r}{C_e k_BT}dQ_0
-\frac{Q_1-r}{C_e k_BT}dQ_1
-\frac{Q_0-Q_1}{C_e k_BT}dr
-E\frac{dC_e}{C_e}-E\frac{dT}{T}.
\tag{22}
\]

For a local linear uncertainty approximation, u_E²=g^T Sigma g with the complete covariance matrix; use a justified nonlinear enclosure when the linear approximation is inadequate [X13]. The gradient g follows (22). Shared thermometer and electrical-reference errors are retained. Independent sensor readings do not automatically have independent calibration errors.

If the raw measured endpoints are voltages, the model is E=C_e(u_0²−u_1²)/(2k_BT) in the unbiased branch. Its C_e sensitivity has the opposite sign to the fixed-charge parameterization because the implied Q endpoints depend on C_e. One must not infer Q=C_e u and then treat Q and C_e as independent. Use primitive observations and propagate their actual dependencies. The differential relation dH/H=−dC_e/C_e−dT/T has a covariance cross term in its variance; its apparent precision does not establish the finite endpoint bound.

Separate corrected bias and correction uncertainty, random observation error, systematic scale/reference errors, deterministic constitutive remainder, action-response error, state-reduction error and boundary omission. Charge-dependent C_e errors may already be part of the constitutive envelope; do not count the same term again as an unrelated bias. Conversely, an unmeasured remainder is not zero. Near E=0 use absolute uncertainty and a sign enclosure rather than a misleading relative error.

## 22. Calibration-loop test

Use RF §6/SFE §13's existing affine composition for representations of matching quantities at matching contexts:

\[
v_j=c_{ij}v_i+d_{ij},\qquad
c_{ik}=c_{jk}c_{ij},\qquad
d_{ik}=c_{jk}d_{ij}+d_{jk}.
\tag{23}
\]

For a path P with ordered edges (c_1,d_1),...,(c_m,d_m),

\[
c_P=\prod_{r=1}^m c_r,\qquad
d_P=\sum_{r=1}^m d_r\prod_{s=r+1}^m c_s.
\tag{24}
\]

A closed comparison returning to the same root description requires c_P=1. It additionally requires d_P=0 for aligned potential levels. Fixed-chart endpoint contrasts cannot detect constant offset drift, but a global level comparison can. The maps are only asserted where all intermediate comparison domains overlap.

For the candidate, one conceptual loop is **root-normalized stored-energy contrast → electrical stored-energy representation A → independent corrected work/energy representation B → root-normalized contrast**. A and B compare the same source endpoints and boundary after work/loss corrections. At a common T, the energy-to-root conversion is fixed by k_BT. This is a metrological comparison, not a physical sequence that moves charge through three devices. A lossy converter's efficiency changes its endpoints and energy balance, not the root exchange rate.

The loop is physically informative only if at least one comparison supplies genuinely independent information. Defining the return map as the exact inverse of fitted outward maps guarantees closure algebraically and tests nothing. Likewise, a common wrong temperature or common electrical scale can cancel around a loop. Root traceability to independent standards remains indispensable.

For measured positive slopes use log residual r_P=sum s_e log(c_e), where s_e records edge direction. Its first-order variance is s^T Cov(log c) s. Also propagate the offset residual and its scale–offset covariance. A **resolved nonzero loop residual**, after accounting for context changes, correlations and declared remainder, is source-to-root calibration failure on that comparison domain. An unresolved residual is inconclusive metrology. No threshold, coverage rule, sample count or fitted loop projection is selected in this review.

An exact arithmetic illustration has maps v_A=2v_R+3, v_B=3v_A−4=6v_R+5, and v_R=(v_B−5)/6. The loop is identity. Replacing the final slope by 1/5 creates multiplicative drift; replacing only its offset creates level drift with possibly preserved difference scale. These numbers are algebra witnesses, not candidate conversion factors.

## 23. Falsifiers and refusal conditions

Failure classes name the claim actually challenged. They are not a new sign taxonomy or a procedure for automatically attributing every discrepancy. A later prospective design must specify discrimination, uncertainty coverage and the evidence needed to isolate each class before data are observed.

| Failure class | Future observation capable of triggering it | Preconditions / proper interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **ROOT CONTRADICTION** | A reproducible nonconstant mismatch of independently measured canonical log-density contrasts with energy/(k_BT), after independent checks support the stated physical ensemble, measure, boundary and measurement premises | Challenges the scoped physical canonical bridge or its assumed physics; not the algebraic definition E=V_pre−V_post. No such observation is supplied here. Defining V by (2) makes its equality to that ratio tautological, not experimentally falsifiable. |
| **ROOT REALIZATION REFUSAL** | Proposed root setup cannot establish the needed energy/temperature/support/observation conditions or independent branches | Refuses that realization. An unqualified setup or missing accuracy certificate is not evidence against the canonical theorem or all EBU. Existing E1a limitations remain in this category as appropriate to their actual records. |
| **SOURCE CONSTITUTIVE REFUSAL** | After corrections, charging work depends on unresolved history, or no single bounded scalar matches the stated source-energy boundary | Refuses this reduced constitutive source; a larger state or other potential needs a new contract. A port balance error must first be distinguished from a false scalar claim. |
| **REFERENCE FAILURE** | Independently located minimum lies outside the declared r interval, varies with omitted state, or is inaccessible contrary to the claim | A sensor offset alone is a measurement correction; a physical reference shift is a context/state issue. An engineering target is not substituted. |
| **CURVATURE FAILURE** | Independent static slope/finite-energy contrasts disagree with 1/C_e or the constant-H remainder envelope | Refuses the claimed quadratic domain or terminal reduction. A changed response rate alone does not establish this failure. |
| **SOURCE-TO-ROOT CALIBRATION FAILURE** | Independent same-contrast root conversions or a qualified calibration loop show a resolved scale/level inconsistency | Check quantity kind, context, terminal conventions and correlated uncertainty first. Does not follow from physical conversion losses. |

| **RECURSIVE-SUFFICIENCY FAILURE** | Matched retained states with different histories exhibit distinguishable available actions or joint value/next-state/passive laws outside their declared bounds | Requires matched new inputs and observation controls; refuses the retained summary, not necessarily the full physical field. Equation (20) is an explicit mechanism. |
| **INCONCLUSIVE METROLOGY** | The uncertainty/remainder region includes both contract-consistent and materially inconsistent explanations, or is not bounded | Neither a pass nor a physical contradiction. More averaging cannot automatically resolve a common systematic or a missing model term. |

A further scope refusal applies if a source-only contrast is presented as a complete source–driver–bath settlement or a welfare score. That is a semantic/boundary overclaim, not a measured failure of electrostatic energy. Charge breakdown, field emission, mechanical instability or a changed terminal topology places an event outside the admitted domain unless explicitly modeled; extrapolating (8) across it is invalid.
