## 4. Constant physical normalization: theorem and scope

**Theorem N1 (fixed-scale normalization).** Fix a physically declared comparison domain and context. Suppose \(\Phi\) is a twice differentiable physical scalar; \(\Phi^*=\Phi(x^*)\) is a fixed reference level; and \(\Lambda>0\) is independent of \(x\) on that domain. Define

\[
V(x)=\frac{\Phi(x)-\Phi^*}{\Lambda}.
\]

Then

\[
\nabla V=\frac{\nabla\Phi}{\Lambda},\qquad
H(x)=\frac{K_\Phi(x)}{\Lambda},\qquad
E=\frac{\Phi_{\rm pre}-\Phi_{\rm post}}{\Lambda}.
\tag{2}
\]

These identities follow by differentiation and endpoint subtraction; no probability or dynamics is used. If \(\nabla\Phi(x^*)=0\) and \(K_\Phi\) is constant on a connected open domain containing the reference, integration gives the exact quadratic there. If only local regularity and stationarity hold, Taylor's theorem instead gives

\[
V(x^*+y)=\tfrac12 y^{\mathsf T}(K_\Phi(x^*)/\Lambda)y
+R_3(y)/\Lambda,
\tag{3}
\]

with \(R_3=O(\|y\|^3)\) under a bounded third derivative near the reference. Omitting that remainder is a local approximation, not the exact finite formula (1). A nonstationary reference also leaves a linear term. Positivity is not needed for (2); it is needed on the directions where a stable inverse-compliance interpretation is claimed.

For dimensionless V, \(\Lambda\) must carry the units of \(\Phi\). For physical denomination it must also match its quantity kind and operational meaning: matching dimensions is insufficient. A numerical conversion of units rescales \(\Phi\), its reference and \(\Lambda\) together and leaves V unchanged. Altering \(\Lambda\) alone changes the physical normalization contract. A positive denominator preserves the sign of each fixed-context native contrast; it cannot repair an incorrectly oriented source.

A physical denominator requires an identified physical quantity, a reason that this quantity normalizes this source on this domain, independently traceable inputs, and stated uncertainty and dependence. A familiar constant with appropriate units is not automatically that source's root scale. A measured scalar combined with an arbitrary preference weight remains a score. N1 validates the mathematics conditional on a physical contract; it does not manufacture that contract.

## 5. Field compliance without probability

**Theorem C1 (affine compliance and static response).** On an accessible affine space with positive-definite H, the bilinear form H maps state displacements to marginal covectors. Its inverse \(\Xi=H^{-1}\) maps covectors back to displacements. Under an invertible affine coordinate change \(x'=Ax+a\),

\[
H'=A^{-\mathsf T}HA^{-1},\quad
\mu'=A^{-\mathsf T}\mu,\quad
\Xi'=A\Xi A^{\mathsf T},\quad \mu=\nabla V.
\tag{4}
\]

This follows by the chain rule and matrix inversion. Scalar values and contractions are invariant. Entries of H and Xi are coordinate-dependent; the operator and its dual pairing are not. In particular, Xi's components have the units of products of the corresponding state coordinates when V is dimensionless.

To give a physical response meaning, a conjugate load must actually enter the native potential as \(-f^{\mathsf T}y\). Minimizing the positive quadratic \(\Phi-f^{\mathsf T}y\) gives \(\delta y=K_\Phi^{-1}f\). For normalized load \(j=f/\Lambda\),

\[
\delta y=\Xi j,\qquad \Xi=\Lambda K_\Phi^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]

For a nonquadratic potential this is the infinitesimal response at a stable stationary state. Thus **field compliance** is scientifically defensible as normalized inverse static curvature. Physical mechanical compliance with respect to f is \(K_\Phi^{-1}=\Xi/\Lambda\); conflating these two changes units. Without a physically realizable conjugate-load relation, Xi has the geometric interpretation but no demonstrated response instrument. Xi alone specifies no mobility, response time, noise amplitude, or trajectory.

General nonlinear coordinate changes preserve V but not the ordinary Hessian tensor law away from a stationary point. If \(x=x(q)\), with coordinate Jacobian \(D=\partial x/\partial q\),

\[
H^{(q)}=D^{\mathsf T}H^{(x)}D+
\sum_i (\partial_{x_i}V)\,\nabla_q^2x_i.
\tag{6}
\]

At a stationary point the second term vanishes. Elsewhere a covariant Hessian requires an independently declared connection; this report does not install one or identify all EBU Hessians with a universal thermodynamic metric. Equation (4) is exact on the retained affine geometry.

### Constraints and null directions

\[

## 9. Common-root compatibility and affine closure

The following consolidates RF's affine calibration theorem; it does not claim a new proof of the already-cleared result.

**Theorem RC1 (affine root-chart compatibility).** On a fixed common comparison domain, let corrected realizations of the same scalar be linked by invertible affine maps

\[
C_{ij}(v)=c_{ij}v+d_{ij},\qquad c_{ij}>0.
\]

Assume matching physical meanings and independent calibration evidence for these maps, with enough distinct levels to identify their parameters. On a connected calibration graph, a path-independent global affine denomination exists if and only if every closed calibration walk composes to the identity. For triples with all comparison maps present (or after extending to composed path maps), this requires

\[
c_{ik}=c_{jk}c_{ij},\qquad
 d_{ik}=c_{jk}d_{ij}+d_{jk};
\tag{16}
\]

for a closed walk with maps \((c_1,d_1),\ldots,(c_n,d_n)\),

\[
\prod_{r=1}^n c_r=1,\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^n d_r\prod_{s=r+1}^n c_s=0.
\tag{17}
\]

For contrast-only calibration the scale condition suffices; offsets cancel from fixed-context differences. Level alignment also requires offset closure. Inverses and identities are included; a comparison at only one level cannot test the full affine identity.

**Proof consolidation.** Compose the affine maps to obtain the products and offset sum. If a global chart exists, transporting around any closed walk returns the same value, proving necessity. Conversely, choose a reference chart and transport along any path. Two paths differ by a closed walk; identity closure makes the transported chart independent of the path. The remaining common affine freedom is fixed by the physical scale anchor and reference convention. This is the RF cocycle argument, applied here to normalization realizations.

**Counterexample RC2 (tautological closure).** Choose arbitrary numbers \(s_i>0,t_i\), and set \(c_{ij}=s_j/s_i\), \(d_{ij}=t_j-(s_j/s_i)t_i\). Every loop closes identically, even if no physical quantity was ever measured. A common erroneous standard can also cancel around a loop. Algebraic closure is necessary for the claimed chart structure, but cannot identify a physical denomination or independently validate its accuracy. Fitting maps with closure enforced is not then an independent test of closure.

### Qualified maps, uncertainty and equivalence classes

Exact maps of the same physically identified quantity on the same domain define an equivalence relation: identity, inverse and composition supply reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity. **ROOT CLASS** can therefore name that equivalence class of compatible realization contracts. Choice of representative is a calibration choice, not a choice of new physics. A disconnected comparison graph does not establish one class across its components without another physical bridge.

Empirical pairwise agreement “within tolerance” is not itself an equivalence relation. Values \(0,0.75\epsilon,1.5\epsilon\) make neighboring pairs agree within epsilon while the end pair does not. Qualified maps must instead describe uncertain evidence about a common underlying class and retain a joint uncertainty model, shared standards, validity domains and unresolved discrepancies. There is no declaration of exact equality from overlapping error bars.

If a corrected map on its validated domain has additive residual bounded by \(\epsilon_r\), exact slopes give the conservative composed bound

\[
\epsilon_{\rm path}\leq
\sum_{r=1}^n\epsilon_r\prod_{s=r+1}^n|c_s|.
\tag{18}
\]

Uncertain slopes require propagation over their joint admissible set as well; correlated stochastic uncertainties cannot be replaced by independent error bars. Equation (18) is a deterministic bound, not a new acceptance tolerance. No tolerances are set in this task.

Maps on partial overlaps need compatible transport domains. One can describe a groupoid of local realizations, or restrict to a common domain; disconnected or nonoverlapping local agreements cannot silently become a global equivalence class. Nonlinear sensor correction may precede this affine comparison. A genuinely nonlinear scalar bridge requires separate analysis: \(g(V_{\rm pre})-g(V_{\rm post})\) is not a constant conversion of E in general. Nothing here proves that every possible physical root relation is affine.

## 10. Joint-scalar existence: a conditional necessary-and-sufficient theorem

Denomination is one requirement; integrability and complete physical assembly are another. This section states exactly what “necessary and sufficient” means. It is a theorem conditional on physical identification, not an algorithm that decides the physical meaning of arbitrary measurements.

Let \(\mathcal Z\) be the admitted joint state, including context and any required memory. Its connected comparison graph has oriented edges \(z\to z'\) and formal reversed comparisons; formal reversal need not be a physically executable reverse process. Let \(e(z,z')\) be the proposed complete contrast **after** conversion into one physically justified denomination. Reversal is antisymmetric. Contributions must describe that same comparison, with overlap, coupling and boundary terms identified and counted once.

**Theorem J1 (scalar assembly conditional on a physical contract).** Such contrast assignments are representable by a single-valued potential,

\[
e(z,z')=V(z)-V(z'),
\tag{19}
\]

if and only if the sum of e on every closed comparison walk is zero. V is then unique up to a constant on each connected component.

**Proof.** A potential telescopes around every walk. Conversely, fix \(z_0\) and define \(V(z)=V(z_0)-\sum_{z_0\leadsto z}e\). Two paths differ by a closed walk, so zero cycle sums make the definition unambiguous. The edge relation and uniqueness follow immediately. Multiple reports of the same comparison must be consistent after the declared measurement uncertainty is accounted for; exact theorem assertions refer to the underlying field, not perfectly noiseless empirical data.

For a smooth local marginal one-form \(\omega\), the corresponding condition is \(\omega=dV\), equivalently all periods \(\oint\omega=0\). Symmetric cross derivatives give local closure; on a simply connected domain they suffice for a smooth closed form to be exact. On a domain with holes, periods remain necessary. These are the existing RF exactness conditions, now explicitly separated from root-map closure.

Consequently, a **physical single-scalar EBU representation of the specified assignments** exists precisely when (i) an independently justified common denomination and interpretation has been supplied, (ii) the assignments are the complete, uniquely counted physical comparisons on a sufficient joint state, and (iii) they satisfy J1. Conditions (i) and (ii) are scientific premises about what is represented, not conclusions obtained merely by testing cycles. If a complete independently justified joint scalar Phi already exists and its normalization contract is fixed, it supplies a witness directly. If source contributions are globally defined compatible factors \(v_\alpha(z)\) of one field, their complete sum also supplies a witness.

This theorem does not require physical work or dissipated heat to be endpoint quantities in general. A path-dependent heat account may have nonzero cycles; relabeling it V would be wrong. Extending the state can sometimes restore an endpoint representation, but only if the additional variables have legitimate physical meaning and satisfy the existing recursive sufficiency requirement. An arbitrary accumulated bookkeeping variable must not be mistaken for a physical root repair.

For EBU, the practical protection is that a closed loop of calibrators and a closed loop of source-state comparisons are tested for different reasons. Success of either test cannot replace the other.

## 11. Noncommensurability refusal

**Theorem RC3 (identification refusal).** Given valid physical fields with no justified relative denomination, their individual contrasts do not identify one physical scalar total. They identify a tagged vector \(\mathbf E=(E_1,\ldots,E_m)\), or one scalar component per already compatible root class.

**Proof.** Within-field data remain unchanged under choosing different positive relative conversion weights when no bridge fixes them. The proposed combined values and even signs can differ, as D1 shows. A purported unique physically calibrated sum therefore does not follow from those data. This is nonidentifiability, not a prohibition on writing numerical weighted scores. Any chosen score must be described as that extra convention rather than a calibrated physical EBU root.

Transition to a scalar requires independently justified matched physical contrasts or a physical bridge between quantity kinds, traceable positive conversions and orientation, compatible comparison domains/context, and treatment of joint interaction/overlap. Calibration closure and J1 must hold for the resulting complete account. Uncertainty must be carried through rather than hidden in arbitrary weights. If the physical bridge fails, the vector remains the scientifically faithful representation; there is no theorem that every vector can ultimately be reduced.

This refuses unsupported settlement aggregation without discarding information from valid individual fields. It can prevent one resource improvement from concealing an unrelated deterioration through an unidentified exchange weight. That is an accounting consequence, not a proved social-welfare theorem.

## 12. Joint quadratic fields and the meaning of cross curvature

Let \(u=x-x^*\), \(v=y-y^*\), with fixed physically declared subsystem coordinates. Write

\[
V(u,v)=\tfrac12u^{\mathsf T}H_xu+u^{\mathsf T}Gv+
\tfrac12v^{\mathsf T}H_yv,
\quad
H=\begin{pmatrix}H_x&G\\G^{\mathsf T}&H_y\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]

The mixed derivative G gives the change of one subsystem's marginal when the other's coordinates change. It represents physical static coupling only when the joint scalar, subsystem partition, coordinates and constitutive cross-response have that independently justified meaning. Conceptual association between resources is not a source for G.

If the complete scalar separates in the declared subsystem coordinates, G=0. Conversely, vanishing mixed derivatives on a connected product domain imply additive scalar separation up to constants. Neither statement proves dynamical or stochastic independence. Shared noise, reservoirs, constraints, initial correlations or kinetic coupling may persist. Under the full joint Gaussian premises only, block-diagonal H implies statistical factorization; arbitrary observed covariance is a separate issue.

**Counterexample J2 (self-curvatures do not identify coupling).** Both

\[
H_+=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H_-=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix}
\tag{21}
\]

are positive definite and have identical diagonal blocks. From the origin, increment (1,1) gives E=−3/2 and E=−1/2 respectively. Separate self-curvature calibration cannot choose between them.

**Counterexample J3 (coordinate cross term).** Begin with \(V=(q_1^2+q_2^2)/2\) and define \(x=q_1\), \(y=q_1+q_2\). Then

\[
V=\tfrac12[x^2+(y-x)^2],\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{22}
\]

The cross term arises from mixing coordinates, not a newly discovered interaction between the original physical subsystems. Conversely, diagonalizing a physically coupled system into normal coordinates does not remove its original subsystem coupling. A cross block has meaning relative to a physically declared partition.

Positive self-blocks alone do not ensure a positive joint field. With \(H_y\succ0\), joint positivity is equivalent to \(H_x-GH_y^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}\succ0\). In the full Gaussian case with b=1,

\[
\Sigma_{xx}=(H_x-GH_y^{-1}G^{\mathsf T})^{-1},\quad
\Sigma_{x\mid y}=H_x^{-1}.
\tag{23}
\]

Thus holding y fixed and letting it fluctuate answer different compliance questions. M §11 already establishes the Schur reduction and the determinant contribution from marginalization. If parameters depend on retained state, integrating out hidden coordinates can add state-dependent log determinants; taking a conditional minimum and marginalizing are different operations. Neither operation alone proves recursive closure of a reduced model.

## 13. Complete finite interaction and unique counting

For simultaneous increments a and b in (20), expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E(a,b)={}&-(H_xu+Gv)^{\mathsf T}a
-(G^{\mathsf T}u+H_yv)^{\mathsf T}b\\
&-\tfrac12a^{\mathsf T}H_xa-a^{\mathsf T}Gb
-\tfrac12b^{\mathsf T}H_yb.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The complete interaction-factor contrast is

\[
E_{\rm int}=u^{\mathsf T}Gv-(u+a)^{\mathsf T}G(v+b)
=-a^{\mathsf T}Gv-u^{\mathsf T}Gb-a^{\mathsf T}Gb.
\tag{25}
\]

It is not merely the final bilinear action term. With all coalition values evaluated from the same base state and additive subset endpoints, the pair Möbius coefficient is

\[
I_{12}=E(a,b)-E(a,0)-E(0,b)=-a^{\mathsf T}Gb.
\tag{26}
\]

The empty increment has E=0 here. More generally, for additive joint increments \(\Delta_i\), the inherited quadratic result is \(I_{ij}=-\Delta_i^{\mathsf T}H\Delta_j\). Higher coefficients vanish for this exact quadratic/additive-endpoint specialization; nonlinear coalition endpoint maps can invalidate that conclusion. These formulas retain F, SFE and M's sign convention and endpoint semantics.

Equation (20) has \(V\ne V_x+V_y\) whenever its nonconstant interaction is omitted. Conversely, adding a subsystem's already inclusive interaction contribution to a total that already contains it counts it twice. Use a complete factorization \(V=\sum_\alpha v_\alpha\), and take each factor contrast once. The sum is one action value. **Source factors explain one complete action value; they do not issue independent settlements.**

The source-decomposition gauge remains: \(v_1\mapsto v_1+g\), \(v_2\mapsto v_2-g\) preserves V, while shifting their attributed contrasts by opposite amounts \(g_{\rm pre}-g_{\rm post}\). Only a constant g on the relevant comparison component leaves each individual attribution invariant. A physical ownership/allocation rule requires extra identification; total-value invariance does not uniquely allocate interaction to sources or actors. This consolidates SFE's result rather than replacing it.

## 14. Multiple normalizations inside one joint system

The answer is not an unconditional yes or no. The quantity represented by the proposed sum must be specified.

**Theorem N2 (when sourcewise normalization equals one native normalization).** Let a connected joint domain carry a complete native scalar \(\Phi=\sum_\alpha\Phi_\alpha\), including each interaction once. Assume all factors are in the same physical quantity kind and all scales below are positive and fixed on the domain. Define

\[
V_{\rm sep}=\sum_\alpha\frac{\Phi_\alpha-\Phi_\alpha^*}{\Lambda_\alpha},\quad
V_R=\frac{\Phi-\Phi^*}{\Lambda_R},\quad \Phi^*=\sum_\alpha\Phi_\alpha^*.
\]

Then the two fields have identical contrasts if and only if

\[
\sum_\alpha(\Lambda_\alpha^{-1}-\Lambda_R^{-1})\,d\Phi_\alpha=0
\quad\text{throughout the admitted domain}.
\tag{27}
\]

**Proof.** Their difference has exactly the displayed differential. It is constant on a connected domain exactly when that differential vanishes. Reference alignment removes the constant. An equivalent finite statement is that the corresponding weighted sum of all admitted factor contrasts vanishes. If the factor values can vary independently (their differentials have full row rank somewhere), (27) requires all Lambda_alpha=Lambda_R. Restricted domains can hide differences between weights; that is not a proof valid outside those constraints.

If a nonconstant interaction factor has been omitted, its differential appears as an additional missing term; checking only the self-factors does not satisfy the theorem. If the physical contract is one native scalar with one scale, **assemble that complete scalar and normalize it once**. Local representations can first be converted with \(c_{iR}=\Lambda_i/\Lambda_R\); their raw unconverted sum is not generally that same field.

### Cases that must remain distinct

| Situation | Scientifically justified conclusion |
|---|---|
| Factors already in one root denomination | Their complete uniquely counted sum is permitted when they genuinely compose one field; denomination alone does not prove composition |
| Same thermal T, additive energies, declared boundary | Normalize the complete energy, including interaction, by that one physical k_BT |
| Separate independent canonical systems at different fixed temperatures | Their product distribution has a valid joint negative log density equal to the sum of local normalized energies plus a constant; this is not one common-joule energy contrast |
| Coupled subsystems maintained at different temperatures | No general single-temperature canonical potential or componentwise thermal normalization; require a justified joint field/dynamics and reservoir accounting |
| Different forms of energy | Mechanical and electrical energy can share joules with complete boundaries and conversion physics; numerical equality of dimensions for another quantity kind is insufficient |
| Different nonthermal scales | Each may be meaningful locally; a common bridge or independently justified joint normalized functional is still required |
| Shared reservoir | Include the reservoir/boundary contribution once, or derive a consistent reduced potential; do not attach duplicate copies to each source |
| Correlated states | A product log-density sum generally omits a dependence factor; curvature/statistics/physical interaction must be distinguished |

For the independent canonical case, with fixed product reference measure and \(p(x,y)=p_1(x)p_2(y)\),

\[
-\log p=\Phi_1/(k_BT_1)+\Phi_2/(k_BT_2)+\text{constant}.
\tag{28}
\]

This is a legitimate single scalar log weight even when no Lambda_R converts the **sum of native energies** into it. Its physical justification is the independently established product ensemble and each statistical bridge. It therefore passes J1 for that meaning, but need not pass N2 for the different meaning of a single normalized joint native energy. P §5.1 also permits scoped ledger sums of local normalized-energy contrasts across fixed contexts; this report does not revoke that convention or turn it into a universal fixed-joule or entropy unit. Historical accumulation and a presently coupled joint physical potential are separate objects.

For correlated states on a compatible product support,

\[
-\log p(x,y)=-\log p_1(x)-\log p_2(y)
-\log\frac{p(x,y)}{p_1(x)p_2(y)}.
\tag{29}
\]

The final term cannot be dropped unless the distribution factorizes. Statistical dependence may arise from conditioning, shared causes or marginalization; it is not by itself identification of a bare energetic cross coupling G.

### Explicit failure of componentwise normalization

Let

\[
\Phi(x,y)=\tfrac12k_xx^2+\tfrac12k_yy^2+\kappa xy,
\quad k_xk_y>\kappa^2,\quad k_x,k_y>0.
\]

If each marginal force is separately divided by its local scale,

\[
\mu_x=(k_xx+\kappa y)/\Lambda_1,\quad
\mu_y=(k_yy+\kappa x)/\Lambda_2,
\]

then

\[
\partial_y\mu_x=\kappa/\Lambda_1,\quad
\partial_x\mu_y=\kappa/\Lambda_2.
\tag{30}
\]

For \(\kappa\ne0\) and \(\Lambda_1\ne\Lambda_2\), no scalar V has these marginals on an open rectangle. Around a counterclockwise unit rectangle,

\[
\oint(\mu_xdx+\mu_ydy)=\kappa(\Lambda_2^{-1}-\Lambda_1^{-1})\ne0.
\tag{31}
\]

Each isolated source can have a perfectly valid calibration, yet their proposed assembled marginal is inconsistent. Better individual metrology cannot remove this structural failure.

Trying instead to assign fraction lambda of the interaction to source 1 produces the scalar

\[
V_\lambda=\frac{\Phi_1}{\Lambda_1}+\frac{\Phi_2}{\Lambda_2}
+\left(\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda_1}+\frac{1-\lambda}{\Lambda_2}\right)\Phi_I,
\quad
\partial_\lambda V_\lambda=(\Lambda_1^{-1}-\Lambda_2^{-1})\Phi_I.
\tag{32}
\]

For \(k_x=k_y=1,\kappa=1/2,x=y=1,\Lambda_1=1,\Lambda_2=2\), the unchanged native energy is 3/2 but \(V_\lambda=1+\lambda/4\). Repartitioning the same interaction changes the supposed total. At one common scale the dependence cancels. Equation (32) is the concrete conflict between decomposition gauge and naive separate normalization; a newly chosen V_lambda does not reproduce both marginals in (30).

### Different baths need not possess even one covariance scale

A frozen algebraic example of (14) has

\[
A=K_\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_0B_0^{\mathsf T}=2\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&-1/2\\-1/2&5/4\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{33}
\]

Here Sigma is positive definite (determinant 11/16) and

\[
K_\Phi\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/4\\-1/4&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_\Phi\Sigma+\Sigma K_\Phi=2\operatorname{diag}(1,2).
\]

No scalar Lambda makes Sigma=Lambda K_Phi^-1. The example is consistent with an overdamped two-bath model with different noise strengths; the Brownian-gyrator literature provides the close physical precedent [X07]. The matrices are an illustrative calculation here, not published measurement data or a simulated EBU outcome. Multi-reservoir thermodynamics assigns heat/entropy to the relevant baths under its physical assumptions; it does not identify every such sum with a subsystem state potential [X08].

## 15. Calibration is not coupling or conversion efficiency

Calibration maps answer how two descriptions represent the same quantity. Coupling specifies how components of a joint system influence one another. A device can couple two already commensurable energies inefficiently; it has not changed the joule or generated a root exchange rate.

For example, an input of 2 J produces 1 J useful output and 1 J dissipated to the environment. Efficiency is 1/2. Both energy meters still use the same joule, with unit-conversion factor 1. Taking efficiency as \(c_{ij}=1/2\) confuses different physical endpoints and omitted dissipation with two descriptions of one contrast. Reverse-process efficiencies would then produce spurious calibration-loop failure. The complete physical boundary identifies the energy disposition; thermodynamic irreversibility belongs to that process, not the denomination map.

Conversely, a coordinate-unit change with \(c_{ij}=1000\) can exist between uncoupled measurement descriptions without any physical transfer at all. Energy-based interconnection frameworks distinguish stored energy, power ports and dissipation explicitly [X09]. EBU's source/root bridge and joint-factor accounting must preserve the same distinction; no allocation price or resource exchange market is derived from coupling.

## 16. State-dependent normalization: exact derivatives

Let \(A(x)=\Phi(x)-\Phi^*\), with \(\Phi^*\) constant in x, and \(\Lambda(x)>0\). Set \(g=\nabla\Phi\), \(\ell=\nabla\Lambda\), \(L_\Lambda=\nabla^2\Lambda\). Product differentiation gives

\[
\nabla V=\frac{g}{\Lambda}-\frac{A\ell}{\Lambda^2},
\tag{34}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\nabla^2V=\frac{K_\Phi}{\Lambda}
-\frac{g\ell^{\mathsf T}+\ell g^{\mathsf T}}{\Lambda^2}
-\frac{A L_\Lambda}{\Lambda^2}
+\frac{2A\ell\ell^{\mathsf T}}{\Lambda^3}.}
\tag{35}
\]

At a stationary native reference with A=0 and g=0, \(H(x^*)=K_\Phi(x^*)/\Lambda(x^*)\). Smoothness then allows a local quadratic Taylor term, not an exact constant-H field over a finite domain. At another native stationary point g=0 but A need not vanish, so the last two terms remain. At a stationary point of V, \(g=A\ell/\Lambda\), and (35) reduces to \(K_\Phi/\Lambda-A L_\Lambda/\Lambda^2\). These stationary conditions must not be interchanged.

**Counterexample N3.** For \(\Phi=x^2/2,\Phi^*=0,\Lambda=1+x^2\),

\[
V=\frac{x^2}{2(1+x^2)},\quad
V'=\frac{x}{(1+x^2)^2},\quad
V''=\frac{1-3x^2}{(1+x^2)^3}.
\tag{36}
\]

At x=1, the Hessian is −1/4, whereas \(K_\Phi/\Lambda=1/2\). The native energy remains convex. A positive but state-varying scale can destroy normalized convexity and the positive-compliance interpretation. Constant H survives only if the complete expression (35) is constant for the actual Phi and Lambda; special cancellations are possible and must be proved, not presumed.

A second ambiguity is equally consequential:

\[
\frac{d\Phi}{\Lambda}\ne d\left(\frac{\Phi-\Phi^*}{\Lambda}\right)
\quad\text{when the extra scale term is nonzero}.
\tag{37}
\]

Indeed, \(d(\Lambda^{-1}d\Phi)=-\Lambda^{-2}d\Lambda\wedge d\Phi\). On an open multivariate domain, a nonparallel scale gradient generally makes that one-form nonclosed. If \(\Lambda=L(\Phi)\), it is locally integrated by \(\int d\Phi/L(\Phi)\), generally not the ratio in (37); global periods still matter. Thus a proposed spatial “EBU temperature” does not automatically normalize a force field into a scalar potential.

If the reference itself depends on the differentiated state, replace g and K_Phi in (34)–(35) by the gradient and Hessian of the entire numerator A. Treating a moving reference as constant would omit further terms. Fixed-context partial derivatives and total derivatives along a changing context are different operations.

## 17. Recursive state and moving calibration

Use \(z_{t+1}=F(z_t,a_t,\xi_t)\) for the recursive physical state and retain theta for its field context. Lambda denotes only the normalization scale. H, Lambda, cross factors G and Xi are functions of that state/context when the physical contract says so; they are not five unrelated controls that may be tuned independently.

The recursive state must retain whatever determines both current contrasts and the joint next-state/value law: resource coordinates, geometry, temperature or other scale-generating state, constraints, interactions and required memory. Calibration records and uncertainty describe knowledge of that state; a software record is not automatically an additional physical degree of freedom. RF §8's joint-law sufficiency requirement remains the applicable inherited criterion. Equal present curvature, covariance or scalar value does not establish equal responses to future actions.

For a changing physical field the exact differential is

\[
dV=\frac{d(\Phi-\Phi^*)}{\Lambda}
-\frac{\Phi-\Phi^*}{\Lambda^2}\,d\Lambda.
\tag{38}
\]

For the quadratic representation \(y=x-x^*(\theta)\),

\[
dV=(Hy)^{\mathsf T}dx-(Hy)^{\mathsf T}dx^*
+\tfrac12y^{\mathsf T}(dH)y.
\tag{39}
\]

Where the fixed-context normalization identity is valid, context changes satisfy \(dH=dK_\Phi/\Lambda-K_\Phi d\Lambda/\Lambda^2\), and on a fixed positive accessible space \(d\Xi=-\Xi(dH)\Xi\). Changing constraints/rank additionally changes the accessible space; this inverse derivative alone is then insufficient. These are bookkeeping/differential identities, not a new dynamical equation for the physical parameters.

M §6 already establishes the moving-calibration issue. If \(V'=c(\theta)V+d(\theta)\), an across-context endpoint difference is

\[
E'=c_{\rm pre}V_{\rm pre}-c_{\rm post}V_{\rm post}
+d_{\rm pre}-d_{\rm post},
\tag{40}
\]

not generally \(cE\). A changing offset cancels only within its fixed context. A moving physical reference can represent real field change rather than gauge. Context drift, passive motion and action contributions must be distinguished using the existing complete-state contrast framework; normalizing at only one endpoint can erase the others.

Historical entries retain their original physical context, source identification and denomination. A later calibrated translation can provide an explicitly identified comparison view; it does not silently replace past values or supply absent calibration. If a change leaves root compatibility unresolved, maintain tagged vector components. Joint-field cross terms evolve only through physically identified changes of the joint scalar and state. Schur reduction or replacing hidden variables by their covariance cannot by itself close this recursion.

## 19. Scarcity: what is clarified and what remains to construct

Five effects must be distinguished:

| Effect | Meaning | What does not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|
| State position | A resource coordinate lies away from its physical reference | That the chosen displacement represents a socially relevant deficit |
| Native curvature change | The constitutive cost/response of displacement changes | That stiffness must increase monotonically with depletion |
| Normalization-scale change | The same native contrast is expressed relative to another physically changing scale | A demand, welfare or scarcity multiplier |
| Joint interaction | One subsystem changes another's marginal through a justified joint factor | A market exchange rate or automatic beneficial sign |
| Deficit semantics | A physical source construction identifies what depletion/restoration means | A universal interpretation from the word “resource” alone |

At fixed Lambda, \(\Xi=\Lambda K_\Phi^{-1}\) measures normalized softness, not abundance. Raising Lambda at fixed native K increases Xi and reduces the normalized penalty of a fixed displacement; it need not signal greater scarcity. Within one fixed context a positive scale cannot reverse the native action sign. Across a changing context, (35) and (38) show why the conclusion differs.

A scarcity-compatible field still needs a justified resource coordinate and boundary, a physically identified reference and native scalar, a scale/root bridge, complete joint interactions, and a demonstrated relationship between depletion, that scalar and restorative actions over the declared domain. For example, a construction would have to establish that relevant depletion raises the complete V and that a specified restorative action lowers it after all source and boundary effects are included. This is a theoretical obligation, not an apparatus design or an inference from H's positivity.

The contribution is to prevent arbitrary price-like weights from being disguised as physics and to make omitted burdens visible in joint accounting. Those properties may support transparent resource decisions. They do not prove efficient allocation, fairness, welfare gain, or that the specific scarcity mechanism has been physically constructed. W1's scoped result and all E1a dispositions remain unchanged.
