## 1. Main result and its limits

**A. SOURCE-FACTOR EMBEDDING AND ORIENTATION ARE MATHEMATICALLY COMPATIBLE
WITH THE CLEARED RECURSIVE EBU ARCHITECTURE UNDER EXPLICIT CONDITIONS.**

A physically identified source factor can have its own endpoint contribution
inside one compatible joint potential. That contribution can be evaluated from
sufficient current state, retain the root denomination and telescope without
replaying historical action values. None of these facts makes its contribution
the total registered-action settlement or selects its allocation to an actor.

Three findings resolve the missing interface:

1. **Algebraic embedding is insufficient.** For any scalar candidate one can
   write `𝒱=φ_s+(𝒱−φ_s)`. Scientific admission additionally requires independent
   physical identification, common denomination, a justified remainder and
   interaction accounting. Refactorization can change a factor's sign while
   leaving the joint potential and all total settlements unchanged.
2. **Source and total orientation are distinct properties.** That an
   identified source contribution carries a declared physical sign, and that
   the complete action value carries it, are two separate mathematical facts.
   Neither implies the other. Their conjunction is strictly stronger than
   either in the unrestricted class. All three situations occur and none is
   eliminated by the current foundation alone. None of them is a mode, branch
   or regime of valuation: each is a property of an already defined quantity.
3. **Exact embedding gives an exact sign-margin test.** With
   `E_tot=E_s+E_rest` and declared sign `s∈{−1,+1}`, the identified source
   contrast and the complete action share that sign precisely when
   `M_s=sE_s>0` and `sE_rest>−M_s`. A sign-aligned remainder needs no magnitude
   ceiling. A sufficiently adverse remainder defeats the total sign.

The repaired W1 finding is preserved: its canonical water coordinate has the
requested signs on its restricted domain; its selected complete finite-bath

A uniform positive orientation margin is a useful robust certificate, but is
stronger than pointwise strict orientation. Arbitrarily small actions can have
positive margins tending to zero. This distinction is essential for later
uncertainty qualification and is made explicit below.

EBU has one valuation architecture: a complete multidimensional physical
state, an admitted joint potential, its differential or marginal covector, a

**One settlement channel.** There is one complete finite action EBU value,

differential is not another EBU settlement. A local action slope is not another
EBU settlement. A Möbius coefficient is not another EBU settlement. A

diagnoses or bounds the one value; none competes with it.

Orientation statements are ordinary properties of those already defined

## 3. Objects, domains, events and orientation labels

Let `Z` be the complete declared physical state domain. Context, boundaries,
reservoirs and present memory belong to `z∈Z` whenever they can affect an
endpoint or its value. Fix the model/calibration version over each comparison.
A registered action has an identified transition `z⁺=A_a(z,ξ)` with admitted
input/outcome `ξ`, both endpoints in `Z`. A separate passive map is `P`.
No value below requires a differential equation to generate those endpoints.

A finite factor family has support projections `p_α:Z→Z_{S_α}`. Write
`φ_α(z)` for the pullback `φ_α(p_αz)`. All potentials are finite real,
single-valued functions on the compared endpoints. Differentiability is an
additional assumption only where used. Infinite families need convergence
and interchange hypotheses; no infinite sum is used in the main theorems.

The three objects are

\[
\phi_s:Z_{S_s}\to\mathbb R,\qquad
\mathcal V:Z\to\mathbb R,\qquad
E_{\rm tot}(a;z,\xi)=\mathcal V(z)-\mathcal V(z^+).
\tag{1}
\]

The factor contrast is `E_s=φ_s(z)−φ_s(z⁺)`. It is an endpoint contribution
only when its physical identity and embedding are justified; otherwise it is
at most a declared scoped diagnostic. A factor, the joint potential and the
settlement are not interchangeable objects. `E_tot` here denotes EBU action
value; W1's conserved physical energy is written `H_0` when quoted in §14.

An orientation domain is a set of **state/action/outcome comparisons**
`Ω_s={(z,a,ξ): z∈D_s, z⁺∈D_s, a permitted}`. Merely specifying initial states
is insufficient. Declare depletion/degradation and restoration classes on this
set and a desired sign

\[
s(z,a,\xi)=-1\ \text{on }\mathcal A_-,\qquad
s(z,a,\xi)=+1\ \text{on }\mathcal A_+.
\tag{2}
\]

The notation `s(a)` below abbreviates this contextual label. The labels must
be based on the declared physical meaning and fixed independently of observing
the sign one wishes to certify. They are not social desirability labels.
Strict orientation means `sE>0`. A separately declared neutral class may
require `E=0`; one may not reinterpret a failed strict sign as neutral afterward.
An uncertain class boundary must be included in the qualification domain.

If one action depletes one source but restores another, their source labels
can differ. A claim about the complete action value needs a **single consistent
total-action label** or an explicit restricted class. Requiring both `E_tot<0`
and `E_tot>0` for the same event is inconsistent. Claims about separately
identified source factors carry no such logical conflict merely because their
individual signs differ, and a claim about both source and total inherits the
need for compatible total labels. This is an additional specification
obligation on whichever claim is made.

## 4. Physical root contract and Theorem 1 — Positive-scale orientation invariance

### 4.1 Four maps, four different obligations

A root `V_R` specifies a physical quantity kind, orientation, reference/unit,
comparison domain and independent calibration with uncertainty. A raw source
coordinate `Q_s` reaches a candidate factor through distinct steps:

| Step | Mathematical form / obligation |
|---|---|
| Raw sensor conversion | `y_s→Q_s`, possibly nonlinear or decreasing; instrument response and units must be known |
| Source physical mapping | `Q_s` together with required state/context maps to a justified potential quantity `q_s=F_s(Q_s,z,λ)`; this constitutive relation is not supplied by sensor calibration |
| Same-kind calibration | Valid representations of the same `q_s` obey an independently fixed positive affine unit map into root coordinates |
| Joint embedding | The root-normalized `φ_s` and the identified remainder/interactions reconstruct or approximate the same complete physical `𝒱` |

For a quantity kind with an independently specified positive root unit `q_R`,
one possible normalized source coordinate is `(q_s−q_s*)/q_R`. An intrinsically
dimensionless physical quantity can instead be used directly. This notation
is not a universal identification with energy, entropy or a fixed number of
joules. A further physical bridge is required when `Q_s` is of a different
kind. Dividing an unrelated score by a constant is insufficient.

An admissible `v_s=c v_R+d` compares matching representations of the **same
quantity**, not arbitrary values of a trap and a water tank at unrelated states.
Once both are expressed in the root unit, a same-contrast comparison has slope
one. A source's state dependence and functional form may differ from the root
realization; its unit cannot be a freely chosen multiplier.

Independent calibration here means independent of the desired sign or fitted
agreement being tested. It does not mean statistically independent errors.
Shared reference, geometry and temperature errors remain correlated.

### 4.2 Theorem 1

**Assumptions.** One finite scalar factor `φ_s`, matching endpoints, a fixed
representation change `φ_s'=cφ_s+d` with independently justified constants
`c>0,d` on the comparison domain. Context remains fixed or has an aligned
extended-state declaration.

**Statement.**

\[
E_s'=cE_s,\qquad \operatorname{sign}E_s'=\operatorname{sign}E_s.
\tag{3}
\]

Positive calibration preserves zeros and orientation. A constant gauge changes
neither the value nor its sign. It cannot repair a wrong physical sign.

**Proof.** Subtract `cφ_s(z⁺)+d` from `cφ_s(z)+d`; the offset cancels and a
positive multiplier preserves the ordering of real numbers. **QED.**

**Removed assumptions.** With `φ_s(x)=x`, endpoints `0→1` give `E_s=−1`.
Replacing it by `−φ_s` gives `+1`; this is a physical orientation reversal,
not harmless calibration. `c=0` destroys the contrast. If `d=d(λ)` and context
changes, `d(λ_pre)−d(λ_post)` need not vanish. A varying `c` introduces extra
endpoint terms as well. A raw detector's negative response slope is different:
it can be inverted to recover the correct physical coordinate before (3).

**EBU meaning and authority.** This is F/B/RF gauge algebra stated explicitly
for a factor. It preserves the ruler without freezing future action values.
Neither this theorem nor root calibration makes the factor an actor payment.

## 5. Theorem 2 — Exact factor decomposition and invariant total settlement

**Assumptions.** A common state, boundary, context, root denomination and pair
of actual endpoints. A finite family of single-valued factors with an exact
identity on the full admitted domain,

\[
\mathcal V(z)=b+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal F}\phi_\alpha(p_\alpha z),
\qquad b\ \text{constant}.
\tag{4}
\]

Scientific use additionally requires the independent factor identification
and unique counting specified below. Algebra alone only requires (4).

**Statement.**

\[
E_{\rm tot}=\sum_\alpha E_\alpha,\qquad
E_\alpha=\phi_\alpha(z)-\phi_\alpha(z^+).
\tag{5}
\]

Any other exact legitimate factorization of the **same** `𝒱` gives the same
total, irrespective of its individual contributions. The factors may overlap
in variables and may be internally nonseparable.

**Proof.** Subtract (4) at the two endpoints and distribute the finite sum.
The constant cancels. Repeating this calculation for any other factorization
yields the same `𝒱(z)−𝒱(z⁺)`. **QED.**

**Removed assumptions.** Summing factors at different post-states, fields or
unit scales is not subtraction of (4). Duplicating the one physical term
`𝒱(x)=x` as two copies of `x` produces `2x`: the comparison `0→1` changes
from `−1` to `−2`. Two physically distinct contributions may legitimately
have identical formulas; duplication concerns physical identity, not visual
similarity of formulas. Conditional infinite series cannot be freely reordered.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This is F §13's linearity of differences and
RF's joint assembly, not a new definition of settlement. “Factor settlement”
means decomposition of one total, not adding a second issue of each component
on top of that total. For one registered actor, the authorized total entry is
`E_tot`; for several actors, an additional allocation must sum to `E_tot`.
An identified source contribution does not alter this rule.

### 5.1 Unique counting and locality

Use a factor-incidence hypergraph: state blocks are vertices, factor identities
are hyperedges with specified support, boundary and constitutive provenance.
Each physical contribution has one identity, one declared scope, one endpoint
change per registered event and one place in the joint sum. Several physical
factors may share a support. A coupled term is one hyperedge, not a duplicate
term belonging independently to each endpoint source.

If a factor is physically split into subterms, their sum must reconstruct that
factor and their counting record must replace, not supplement, the original.
Event identity is also necessary: recording the same transition twice doubles
its entry even with a correct factorization. An unchanged inherited factor
contributes zero; an old historical amount is never added to a new contrast.

For changed state blocks `T`, factors with `S_α∩T=∅` cancel. A touched coupled
factor is evaluated in full. Response support, reservoir/boundary state and
long-range dependence must also be included; structural locality of a formula
alone does not prove locality of the action's response. If omitted contrasts
obey `Σ_omitted |E_α|≤ε_local`, their total omission is bounded by `ε_local`.
Otherwise the local total is uncertified. This is F §13 and RF §11, preserved.

## 6. Theorem 3 — Physical identifiability and the refactorization obstruction

**Assumptions.** Fix one joint potential and comparison graph whose edges are
the source actions of interest; add formal reverse edges for comparisons.
Consider the class of factorizations consistent with the **same independent
physical source identity**, root calibration and boundaries. For two such
representations let `g=φ_s'−φ_s`, with the remainder changed by `−g` so that
the joint potential is identical. The shifts must respect the allowed supports.

**Statement.**

\[
E_s'-E_s=g(z)-g(z^+).
\tag{6}
\]

The source's endpoint contribution is identifiable across this class if and
only if every permitted `g` is constant on every connected component of the
comparison graph. If all states are joined by comparisons, this means a single
constant gauge. Independent physical identification sufficient to fix the
factor up to such gauges therefore fixes its action contributions. Total
settlement remains identifiable even when the individual contribution is not.

**Proof.** Subtraction gives (6). Equality of all source edge values is
therefore equivalent to equality of `g` at the ends of every edge. Propagating
along a finite comparison walk makes `g` constant on a component. Conversely,
component constancy makes every edge difference zero. Theorem 2 fixes the total.
**QED.**

**Counterexample: same units, arbitrary signs.** On `x∈[0,1]`, let `𝒱=x`,
`φ_s=x`, `Ψ=0`. The redistribution `g=−2x` gives `φ_s'=−x`, `Ψ'=2x`, still
`𝒱=φ_s'+Ψ'`. For `0→1` the source contrast changes from `−1` to `+1`, while
the total stays `−1`. Unit labels and exact summation have not selected a
physical source. No independent physical evidence authorizes this shift.

The identity `Ψ:=𝒱−φ_s` is thus a formal embedding for every candidate. It is
not a constitutive derivation of `Ψ`, a proof of unique counting, or a physical
certificate for `φ_s`. Calibration of the total alone cannot identify its
parts. Likewise, calibration of a scalar factor's scale alone does not select
its physically correct functional form.

### 6.1 Gauge classification

| Transformation | Effect and permission |
|---|---|
| Constant factor offset, with compensating reference if needed | Harmless to all endpoint contrasts |
| Function constant on each comparison component | Harmless for that comparison family only; cross-component/context events require compatible anchors |
| State-dependent transfer between factors | Preserves the total but generally changes component values; permissible as a declared alternative decomposition only when its physical meaning is independently justified |
| Redistribution selected to improve a sign | Scientifically inadmissible; it changes the source identity without justification |

State-dependent functions invariant under every present action can be harmless
to action values without being constant globally. Passive evolution or a later
expanded action family may connect previously separate classes and remove that
freedom. Absolute potential levels and action contrasts have different
identifiability requirements. Bounded physical identification uncertainty can
instead yield a set of possible contrasts; sign certification then needs the
same sign over that entire set, not selection of a favorable representative.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This is a precise obstruction underlying F §13,
RF §7 and W1 §6.3. It prevents sign engineering while permitting legitimate
factorization choices. It does not claim that algebra alone can establish
physical identity or that every physical decomposition is unique.

## 7. Theorem 4 — Nonseparability and coupled factors

**Assumptions.** For the converse, `D=I×J` is a product of connected open
intervals, `𝒱∈C²(D)`, in declared independent physical coordinates `(x,y)`.
For the necessary direction, any open domain suffices locally.

**Statement.**

\[
\mathcal V(x,y)=f(x)+g(y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_{xy}^2\mathcal V=0\ \text{everywhere on }I\times J.
\tag{7}
\]

A nonzero mixed partial at even one point prevents independent unary
factorization on a neighborhood of that point. It does not prevent a coupled
interaction factor or an exact single-valued potential.

**Proof.** Independent unary functions have zero mixed derivative. Conversely,
fix `(x₀,y₀)`. Vanishing `∂_y(∂_x𝒱)` on connected `J` makes `∂_x𝒱(x,y)`
independent of `y`. Integrating on `I` gives
`𝒱(x,y)−𝒱(x₀,y)=𝒱(x,y₀)−𝒱(x₀,y₀)`. Set
`f(x)=𝒱(x,y₀)` and `g(y)=𝒱(x₀,y)−𝒱(x₀,y₀)`. **QED.**

The proof extends to products of connected open coordinate blocks when every
cross-block derivative vanishes: each block gradient is independent of the
others. A coordinate change mixing physical sources can change separability;
it does not preserve the original source identities automatically. On a
constraint manifold, use actual independent coordinates and stated supports,
not inaccessible ambient derivatives.

**Removed-domain counterexample.** Connectedness alone is insufficient. Let
`D=((-2,1/2)×(-2,2)) ∪ ((-2,2)×(1,2)) ∪ ((-2,2)×(-2,-1))`.
On the upper strip set `𝒱=h(x)=(x−1/2)_+³`; on the lower strip and connecting
left strip set `𝒱=0`. These definitions agree on overlaps and give a `C²`
function on connected open `D` with zero mixed partial everywhere. But the
upper-minus-lower difference is zero at `x=0` and `1/8` at `x=1`. A unary
sum would make that difference independent of `x`. Thus the product-domain
hypothesis, or another sufficient fiber-compatibility hypothesis, matters.

**Interaction counterexample.** `𝒱=xy` has mixed partial one; it is itself a
valid coupled scalar term and needs no unary decomposition. W1 provides the
already-cleared physical-model example in §14.

### 7.1 What can be called the source contribution with interactions?

Suppose a physically identified representation is
`𝒱=φ_W(w)+φ_E(e)+φ_WE(w,e)`. Then `E_tot=E_W+E_E+E_WE` exactly.
The exclusive water contrast `E_W` is meaningful if its identity is independently
fixed. The interaction contrast `E_WE` remains a separate joint term. This is
a sufficient convention for analyzing the exclusive water orientation, but
not for inferring total orientation or assigning the interaction to an actor.

An allocation `E_W+λE_WE` and `E_E+(1−λ)E_WE`, for fixed `λ`, closes for every
`λ`; closure does not select `λ`. “Water-exclusive,” “water plus the whole
interaction,” and “interaction unallocated” answer different scoped questions.
Crediting the whole interaction to both sources doubles it. A state-dependent
allocation of the potential introduces endpoint differences of its weights;
it cannot silently be treated as a constant share of `E_WE`.

Invariant quantities are the joint potential contrast, and the contrasts of
independently fixed factors up to Theorem 3's gauges. Without that physical
identification, neither exclusive factors nor arbitrary source bundles have
canonical individual values. A reference-anchored or averaged algebraic
interaction decomposition can be unique **after** its reference/measure is
chosen, but those choices do not create physical ownership or allocation.

**EBU meaning and authority.** Nonseparability is compatible with F §13 and RF
§7. No unary requirement is added to the architecture. This result clarifies
why W1's joint gradient cannot be split into independently priced source nodes.

## 8. Theorem 5 — Source and total orientation under residual factors

**Assumptions.** Independently identified `φ_s` and a justified exact embedding
`𝒱=φ_s+Ψ` on the same comparison domain. Let `E_rest=Ψ(z)−Ψ(z⁺)` and
use the fixed physical label `s` of (2). The remainder may contain other source,
interaction, boundary and reservoir factors and may still depend on source
variables. It need not be independently unary or sign definite.

**Statement.** Define

\[
M_s=sE_s,\qquad M_r=sE_{\rm rest},\qquad
M_{\rm tot}=sE_{\rm tot}=M_s+M_r.
\tag{8}
\]

For every comparison in the certified domain the exact necessary and sufficient
conditions for the source contrast, for the complete action, and for both
together are:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{source:}&M_s>0,\\
\text{complete action:}&M_s+M_r>0,\\
\text{both:}&M_s>0\ \text{and}\ M_r>-M_s.
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]

These are three statements about identified quantities, not three semantics.
For the complete action without an identified additive factorization the
equivalent criterion is directly `s[𝒱(z)−𝒱(z⁺)]>0`, which requires no
artificial decomposition. When `M_s>0`, `|E_rest|<|E_s|` is sufficient for both
to hold but not necessary.
The sharper adverse-residual condition is
`max(0,−M_r)<M_s`. If `M_r≥0`, any magnitude is permitted. Equality
`M_r=−M_s` gives zero total and fails a strict requirement.

**Proof.** Theorem 2 gives (8); the definitions of the three statements give (9).
Since `M_r≥−|E_rest|` and `M_s=|E_s|` when oriented correctly, the absolute
bound ensures a positive sum. Only the negative part of `M_r` can reduce it,
which proves the sharper statement. **QED.**

### 8.1 Source sign and total sign are incomparable; both can hold together

The following are finite **mathematical countermodels**, not new physical
source designs or certification claims. Fix the same factor identities and
root-unit functions `φ_s(w)=(w−2)²`, `Ψ(y)=y`, `𝒱=φ_s+Ψ` on
`[0,3]×[0,10]`. Label a decrease of the stock coordinate `w` as depletion
and its reverse as restoration. Within each test action family include the
listed transition and its reverse. No factor is redefined after selecting a
sign, and no claim that lower stock is universally harmful is needed.

| Test family: depletion endpoint comparison | `E_s` | `E_rest` | `E_tot` | Sign outcome for the action and its reverse |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `(1,10)→(0,0)` | `−3` | `+10` | `+7` | source orientation satisfied; complete-action orientation not satisfied |
| `(3,0)→(2,2)` | `+1` | `−2` | `−1` | complete-action orientation satisfied; source orientation not satisfied |
| `(1,0)→(0,0)` | `−3` | `0` | `−3` | both satisfied |

The reverse comparison negates every contrast and reverses the label, so each
row tests both signs. These identities show that neither sign predicate implies
the other even with an exact decomposition. Physical use additionally requires
independent legitimacy of the chosen factors, labels and transitions; numerical
examples do not supply those premises. The third row demonstrates mathematical
nonemptiness within the foundation's conditional-potential framework, not a
new certified apparatus. W1 supplies the already-derived physical-model
factor/total sign mismatch, with its separate embedding limitation (§14).

**Removed residual condition.** The first row has a correctly oriented source
but an adverse residual larger than its margin. Conversely, an oriented
source with `M_s=1` and `M_r=100` satisfies both statements although the
absolute residual bound fails. A derivative sign at one state does not certify finite-action
orientation over an entire domain; the finite endpoint contrast is controlling.

**EBU meaning and authority.** A source-orientation statement preserves the
total actor settlement `E_tot`, which may carry a different sign. A
complete-action orientation statement constrains that total without promising
each source factor's sign. Asserting both restricts the admissible comparison
family further. None of the three is an alternative settlement: the registered
value is `E_tot` in every case. All preserve F's endpoint definition
and RF's recursion/passive/ledger separation. None independently certifies a
source or determines actor allocation.

### 8.2 Exact, robust and uniform orientation margins

Let `𝒦` be the jointly admissible set of states, actions, outcomes, calibration
parameters, model errors and boundaries. It must retain their physical
constraints and correlations. Certification of a claimed exact sign requires
that its margin be positive for **every** element of `𝒦`.

A stronger certificate with a uniform safety gap is

\[
m_s=\inf_{\omega\in\mathcal K}M_s(\omega)>0,
\qquad
m_{\rm tot}=\inf_{\omega\in\mathcal K}M_{\rm tot}(\omega)>0,
\tag{10}
\]

using the relevant expression for the source contrast, for the complete action,
or for both. A useful sufficient condition for both is `m_s>0` and
`sup_𝒦 max(0,−M_r)<m_s`. It can be conservative
because the weakest source margin and worst residual may not occur together.
The exact robust total test uses their **joint** sum on `𝒦`.

Pointwise strict positivity does not always imply (10). For action sizes
`q∈(0,1]`, the margin `M(q)=q` is positive everywhere but has infimum zero.
This is relevant to W1's allowed arbitrarily small positive transfers. On a
compact uncertainty/action set, a continuous strictly positive margin attains
a positive minimum; otherwise that conclusion needs separate proof. One may
use action-specific or relative error bounds instead of silently imposing a
new positive minimum action size. No numerical tolerance or action cutoff is
chosen here.

For stochastic actions, a positive expected margin does not guarantee the
realized sign. All-outcome certification needs the bound on the permitted
outcome set, or an explicitly weaker almost-sure/probabilistic claim. Example:
a margin `+2` with probability `3/4` and `−1` with probability `1/4` has
positive mean but sometimes the wrong sign. No samples are required to see it.

## 9. Theorem 6 — Recursive source-factor sufficiency

**Assumptions.** Detailed state `z`, retained current state `θ=π(z)`, explicit
new inputs, well-defined action and passive maps preserving the domain. Require:

1. Admitted physical actions and their source/total orientation labels are
   constant on each fiber of `π`, for the inputs being conditioned on.
2. Action and passive state updates descend:
   `πA_a(z,ξ)=Ā_a(θ,ξ)` and `πP(z,ζ)=P̄(θ,ζ)`.
3. The physically identified factor descends:
   `φ_s(z)=f_s(πz)+k_s`, with a fixed gauge. For a joint reduced endpoint
   formula also require `𝒱(z)=v(πz)+k`, or the cleared weaker joint-reward
   sufficiency condition in RF §8.2.
4. Required physical memory, boundary/context input and calibration version
   are retained; an unnamed random input does not conceal dependence on history.

**Statement.** The source action value can be evaluated recursively as

\[
\widetilde\theta_n=\bar A_{a_n}(\theta_n,\xi_n),\quad
\theta_{n+1}=\bar P(\widetilde\theta_n,\zeta_n),\quad
E_{s,n}=f_s(\theta_n)-f_s(\widetilde\theta_n).
\tag{11}
\]

Given equal current retained states and equal new inputs, admissible actions,
values and next retained states agree. No vector of previous action prices is
required. Joint settlement remains that of `𝒱`, and the source state need not
be just the variables appearing explicitly in `φ_s`.

**Proof.** Factor descent and cancellation of `k_s` give (11). Fiber-constant
availability and action descent supply its endpoint independently of the
representative detailed state. Passive descent supplies the next state.
Induction repeats the same argument after every update. Joint-value sufficiency
is exactly RF's cleared condition, now accompanied by the source contrast.
Historical values do not enter any induction step. **QED.**

**Weaker exact version.** It suffices that `E_s(z,a,ξ)` itself be constant on
fibers together with the update/availability conditions. Factor-level descent
is stronger, and is what supplies the reduced scalar formula (11). A hidden
term invariant on all comparisons can cancel without its level being retained.
If only rewards descend, existence of a reduced state potential additionally
requires zero sum on every closed **comparison** walk; it is not automatic.

For stochastic dynamics require equality on fibers of the joint law of
`(E_s,E_tot,θ̃,θ_next)` conditional on the action and declared inputs, including
the corresponding passive law. Equality of separate marginals is insufficient
when their correlations affect future records. Successive conditioning proves
recursive equality in law under retained-state policies. It does not force
independent random realizations to have identical outcomes or make an expected
quote equal realized settlement.

**Counterexample: static formula, insufficient dynamics.** Let
`φ_s(x)=x²/2`, detailed state `(x,h)` with `h∈{−1,+1}`, and action
`A(x,h)=(x+h,h)`. Retain only `θ=x`. At the same `x=1`, the source action
contrasts are `−3/2` and `+1/2`; the next reduced states are 2 and 0. The static
factor is well defined but its endpoint law is not determined by `x`. Retaining
`h` repairs this mathematical omission. It is an abstract state-reduction
counterexample, not adoption of a new physical dynamics.

**Joint sufficiency alone is insufficient for component values.** With
`φ_s=xh`, `Ψ=−xh`, `𝒱=0`, action `x→x+1` and fixed `h`, the projection
`π(x,h)=x` has a perfectly closed zero joint reward and reduced state update,
but `E_s=−h` is hidden. Factor sufficiency must be checked as well.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This specializes RF §8's reduction theorem.
Physical memory survives in current state; historical price replay is absent.
A factor may need remote/interface state to predict its next endpoint even if
its formula has small support. Neither algebraic sufficiency nor this report
proves a small observable finite state for arbitrary real systems. Historical
actor records and calibration provenance remain separate records.

## 10. Theorem 7 — Factor and joint telescoping with passive evolution

**Assumptions.** One fixed factor identity and one single-valued joint
potential, in a consistent root representation. Actual adjacent states satisfy
`z̃_n=A_an(z_n)` and `z_n+1=P_n(z̃_n)`. Context changes have either been
included in a valid extended state/potential or excluded from the comparison.
Define

\[
D_{s,n}=\phi_s(\widetilde z_n)-\phi_s(z_{n+1}),\qquad
D_n=\mathcal V(\widetilde z_n)-\mathcal V(z_{n+1}).
\tag{12}
\]

**Statement.**

\[
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(E_{s,n}+D_{s,n})
=\phi_s(z_0)-\phi_s(z_N),\qquad
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(E_{{\rm tot},n}+D_n)
=\mathcal V(z_0)-\mathcal V(z_N).
\tag{13}
\]

With no passive changes the `D` terms vanish. For an exact factorization, sums
of factor action drops give the joint action drop and sums of factor passive
drops give the joint passive drop. A single factor cannot replace this joint
identity when other factors change.

**Proof.** For each step, adding its action and passive drops cancels the
intermediate `z̃_n`. Summing cancels every adjacent `z_n`; apply the same
calculation to `φ_s` and `𝒱`. Interchange only finite factor and event sums.
**QED.**

**Removed-passive counterexample.** For `φ_s(x)=x`, action `0→1` gives `−1`
and passive return `1→0` gives `+1`. The full physical comparison closes but
the registered-action sum is `−1`, not zero. Adding the passive drop restores
(13); it does not create an actor credit. With no registered action, all actor
balances remain unchanged however much the field moves.

Same-base quotes also fail: F's `φ=x²/2`, successive `0→1→2`, gives
`−1/2−3/2=−2`, whereas two quotes at 0 give `−1`. Rebasing, not old price
replay, supplies the correct increment. Later calibration or field changes do
not reprice previously recorded entries. Changing a factorization between
steps requires an explicitly aligned audit comparison; it is not telescoping
one unchanged factor.

### 10.1 Four loops and a compatibility constraint on labels

| Loop | Exact condition / implication |
|---|---|
| Root calibration loop | Product of slopes one; composed offset zero for aligned potential levels (§13) |
| Source-factor potential loop | Sum of factor drops zero when its full relevant state/context returns and its identity is fixed |
| Joint-potential loop | Sum of all action and passive drops zero when the complete state/context returns |
| Physical carrier loop | Declared mass/energy balances hold with all ports/reservoirs; returning a route label or water volume alone is not return of the complete state |

A closed all-action loop under one potential has zero aggregate settlement.
It need not have zero settlement per actor. With passive intervals, even the
aggregate action sum can be nonzero, balanced by passive drops. Physical energy
and material conservation have their own units and laws; no EBU carrier is
created by writing (13).

Strict orientation also constrains the label graph. Orient each comparison
edge in the direction demanded to have positive potential drop: keep a
restoration edge, reverse a depletion edge as a formal comparison. A directed
cycle in this graph makes a strict scalar orientation impossible, because it
would sum strictly positive drops around a zero cycle. On a finite graph,
acyclicity is also sufficient for **some scalar** satisfying the strict
inequalities: assign decreasing potential levels along a topological ordering.
This construction does not physically identify that potential and cannot
replace the prescribed `φ_s` or `𝒱`. For a prescribed potential, each inequality
in (9) still must hold. Self-loops demanding a strict sign fail immediately.
For infinite graphs no converse is asserted here.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This is F §§12, 17–18 and RF §§10, 14 applied
also to a source factor. It exposes inconsistent semantic labels without
selecting any orientation statement or introducing actor-level conservation.

## 11. Theorem 8 — A sharply sufficient source-factor embedding criterion

“Certified” below names a contract whose premises must be established. This
report proves what follows **if** they hold; it certifies no new physical
source by declaration. Separate three statuses: a scoped diagnostic; an
embedded source factor; and an orientation-certified embedded source factor.
A wrong sign can defeat the third without making the physical scalar meaningless.

**Assumptions / certificate obligations.**

| ID | Obligation |
|---|---|
| C1 | Independently constituted physical meaning of `Q_s`, its map to `φ_s`, the joint `𝒱`, and remainder/interactions; no circular fit to the desired sign |
| C2 | Same-kind root bridge and independently determined positive calibration; valid path composition with uncertainty and sufficient nonconstant comparisons to identify a slope |
| C3 | Declared state variables, support/measure where relevant, domain, boundaries, context, action family and physical orientation labels; all endpoints lie inside |
| C4 | Finite single-valued potentials; appropriate regularity only for derivative claims; fixed or aligned gauges |
| C5 | Exact physical embedding `𝒱=φ_s+Ψ`, or an independently justified bounded residual on the declared domain (§12), not merely defining `Ψ` by subtraction |
| C6 | All interactions, reservoirs and boundary terms identified once; `Ψ` may depend on source variables; factor provenance prevents duplicate physical contributions |
| C7 | Source identity independently fixed up to Theorem 3's harmless gauges, or a declared identification set with bounded ambiguity; no arbitrary sign-changing redistribution |
| C8 | Action availability, source value and next-state law recursively sufficient; joint settlement also satisfies RF's sufficient-state requirements |
| C9 | Action/passive transitions and any shared/concurrent event boundary identified; no duplicate event and no passive actor issuance |
| C10 | Actual affected spatial support and boundaries included, or omitted contrasts bounded; needed delays and memory retained |
| C11 | Approximation, calibration, state-observation and numerical uncertainty separately bounded, with dependencies retained and qualification valid over the required horizon |
| C12 | For any claimed source or complete-action orientation, the relevant exact or error-adjusted margins are positive throughout the declared uncertainty/action set; neutral cases separately specified |

**Definition.** An EBU-admissible source-factor embedding on a specified
comparison domain is the data `(Z,Ω_s,root,φ_s,𝒱,Ψ,π,A,P)` together with
physical identification/calibration records and declared bounds satisfying
C1–C11. It is *exact* when the embedding and retained-state claims are exact;
it is *bounded* when declared embedding/evaluation error sets replace exact
values. Approximate reduced dynamics additionally require RF's separately
justified horizon-valid closure bounds. An orientation-certified
embedding additionally satisfies C12 for the sign statement actually claimed.
These are
factor-specific admission conditions within the existing conditional physical
framework, not a change to frozen authority.

**Statement.** C1–C11 with exact embedding and exact sufficient state produce
a consistent, root-denominated embedded factor with incremental current-state
valuation, unique physical counting, invariant joint settlement and the factor/
joint audit identities. With bounded embedding/evaluation and sufficient
endpoint state, the contrast statements hold with Theorem 9's explicit error bounds, not as exact point-valued
claims. Approximate recursive dynamics require separate, horizon-valid state
and reward bounds; Theorem 9 consumes such endpoint bounds but does not derive
them or prevent their accumulation.
Where identification remains set-valued, the identities apply to each admitted
physical model; a unique point value is not asserted across that set. C12
supplies precisely the additional sign certification selected in (9).
Thus each of the three sign statements is compatible with the cleared
architecture under these conditions, without changing its endpoint definition
or passive/actor boundary.

**Proof.** C1–C4 supply the physical and mathematical objects; their physical
truth is a premise, not a conclusion of calculus. Theorems 1 and 10 give a
consistent positive root scale. C5–C7 and Theorems 2–4 give a legitimate exact
or bounded decomposition with identifiable source contrasts and accounted
interactions. C8 and Theorem 6 provide recursive values and updates. C9–C10
permit the registered-event and local evaluation of the complete affected
factors, with no duplicate contributions. Theorem 7 supplies the audit
identities. C11 invokes Theorem 9 where errors are nonzero. Theorem 5 and C12
supply the chosen sign guarantee. None replaces the joint settlement by the
source contrast. **QED.**

This is a **sharply sufficient composition criterion**, not an algorithm that
infers physical meaning from arbitrary formulas and not a claim that these
are the weakest premises possible. For example, factor-level state descent
can be weakened to contrast sufficiency, and an action-invariant ambiguity
need not defeat valuation. Exact empirical calibration is not manufactured:
an actual certificate normally supplies an uncertainty set, hence a bounded
claim. Approximate embedding cannot be promoted to an exact settlement without
resolving the residual.

**Removed-assumption examples and necessity within the contract.** Removing
C1/C7 permits the sign-engineering example in §6; C2 permits arbitrary unit
drift; C5 permits incompatible factors despite common units; C6/C9 permits
double counting; C8 permits §9's hidden-state failure; C10 permits omitted
physical effects without an error claim; C11/C12 permits errors to cross zero.
These counterexamples show that the corresponding scientific obligations
cannot simply be dropped. They do not claim necessity of one particular
sensor design, finite state dimension or decomposition representation.

**EBU meaning and authority.** The criterion fills the factor-specific interface
below F's physical-hypothesis boundary and RF's closure theorem. It requires no
foundation amendment. Useful consequences are auditable units, local incremental
evaluation and preservation of physical memory without price-history replay.
They do not prove societal benefit or select institutional use of the values.

## 12. Theorem 9 — Approximate embedding and sign stability

**Assumptions.** On the declared domain, the same independently identified
factor and remainder form `F=φ_s+Ψ`, and

\[
\mathcal V(z)=F(z)+r(z),\qquad |r(z)|\leq\varepsilon_{\rm emb}(z).
\tag{14}
\]

All compared quantities have the common root denomination. Bounds refer to
the entire admitted physical/calibration uncertainty set, not just a nominal
state. A bounded algebraic residual alone does not establish C1 or C7.

**Statement: embedding error.** For the actual endpoints,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm tot}-(E_s+E_\Psi)&=r(z)-r(z^+),\\
|E_{\rm tot}-(E_s+E_\Psi)|
&\leq\varepsilon_{\rm emb}(z)+\varepsilon_{\rm emb}(z^+)
\leq2\varepsilon_{\rm emb}
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

when a common bound `ε_emb` applies. This factor of two is sharp without more
information about the residual's correlation between endpoints. A constant
residual cancels exactly.

If measured endpoints `ẑ,ẑ⁺` obey distances at most `ρ_pre,ρ_post`, and `F`
is Lipschitz on the respective neighborhoods with constants `L_pre,L_post`,
then

\[
\left|E_{\rm tot}-[F(\hat z)-F(\hat z^+)]\right|
\leq\varepsilon_{{\rm emb},\rm pre}
+\varepsilon_{{\rm emb},\rm post}
+L_{\rm pre}\rho_{\rm pre}+L_{\rm post}\rho_{\rm post}.
\tag{16}
\]

Add separately justified evaluation/calibration bounds at the two measured
endpoints if the evaluated function is itself approximate. A useful error
inventory is embedding/model residual, state observation, root/constitutive
calibration, omitted locality/boundary terms, and numerical evaluation. Each
physical uncertainty is counted once. For example, a boundary omission already
included in `r` must not be added again. Triangle bounds are safe but may be
conservative; shared endpoint/reference errors may cancel. No independence or
root-sum-of-squares rule is presumed. Direct propagation over a joint admissible
set can be sharper.

**Statement: sign stability.** For either a factor contrast or a total contrast,
suppose `|Ê−E|≤ε_E`. With independently fixed desired sign `s`,

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
sE>\varepsilon_E &\Longrightarrow\ s\widehat E>0,\\
s\widehat E>\varepsilon_E &\Longrightarrow\ sE>0.
\end{array}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus `|E|>ε_E` preserves the sign of `E`; it gives the *desired* sign only if
`E` already has that orientation. For a domain-wise uniform robust guarantee,
one sufficient certificate is

\[
\inf_{\omega\in\mathcal K}
\bigl[s(\omega)E(\omega)-\varepsilon_E(\omega)\bigr]>0.
\tag{18}
\]

Here `𝒦` includes states, actions, outcomes and joint uncertainty. Pointwise
strict positivity of the bracket suffices for pointwise sign stability even
if its infimum is zero. Claiming both orientations requires the factor and the
total bounds; an error bound for the total does not bound the accuracy of an
arbitrary component.
Embedding error (15) alone does not prove accuracy of the factor's constitutive
physical model.

**Proof.** Subtract (14) at the endpoints. The triangle inequality gives (15).
Insert and subtract `F(ẑ)` and `F(ẑ⁺)`, then apply the two Lipschitz bounds to
obtain (16). Since multiplication by `s∈{−1,+1}` preserves absolute error,
`sÊ≥sE−ε_E` and `sE≥sÊ−ε_E`. These give (17) and (18). **QED.**

**Sharpness and removed-assumption examples.** If `r(z)=+ε` and
`r(z⁺)=−ε`, (15) equals `2ε`. At `sE=ε_E` the estimate can be zero, so a
nonstrict margin is insufficient. If `sE=1/2` and the error is `−1`, the
estimated sign reverses. Bounds holding only at a nominal parameter do not
cover an uncertified physical parameter outside it. A purely positive scale
uncertainty, by contrast, cannot reverse a nonzero sign at all: if
`c∈[c_-,c_+]`, `c_->0`, a certified margin `m` becomes at least `c_-m`.
Positive scales approaching zero preserve pointwise sign without a uniform
positive margin. A coarse additive-error enclosure may lose that distinction.

**Telescoping with one global approximation.** If the same `F` and residual
`r` apply to every adjacent endpoint, intermediate residuals cancel. Summing
approximate action **and passive** contrasts differs from the true complete
path contrast by `r(z_0)−r(z_N)`, bounded by the two endpoint bounds, not
necessarily `2Nε`. This tighter bound does not apply to action-only sums with
unaccounted passive gaps, or to independently changing models/calibrations at
each event. Those cases need the actual uncancelled terms and model-transition
bridges. Historical actor entries are not silently repriced.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This extends R's endpoint-error accounting and
RF's bounded reduction to the factor interface. It provides a form for later
qualification without selecting tolerances, action cutoffs, experimental
power, apparatus accuracy or new T/U/V rules.

## 13. Theorem 10 — Calibration transport is not new-source orientation

**Assumptions.** A connected graph of representations of the same physically
identified quantity has invertible affine edge maps
`v_j=c_ji v_i+d_ji`, with `c_ji>0`. The maps are independently justified on
a common nonconstant comparison domain; reverse edges are their actual inverses.
All relevant comparisons use the same fixed context and matching physical endpoints.
To infer a map's slope and intercept from comparison data requires at least
two distinct coordinate values, or an equivalent independent unit standard.

**Statement: root denomination.** A calibration chain transports the root
without algebraic scale/reference drift precisely when every closed path has
the identity affine map. For consecutive maps,

\[
c_{ki}=c_{kj}c_{ji},\qquad
 d_{ki}=c_{kj}d_{ji}+d_{kj}.
\tag{19}
\]

Every loop must therefore have product slope one and composed offset zero
for potential-level alignment. For endpoint differences alone, slope one is
the loop requirement; constant offsets cancel, though common potential levels
remain unaligned when a loop offset is nonzero.

**Statement: transported signs.** Suppose, in addition, a state matching map
`T_ji` carries the compared action endpoints to matching endpoints and

\[
\phi_j(T_{ji}z)=c_{ji}\phi_i(z)+d_{ji}.
\tag{20}
\]

Then `E_j=c_ji E_i`. With corresponding physical orientation labels, an
orientation theorem transports between these representations. A new physical
source whose constitutive function is not related by (20) receives no such
sign theorem merely by sharing the root denomination. Its own action/domain
orientation must be established.

**Proof.** Composition of two affine maps gives (19); induction gives a chain.
If all paths from the root agree, traversing a path and returning along
another has the identity map. Conversely, two paths differ by a closed path;
identity of every such loop makes them agree. Inverses exist because the
slopes are positive. The same argument on differences removes the offsets.
Subtracting (20) at matching endpoints gives Theorem 1. Without (20), no
relation between the two endpoint differences has been assumed, so this last
conclusion cannot follow; W1 in §14 is the physical-model counterexample.
**QED.**

**Removed-assumption examples.** Two routes that multiply one nonzero contrast
by respectively one and two produce inconsistent denomination. A loop
`v↦v+1` preserves contrasts but not common levels. A constant comparison family
cannot empirically identify a slope. Conversely, assigning arbitrary positive
numbers `a_i` and defining all slopes as `a_j/a_i` makes every slope loop close;
this algebra does **not** establish traceability to any physical unit. Physical
calibration is a premise, not a result of loop closure. Endpoint matching and
corresponding labels are necessary for transporting an orientation claim.

### 13.1 Three cross-field relations

| Object | What it relates | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration map `C_ji` | Matching representations of the same physical quantity | Physical transport, coupling dynamics or a new source's sign |
| Physical coupling/update `A` or `P` | Joint physical pre/post states, possibly with reservoir and memory change | A common unit or a decomposition of potential |
| Interaction factor `φ_AB` | A scalar contribution depending on both source states | A unique actor/source allocation or a dynamic law |

For example, two uncoupled thermometers may share calibration. A physical
transfer between unlike quantities can occur without an affine calibration
between their readings. A coupled potential can be specified without selecting
an evolution law; many laws can use the same potential. These independent
examples demonstrate why the three relations cannot be substituted for each
other. Physical coupling can also exist in a model with additive potential.

A chain can cross different sensor types or source realizations only when
each step includes the needed physical quantity-kind bridge and unit
identification. This does not require equal functional forms across fields.
Any state-dependent physical conversion belongs in the constitutive mapping;
it is not a freely changing affine scale. Changing reference/context requires
an explicit extended-state relation, including the corresponding endpoint
terms. New calibration information does not silently rewrite recorded values.

**EBU meaning and authority.** This specializes RF §§5–6's calibration-loop
and cross-field distinction to identified factors. It leaves the thermal root
viable while preserving RF's missing transportability obligation and W1's
separate source/total orientation verdicts.

## 14. W1 test case — Preserved arithmetic and verdict

This section quotes the selected mathematical model in W1; it makes no new
water design, factor admission or numerical calibration. The notation `H_0`
stands for W1's conserved complete physical energy, avoiding confusion with
`E_tot` as an EBU contrast here.

For W1's two-column water geometry, writing `A=A_t` for its column area,

\[
U_w(w)=\frac{\rho g}{2A}\bigl[w^2+(W-w)^2\bigr],
\qquad
\delta_-=U_w(w-q)-U_w(w)
=\frac{\rho g}{A}\left[2q\left(\frac W2-w\right)+q^2\right].
\tag{21}
\]

On the declared lower-column domain `w<W/2`, with positive withdrawal `q`
and both endpoints admitted, `δ_->0`. The derivative
`∂δ_-/∂w=−2ρgq/A<0` gives the already-derived state dependence. For the
scoped canonical coordinate at fixed reference temperature (its constant
reference offset suppressed),

\[
\phi_w^{\rm can}(w)=\frac{U_w(w)}{k_BT_{\rm ref}},\qquad
E_w^{\rm can}=-\frac{\delta_-}{k_BT_{\rm ref}}<0.
\tag{22}
\]

Refilling while remaining below equal head lowers `U_w` and gives the opposite,
positive contrast. Above equal head, a sufficiently small withdrawal toward
equal head lowers `U_w` and reverses (22)'s sign. The factor orientation is
therefore domain-scoped, not a statement that less water is globally worse.

The selected complete finite-bath potential is instead

\[
u=H_0-e-U_w(w)>0,\qquad
\mathcal V(w,e)=-c_b\log(u/u_*),\qquad
\mathcal V_{we}=\frac{c_b U_w'(w)}{u^2}.
\tag{23}
\]

This mixed derivative is nonzero away from equal head, including the admitted
lower-column interior. Theorem 4 therefore forbids an exact independent unary
water-plus-drive decomposition on such an interior neighborhood. It does not
forbid a coupled term, or prove that no other compatible model could exist.

W1's withdrawal map obeys `e⁺=e−δ_-−L`, with nonnegative dissipative loss `L`,
so `u⁺=u+L` and

\[
E_{\rm tot}=c_b\log(1+L/u).
\tag{24}
\]

This is positive for `L>0` and zero for `L=0`. Depletion-negative total
orientation fails. A reversible withdrawal still fails a *strictly negative*
requirement. The companion refill has the same dissipative-bath mechanism;
it does not repair the failed withdrawal class.

Equations (22) and (24) compare two distinct scoped physical quantities. A
fixed-reference canonical water coordinate and a changing finite-bath joint
potential do not become exact additive components just because both use
root-normalized dimensionless notation. Defining
`Ψ:=𝒱−φ_w^can` would produce an algebraic identity, but would not supply
independent physical identification of that remainder or certify this water
factor's embedding. The mixed derivative rules out only the independent unary
form, not every coupled embedding.

| Question | Preserved W1 answer |
|---|---|
| Is the scoped canonical water orientation possible on the restricted domain? | Yes, as a scoped physical-model coordinate |
| Is a physically identified water-factor embedding thereby certified? | No — possible in principle / not certified |
| Does the selected complete potential carry the requested orientation for withdrawal? | No |
| Do the source contrast and the complete action carry it together? | No, because the total sign already fails |
| Does positive root rescaling cure the total sign? | No, by Theorem 1 |
| Does this invalidate the thermal root? | No; scale, embedding and application orientation are separate |

Thus W1 is a counterexample to inferring total orientation from a favorable
scoped diagnostic. It is not a certified example of an admitted embedded
water factor. The exact-factor countermodels in §8 independently establish
logical incomparability of the two sign requirements. This report leaves W1's
arithmetic and narrowed refusal unchanged.

### 15.1 Force/path and extended field

If `φ_s∈C¹` is single-valued on a fixed declared domain and `Γ` is an
admissible piecewise smooth path in that domain, write `z_s=p_s z` and
`μ_s=∇_{z_s}φ_s`. The ordinary chain rule gives

\[
E_s=-\int_\Gamma\mu_s\cdot dz_s.
\tag{25}
\]

The potential is supplied, so simple connectedness is not additionally
required to integrate its own gradient. Reconstructing a potential from an
arbitrary locally curl-free field would be a different problem with possible
topological obstructions. Coupled factors use their complete support, not just
one named source coordinate. `μ_s` is a potential gradient; calling it a
mechanical force requires its separate physical relation and sign/unit bridge.

For an admitted extended function `φ_s(z_s,λ)` of class `C¹`,

\[
d\phi_s=\nabla_{z_s}\phi_s\cdot dz_s
+\nabla_\lambda\phi_s\cdot d\lambda.
\tag{26}
\]

Its full endpoint drop is minus the integral of both terms. Ignoring moving
context changes the quantity being integrated. The two component integrals
need not separately be endpoint-only functions. An extended scalar constructed
algebraically is not automatically the physical potential for a driven process.
Neither (25) nor (26) turns passive field movement into an actor entry.

### 15.2 Static marginalization, Gaussian geometry and Schur reduction

A proposed dimensionless effective potential may be defined by
`V_eff(x)=−log ∫ exp[−𝒱(x,y)] μ(dy|x)` when that integral exists under a
specified reference measure, or by a separately stated minimum over `y`.
Integration and minimization are generally different operations. The result
is an effective scalar, not automatically the original source's contribution
to the complete potential. It must meet the embedding/identification criterion
if proposed as a source factor, and RF's conditions if used for dynamic closure.

For any fixed quadratic factor with symmetric constant Hessian `H_s`, the
finite identity is exactly `E_s=−∇φ_s(z_s)ᵀΔ_s−(1/2)Δ_sᵀH_sΔ_s`.
No equilibrium assumption is needed for this polynomial subtraction. A factor's
Hessian need not itself be positive definite merely because the joint Hessian
is. Gaussian probabilistic conclusions need the stronger hypotheses next.

For a globally quadratic joint potential on its full accessible affine
support, positive definite precision and a flat reference measure give the
Gaussian identity under the canonical bridge. For a two-block quadratic
precision with invertible positive definite `H_yy`, the effective quadratic
precision is

\[
H_{\rm eff}=H_{xx}-H_{xy}H_{yy}^{-1}H_{yx}.
\tag{27}
\]

At fixed parameters and flat measure, Gaussian integration and minimizing the
quadratic over `y` have the same `x`-dependent quadratic, up to an additive
constant. Linear terms must also be reduced consistently. A determinant term
or other “constant” that depends on changing context cannot be discarded in
cross-context contrasts. Truncation, nonflat measures, inaccessible directions
or nonlinear terms require their own treatment. The full Schur proof is not
repeated here. An effective Schur potential is not automatically an independently
settled source factor or a sufficient dynamic state.

### 15.4 Möbius hierarchy and allocation

For a fixed finite actor/control set `N`, suppose **every** coalition endpoint
`Θ_S`, `S⊆N`, exists under the same declared protocol, field and baseline.
Define relative action tables

\[
E_{\rm tot}(S)=\mathcal V(\Theta_\varnothing)-\mathcal V(\Theta_S),
\qquad
E_\alpha(S)=\phi_\alpha(\Theta_\varnothing)-\phi_\alpha(\Theta_S).
\tag{28}
\]

An exact finite factorization at all these endpoints gives
`E_tot(S)=Σ_α E_α(S)`. The Möbius transform is a finite linear sum, so

\[
m_{E_{\rm tot}}(T)=\sum_\alpha m_{E_\alpha}(T),\qquad
m_{E_\alpha}(T)=-\Delta_T(\phi_\alpha\circ\Theta_\bullet)(\varnothing)
\quad(T\ne\varnothing).
\tag{29}
\]

The corresponding nonempty identity holds for `𝒱`. At `T=∅` all the
relative tables have coefficient zero; the negative-difference formula in
(29) must not be applied there. Missing coalition corners cannot be assigned
zero. A common no-action endpoint can differ from the initial state under
passive dynamics; this relative table does not erase the separate raw baseline
drop discussed in MG.

This is a direct application of MG's cleared theorem and linearity, not a new
allocation rule. A physical factor may involve few state coordinates while
its coalition endpoint depends on many controls. Thus its Möbius order need
not equal the size of its physical support. Nonlinear endpoint responses can
produce higher-order terms even for a quadratic potential. Further vanishing
claims require the appropriate composite factor/control independence or
polynomial hypotheses from MG. Joint physical value, identified physical
factors, coalition interaction coefficients and actor/source allocations are
four distinct layers. None of the first three selects ownership of the fourth.
