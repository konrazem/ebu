## 3. One prospectively bounded candidate and action family

### 3.1 Physical identity

W1 consists of two finite, rigid, vertical water stores A and B, one finite
raised-weight drive/store, one specified hydraulic transfer interface, and a
represented finite heat reservoir. A is the designated local stock being
withdrawn from or restored. B is the **physical destination/source**, not a
disappearing sink. Both stores and the machine are inside the action boundary.

The ideal witness has identical constant cross-sectional area `A_t`, equal
bottom elevation, pure incompressible water of density `ρ`, and uniform gravity
`g`. The total movable water volume is `W=w_A+w_B`. Common headspace pressure
acts on both surfaces; its net pressure-volume work on a transfer vanishes
because total volume is fixed. The common headspace has fixed total volume and
is included in the thermal bookkeeping. This avoids assuming an unrecorded
external pressure-work source.

The drive has fixed mass `M_D` and energy `e=M_D g z_D` in its declared height
range. Its coordinate-to-energy Jacobian is constant. No chemical battery or
unspecified composition-dependent source potential is substituted for it.

The settled endpoints have zero fluid/rotor kinetic energy and a specified
interface reset state. Transferred water is not left uncounted in a pipe or
carrier. The ideal endpoint model uses zero changing interface inventory; an
actual installation with fixed hold-up must include that inventory once, and
one with changing hold-up must retain it as state. No compressible accumulator,
trapped-gas energy, soil, groundwater, orchard or ecological response is added.

These are constitutive restrictions of **one candidate**, not empirical claims
that all tanks or real pumps satisfy them. Perfectly specified ideal relations
are granted for the mathematical sign test. A real source would separately need
their calibration, uncertainty and memory checks even if this sign test passed.

### 3.2 Prospective action domain

At settlement endpoints, restrict

\[
0<w_{\min}\le w_A\le w_{\max}<W/2,\quad w_B=W-w_A,
\quad e\ge0,\quad u\ge u_{\min}>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Upper storage/energy bounds are finite. Every action must keep all endpoints
inside the domain, respect tank capacity and drive limits, and begin and end in
the specified machine mode. The full store is “relatively full” **within this
declared lower-than-B range**, not full to its brim.

There are only two nonempty action types:

- `a_−(q)`: metered transfer A→B, `w_A'=w_A−q`, called local withdrawal;
- `a_+(q)`: metered transfer B→A, `w_A'=w_A+q≤w_max`, called local restoration.

Both have `0<q≤q_max` and are stopped before crossing hydraulic equilibrium.
Return/refilling is the second action; it is not a third invented field.
For A→B the lower store is depleted further and water must be lifted to a
higher head. For B→A the head difference can drive a regenerative machine.
The word “restoration” means increasing A toward the physically determined
equal-level state. It does not imply food security, ecosystem recovery, utility
or a preferred market stock.

The no-action case has identity action map. Open-boundary import/export is
assessed separately in §15; it is not quietly added to this closed action family.

This deliberately favorable domain tests whether physical hydraulic imbalance

## 5. Direct physical potential contributions

### 5.1 Gravitational storage and hydraulic head

At bottom elevation `z_i`, integration of the mass-height distribution gives

\[
U_i(w_i)=\rho g\left(z_iw_i+\frac{w_i^2}{2A_i}\right),
\qquad
\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial w_i}=\rho g\left(z_i+\frac{w_i}{A_i}\right).
\tag{4}
\]

The derivative is pressure-equivalent energy per volume. For a quasistatic
transfer, integrating the difference between destination and source heads
gives the **same** gravitational energy change; it is not an additional stored
energy to add to (4). Pressure work in a flow balance and gravitational energy
of a stored fluid must not be counted twice. The standard control-volume,
head, pump-work and loss distinctions are described in the
[DOE Fluid Flow handbook, module 3](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/DOE-HDBK-1012-92_VOL3.pdf).
The particular finite formulas here are derived directly from (4).

For equal bottoms and equal areas, write `w=w_A`, `w_B=W−w`. Then

\[
U_w(w)=\frac{\rho g}{2A_t}[w^2+(W-w)^2]
=\frac{\rho g}{A_t}(w-W/2)^2+\frac{\rho gW^2}{4A_t}.
\tag{5}
\]

The reference `W/2` follows from equality of physical heads and conservation.
It is not an imposed optimum water stock. The quadratic coefficient is fixed
by geometry, density and gravity; it is not a scarcity coefficient. Subtracting
the constant last term is an additive gauge at fixed `W`. No extraction-work
optimization is involved.

There is also a boundary-dependent gauge check. Shifting the common vertical
datum by `b` adds `ρg b W` to the two-store energy, a constant on the closed
domain. Shifting the corresponding total-energy reference consistently leaves
`u` and the joint EBU unchanged. For A considered alone, the extra term is
`ρg b w_A`, which changes when mass crosses its boundary. Its partial energy
contrast is therefore not a datum-independent complete transfer value without
the destination/boundary terms. An arbitrary elevation zero cannot be used to
repair the action sign.

## 7. Physical coupling and one complete action update

\[

(\eta_p^{-1}-1)\delta,&\delta>0,\\

\tag{13}
\]

The available actions also require `e'` and the receiver/drive capacities to

\Delta(w_A+w_B)=0,\qquad

## 8. Sign and local-depletion analysis

### 8.1 The water-only contribution really has physical state dependence

For withdrawal from A, (5) gives

\[
\delta_-(w,q)=\frac{\rho g}{A_t}
\left[2q(W/2-w)+q^2\right]>0
\quad\text{on the declared domain}.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the fixed-temperature water contribution `E_w=−δ_−/(k_BT)` is negative.
For refilling toward equal levels,

\[
\delta_+(w,q)=\frac{\rho g}{A_t}
\left[-2q(W/2-w)+q^2\right]<0,
\tag{16}
\]

when the stipulated endpoint does not cross `W/2`. Its water contribution is
positive. Moreover,

\[
\frac{\partial\delta_-}{\partial w}=-\frac{2\rho gq}{A_t}<0.
\tag{17}
\]

The same withdrawal therefore increases hydraulic energy more from a depleted
A, giving a larger magnitude of the negative water-factor contrast. This is a
positive scientific result derived from **gravity, geometry and finite storage**,
without an arbitrary scarcity coefficient. Withdrawal from the lower local
store raises hydraulic imbalance and has `E_w<0`; restoration toward equal
levels lowers imbalance and has `E_w>0`. Outside the selected domain,
withdrawing from an over-high A can instead reduce imbalance; “less water”
alone is not a universal sign law.

For comparison, treating A alone with `U_A=ρgw_A²/(2A_t)` and discarding its
destination gives a positive thermal potential drop on withdrawal and a
negative one on refilling in that stated datum. The boundary/gauge qualification

\[
