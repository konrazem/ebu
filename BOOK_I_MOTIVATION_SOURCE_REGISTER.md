# Book I motivation — source and claim register

Research/access date: **2026-09-18**.

Status: **editorial research and planning register; not a generated book, completed
full-report verification, EBU theorem, or scientific-study result**.

This register supports the motivation and introductory concepts in
[Book I introduction blueprint](BOOK_I_INTRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md). EBU-specific
mathematical and programme status must be taken from
[current scientific authority](CURRENT_SCIENTIFIC_AUTHORITY.md), not inferred from
the external evidence collected here. These documents are distinct: the blueprint
sets the exposition, the authority coordinate states programme status, and this
register identifies external claims and their limitations.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The planned introduction may explain why a research programme connecting physical
conditions to economic action is worth investigating. It must not present the
existence of inequality, ecological damage, or incomplete accounting as proof that
EBU is correct, unique, implementable, or beneficial.

The work behind this register searched primary institutional sources, official
technical documentation, and original research. It inspected selected official
webpages, report summaries, source records, and indexed passages. It did **not**
read every underlying report in full, reproduce the statistics, verify every
methodological appendix, or archive immutable copies of the remote publications.
Some full-text access was unavailable while official summaries or indexed
passages remained accessible. A claim row is therefore a research lead with a
specified scope, not unconditional approval of a publication-ready statistic.

Publication dates and data periods are different. Where a maintained webpage has
no clear publication date, this register says so rather than using a search-engine
crawl date as its publication date. Future scenario years do not describe
observations. All source discovery in this register is bounded by the access date
above; the register is not an automatically updating dashboard.

No EBU code, experiment, controller decision rule, numerical parameter, or book
manuscript was created by this source-research task.

## 2. Defensible monetary-system framing

Recommended editorial formulation, to be adapted rather than treated as a
scientific theorem:

> Monetary institutions coordinate payments, credit and investment, but monetary
> balances and market prices are not comprehensive accounts of the physical
> conditions that sustain life. Regulations, taxes, public services and
> environmental accounts already provide important connections. Those connections
> are incomplete, uneven and sometimes poorly aligned with ecological limits and
> human needs. EBU investigates a proposed way to make specified physical states
> and consequences explicit in local action evaluation.

The final sentence describes the research motivation. The preceding claims need
the scoped evidence below. None means that all existing institutions fail, that
money has no real-world connection, or that there is one homogeneous worldwide
monetary/economic system.

Do not write:

- “Money has no connection to life or the environment.” Monetary activity funds
  life-supporting services and physical production, and explicit environmental
  policy instruments exist.
- “The economy has no controller.” Central banks, fiscal authorities, regulators,
  public providers, firms and households already respond to measured conditions.
- “All scarcity is manufactured by money.” Physical, spatial, temporal,
  infrastructural, institutional and affordability constraints can coexist.
- “The monetary system causes all ecological damage and socioeconomic inequality.”
  A causal claim needs identified mechanisms, a comparison, and evidence capable
  of distinguishing competing explanations.
- “Economic equilibrium is necessarily healthy.” Market-clearing, financial
  stability, ecological viability, social justice and physiological homeostasis
  are different concepts.

The useful research question is not whether feedback exists, but **what is
observed, what objective is pursued, whose consequences enter the decision, and
what happens when existing feedback is incomplete or conflicting**.

## 4. Open source-quality issues and publication locks

### 4.1 FAO year-to-year hunger decrease — not approved for publication

The [FAO Director-General's SOFI 2026 launch statement](https://www.fao.org/director-general/speeches/details/launch-of-the-state-of-food-security-and-nutrition-in-the-world-%28sofi%29-2026-report-statement/)
reports approximately 645 million people facing hunger in 2025 and a reduction of
nearly **14 million** compared with 2024. The
[FAO Chief Economist's interview](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/progress-on-hunger-continues--but-africa-and-new-crises-raise-concerns--fao-chief-economist/en),
also dated 2026-07-21, reports the same approximately 645-million level but a
reduction of nearly **40 million** compared with 2024.

Do not choose whichever decrease supports the narrative. Do not compute an
alternative by subtracting estimates from different annual report editions.

Before publication, retrieve and lock the SOFI 2026 underlying table and its
revised historical series, uncertainty ranges, definitions and correction notices.
Until then:

- the year-to-year decrease has status **UNRESOLVED — NOT APPROVED FOR
  PUBLICATION**;
- the common headline level is a sourced research candidate, not a fully
  table-verified final book statistic;
- the qualitative distinction between food availability and affordability does
  not depend on resolving the disputed decrease.

### 4.2 Report versions are not interchangeable

SOFI 2026 was released before this research date. SOFI 2025 is not the latest
edition. The older 2.60-billion/2024 affordability estimate may be cited only with
its own report vintage and method; it must not silently become a 2026 current
estimate. The $2.15/2017-PPP World Bank poverty series must not be spliced into the
$3.00/2021-PPP series.

The IPBES assessment landing page describes its February 2026 plenary as 3–8
February; the IPBES Secretariat's IPCC note gives 3–9 February. This register uses
the month and year rather than inventing exact adoption-day certainty.

WMO's annual observation, IPCC's multi-year assessment, and UNEP's conditional
policy scenario answer different questions. Do not plot them as if they were one
homogeneous measured time series.

### 4.3 Minimum publication record

For each numerical statement or figure selected for Book I, record:

1. Exact claim and its type: observation, estimate, conditional projection,
   institutional definition, inference or value choice.
2. Author/institution, title, edition, publication date and data period.
3. Exact table, figure, page or stable section; URL and access date.
4. Units, population/denominator, baseline, geography and measurement definition.
5. Uncertainty range and confidence convention where applicable.
6. Scenario, policy cutoff and conditional assumptions for projections.
7. Revision/correction status and resolution of contradictory source summaries.
8. Evidence actually inspected; distinguish full-report verification from a
   summary or abstract check.

Do not turn this register into a mandatory full-report audit of unused sources.
Lock the specific claims selected for the book. A source-quality issue stops the
affected claim, not independent exposition or mathematical work.

## 5. Socioeconomic mechanisms, not a single-cause story

The introduction should separate mechanisms that often occur together:

- **Material deprivation:** inadequate access to food, housing, energy, water,
  sanitation or care.
- **Distribution and entitlement:** who can use available resources, under which
  rights, prices and public-provision arrangements.
- **Exposure and vulnerability:** different living and working environments,
  hazards, reserves and opportunities to avoid or recover from harm.
- **Opportunity over time:** education, early-life conditions, neighbourhoods,
  employment and access to institutions.
- **Market and institutional mechanisms:** incentives, public goods,
  externalities, infrastructure, discrimination, governance and implementation.

MOT-06 and MOT-07 support a physical/access distinction. MOT-08 supports the
social-determinants discussion. MOT-09 supplies one bounded causal example.
These sources do not justify claiming that an inequality index by itself causes
every health effect, that poverty is entirely monetary, or that changing a
valuation formula is sufficient to remove institutional exclusion.

Use “associated with” for associations and “caused under the study's design and
scope” for causal evidence. Explain plausible mechanisms as mechanisms rather
than upgrading them to measured effects. Present the goal of reducing avoidable
deprivation as an openly stated value commitment, not something deduced from a
Gaussian function.

## 6. Homeostasis, equilibrium and Gaussian safeguards

The mathematical examples below are **expository distinctions**, not new adopted
EBU definitions. The active programme's actual formulas and status must be checked
against [current scientific authority](CURRENT_SCIENTIFIC_AUTHORITY.md).

1. **Functional balance is not thermodynamic equilibrium.** Living systems can
   maintain useful conditions through continuing input, transformation and
   dissipation. A closed lossless model is an abstraction, not a complete organism.
2. **Steady state is not stability.** Unchanging selected variables do not prove
   return after disturbance. Stability itself is not desirability: a harmful
   condition can persist.
3. **Homeostasis is not equality between all nodes.** Components can have different
   roles, capacities, reference states and compatible operating conditions.
4. **Homeostasis does not imply one universal optimal point.** A model can declare
   a target or reference geometry; its empirical and normative basis must be
   stated. Physiological examples do not settle societal choices.
5. **A reference centre is not automatically an observed mean.** A chosen target
   must not be presented as a statistical estimate or universal natural optimum.
6. **A reference scale is not automatically a standard deviation, tolerance,
   uncertainty or measurement error.** State its declared interpretation and
   units. If a probabilistic interpretation is claimed, state its extra basis.
7. **Gaussian shape is not automatically probability.** An unnormalized
   similarity score becomes a density only with normalization, a measure/domain
   and appropriate semantics. Physical capacities and nonnegative stocks remain
   separate constraints; Gaussian support does not make impossible states legal.
8. **Similarity, distance and burden are different functions.** For the
   illustrative definition `g = exp(-d^2 / 2)`, `-log(g) = d^2 / 2`, while
   `1 - g` is bounded and has different gradients. Do not slide between these
   formulas as though they define the same field or controller. If computation
   underflows, that is a numerical issue, not an alteration of the exact identity.
9. **Gauss's normal distribution is not Gauss's divergence theorem.** Neither
   supplies a universal law of economic recovery or an entitlement to issuance.
10. **A potential does not supply its own dynamics.** Actions, observations,
    feasibility, permission, delays and disturbances require separate
    specifications. A gradient need not be a realized physical trajectory.
11. **A chosen scalar does not erase trade-offs.** Which states and effects are
    included, whose interests they represent, and what a scalar comparison omits
    must remain visible.
12. **Mathematical proof and physical validation are distinct.** Exact identities
    and conditional theorems can be established before experiments. Their use as
    empirical descriptions or beneficial economic controllers needs separate
    evidence; neither a book nor a passing software fixture provides that evidence.

The first book may defer obsolete threshold constructions, lower/upper-band
formulas or specialized ecological mechanisms to appropriate later/history
material. It must not remove the assumptions required by a theorem or imply that
real measurement/model uncertainty disappears when those older formulas are
omitted from the introductory narrative.

## 7. Suggested reader progression

This is a content route supporting the blueprint, not an independently governing
table of contents or a generated manuscript:

1. What an economy must keep possible: life-supporting services and the author's
   openly declared values.
2. What money records: payments, credit, claims, prices and their constraints.
3. What prices may miss, and what existing institutions already do about it.
4. Scarcity versus access, using water and food examples without denying physical
   limitations.
5. Unequal conditions and unequal consequences, with causal claims scoped.
6. The measured planet and conditional futures: observations before scenarios.
7. Homeostasis and functional balance: maintained functioning rather than stasis.
8. From a functional question to state variables, physical boundaries and units.
9. Declared Gaussian reference geometry: centre, scale and normalized deviation.
10. From the adopted reference geometry to the actual potential and burden.
11. Finite physical actions and the EBU equation, separating admissibility from
    desirability and action selection.
12. What is proved, proposed and unknown: an explicit status map that does not
    imply the scientific programme has already succeeded.

Theoretical exposition need not wait for experiments. Claims of real-world
adequacy, superiority, long-run homeostasis or social benefit cannot be supplied
by the motivation chapter in place of evidence.
