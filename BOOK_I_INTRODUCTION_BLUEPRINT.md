# Book I blueprint: why EBU, and what its equation means

**Status:** author-requested editorial architecture; no manuscript or PDF has
been generated.
**Working title:** *Why EBU? Life, Balance, and the Accounting of Physical
Action*.
**Primary reader:** an interested non-specialist who can follow arithmetic but
should not need prior economics, calculus, graph theory or programming.
**Current boundary:** plan the new introductory book now; stop before authoring
or rendering it. No experimental or implementation stage is authorized here.

## 1. The book's promise

The book should help a reader answer five questions:

1. Why might monetary accounts and present institutions be insufficient, by
   themselves, to preserve specified life-supporting functions?
2. What do homeostasis, functional balance, equilibrium and physical conservation
   mean, and why are they not synonyms?
3. How can a declared local reference become a transparent mathematical ruler
   for changes in physical state?
4. What does an exact EBU value measure for a finite action?
5. Which conclusions are mathematical, which depend on a chosen model, and
   which remain scientific or institutional questions?

The answer is not that EBU has already solved inequality or planetary decline.
The motivation explains why the question deserves investigation. The equations
explain the candidate accounting construction. Later evidence must establish
what its use does in a declared world.

The first book can be written before new simulations. Neither definitions nor
proved identities require a successful Stage A/B run. Conversely, a valid
equation cannot substitute for evidence about actors, institutions or recovery.

## 2. Narrative progression and chapter specification

Each chapter has one primary question, an example, an explicit bridge to the
next chapter and a claim boundary. Proposed chapter numbers below are local
to the new Book I. They do not rename historical Chapters 1-62 in the existing
PDFs or force immediate cross-reference changes in Parts II and III.

### Movement A: the reason for the project

### 1. The question beneath an economy

**Question:** What are economic systems ultimately meant to make possible?

Begin with a life-facing event: keeping a clinic supplied with water, heat and
medicine. Show the engineer's physical record and the institution's payment
record for the same event. Explain why wages, prices, contracts and accounts
have real coordination functions while none is a complete physical history.

Develop the difference between an economic claim, a physical resource,
service and human need before judging any mechanism. Explain that meeting
needs involves production, care, maintenance and ecosystems, not just exchange.

**Example:** the monetary bill for a water pump versus water delivered, energy
input, wear, leakage and emergency capability. This is an explanatory example,
not a calibrated EBU valuation.

**Bridge:** if the monetary record and physical record answer different
questions, what can prices reveal and what can they omit?

**Boundary:** do not assert that money is unreal, useless or literally unrelated
to life. Distinguish useful monetary institutions from the absence of an
automatic guarantee that their incentives preserve every life-supporting system.

### 2. What monetary signals can and cannot tell us

**Question:** Why can financially attractive action have harmful unpriced
physical or social consequences?

Explain price formation, purchasing power, externalities, public goods, missing
markets, information limits and power in ordinary language. Show how property
rights, regulation, public provision and environmental policy can change these
relationships. Compare market value, social value and modeled physical effect
without treating any pair as identical.

**Example:** maintenance or pollution prevention that benefits people who are
not party to a transaction. Include a counterexample in which regulation or
pricing does internalize a relevant effect, so the argument is not absolute.

**Bridge:** an omitted cost is one problem; inability to access essentials is
another. The next chapter separates scarcity from distribution.

**Boundary:** claims about actual economic mechanisms require the motivation
source register. A theoretical externality example is not a prevalence estimate.

### 3. Scarcity, access and unequal exposure

**Question:** Why can essential needs remain unmet even where some relevant
resources exist?

Separate finite physical availability, bottlenecks, institutions, affordability,
unequal ownership, spatial access, capabilities and vulnerability. Explain
socioeconomic status (SES) on first use. Treat income, wealth, education,
occupation, health and environmental exposure as related but non-identical.

Discuss reciprocal and confounded relationships rather than claiming one
monetary cause for all inequality. Explain why a physically efficient account
would still need fair access, disability support and governance.

**Example:** two households facing the same heatwave with different housing,
health, income and infrastructure. No moral score is assigned to a person.

**Bridge:** unequal exposure takes place inside a changing physical environment.
What do observations and projections actually say about that environment?

**Boundary:** use dated evidence, geography, denominators and uncertainty. Do
not infer individual circumstances from a group-level association.

### 4. The condition of the planet and conditional futures

**Question:** Which environmental changes motivate this research, and what
happens under different future pathways?

Present a small number of well-supported climate, biodiversity, resource and
pollution observations. Distinguish observation, attribution, projection and
scenario. State baseline years, regions and uncertainty. Show that pathways
depend on policy, technology, behavior, inequality and physical feedbacks.

Do not blend unlike indicators into a fabricated planetary damage score. A
figure may display separate series without implying their units are commensurate.

**Example:** one observed pressure, its unequal effects, and contrasting
conditional pathways from an authoritative assessment.

**Bridge:** diagnosis motivates a design question, but does not establish that
the proposed design works.

**Boundary:** no inevitable-collapse claim, no invented date at which Earth
becomes uninhabitable, and no claim that one synthetic EBU result predicts a
planetary outcome. Refresh dated claims at manuscript freeze.

### 5. From diagnosis to a research question

**Question:** What would an additional physical-action account need to provide?

State the design desiderata: explicit represented quantities and boundaries;
local information; finite-action accounting; clear assumptions; auditable
records; falsifiable claims; and separation of measurement from institutional
choice. Acknowledge existing national/environmental accounts, engineering
control, environmental regulation and monetary/fiscal institutions.

Replace the literal statement "the economy has no controller" with the precise
problem: existing feedback institutions do not constitute a universal automatic
guarantee of all specified social and ecological outcomes. EBU is a proposed
additional architecture, not proof that every prior mechanism is absent.

**Example:** questions a community should be able to ask before approving a
resource transformation, with responsibility and access kept separate.

**Bridge:** to say that a system should preserve function, we must first explain
function and balance.

**Boundary:** design goals and ethical commitments are declared, not derived
from Gaussian mathematics.

### Movement B: balance before equations

### 6. Homeostasis: maintaining function through change

**Question:** How can a system remain functional while its components keep
changing?

Introduce sensing, disturbance, response, feedback, reserves and operating
conditions through bounded biological and engineering examples. Emphasize
that homeostasis is a regulatory process, not complete immobility or unlimited
resilience. Different functions can conflict; one measurement cannot certify all.

**Example:** regulated temperature, fluid balance or a managed tank, with the
specific controller named. The example must not imply that the EBU field
automatically contains such a controller.

**Bridge:** homeostasis is related to equilibrium and stability, but those
words answer different questions.

**Boundary:** biological regulation illustrates a concept; it is not evidence
that economies obey the same equations.

### 7. Functional balance, equilibrium and living nonequilibrium

**Question:** Does balance mean that nothing moves?

Explain a reference condition, a fixed point, a steady state with throughput,
forward invariance, boundedness, stability and attraction. Living systems
usually operate away from thermodynamic equilibrium and require exchanges;
do not identify healthy life with thermodynamic equilibrium.

A declared target/reference `x_star` can be useful even before a dynamic law is
specified. It is an equilibrium of a particular law only if that law leaves it
unchanged (with the relevant inputs and other state variables specified).

**Example:** equal inflow and outflow maintain a tank's level while material
continues moving; a ball at rest versus one repeatedly crossing a reference
illustrates why being bounded is not convergence.

**Bridge:** we need a precise account of what exists and what changes before
we can measure deviation from a reference.

**Boundary:** reserve safety, conservation, homeostasis and recovery are not
interchangeable success labels.

### 8. A physical world before an economic account

**Question:** What must a model record so that an action has a physical meaning?

Introduce state, stock, rate, finite quantity, units, boundary, inflow, outflow
and loss. Use the simplest closed lossless scalar transfer as the main example.
Contrast it with an open boundary without adding lossy runtime claims.

**Core relations:** for finite `q`, `x_i' = x_i - q`, `x_j' = x_j + q` and
`sum x' = sum x`. If a rate is used, finite quantity is `q = dt * J`; do not
multiply a quantity by `dt` again.

**Example:** hand-accounted water transfer with named source and destination.
Include a conservation-but-poor-distribution example.

**Bridge:** the physical action exists before someone prices, approves or values it.

**Boundary:** physical conservation is not conservation of EBU, money or utility.
No equation certifies an undeclared boundary.

### 9. Actions, actors and local information

**Question:** Who acts, what is evaluated, and what information is permitted?

Describe an atomic transformation `a = (i, j, q)` and a chain of transformations
without hiding it in a mega-action. Explain physical feasibility, field
valuation, optional capacity affordability, actor choice, execution and audit
as separate layers. Introduce simultaneous groups conceptually without forcing
a one-action-only world.

**Example:** fuel conversion, electricity delivery and pumping are linked
physical transformations when represented. Do not charge extra burden merely
because the chain has several links.

**Bridge:** valuation needs a ruler; the next chapter explains who declares it
and what it means.

**Boundary:** EBU does not choose a maximum, rank human worth or supply physical
permission. In the prospective test an affordability filter constrains choices,
but the actor's random policy remains a separate model component.

### Movement C: from a declared ruler to a local field

### 10. Why a reference is necessary

**Question:** Relative to what does a measured change count as improvement?

Introduce `x_i_star`, `sigma_i > 0`, units, scale, calibration and reference
version. Explain that a reference must be physically and scientifically
justified for its domain. It is not automatically an observed mean, universal
optimum, fair allocation or natural constant.

For the closed nonnegative scalar example with fixed mass `M`, a physically
compatible reference must satisfy `x_i_star >= 0` and `sum(x_i_star) = M`,
as well as any declared physical capacities. Otherwise zero potential may be
unavailable within the physical domain. Even a compatible reference need not
be reachable through the declared topology and action menu.

**Example:** compare a displacement of two units under two declared scales;
make clear that changing the scale changes the ruler and requires justification.

**Bridge:** a quadratic local reference supplies one transparent example of such
a ruler, not the only possible family.

**Boundary:** do not choose scales from evaluation outcomes to make a policy
look favorable; do not infer cross-domain ethical weights from normalization.

### 11. The local Gaussian ruler

**Question:** How can distance from a reference be measured transparently?

Teach in this order: displacement; division by a same-unit positive scale;
squaring; the one-half convention; then the compact equation:

\[
V_i(x_i)=\frac12\left(\frac{x_i-x_i^\star}{\sigma_i}\right)^2.
\]

Explain the Gaussian relative-log-density identity for a fixed normal reference
with mean `x_i_star` and variance `sigma_i^2`. The normalization cancels in the
ratio. This is why the example is called Gaussian-derived. It is not Gauss's
law, a gravitational/electromagnetic field or evidence that the trajectory is
normally distributed.

**Example:** symmetric deviations at equal scale and unequal scales. The
illustration is labeled mathematically derived, not a simulation result.

**Bridge:** local rulers can form a declared potential while still allowing
local evaluation of affected parts.

**Boundary:** nonnegative physical stocks and the conservation constraint remain
the action/state domain. The unconstrained Gaussian reference is an explanatory
geometry, not a claim that independent unbounded Gaussian stocks describe the
physical world. No covariance cross-term does not imply no state correlation.

### 12. From local references to a potential

**Question:** How can local measurements describe a joint state without forcing
every actor to inspect the whole world?

Define `V(x) = sum_i V_i(x_i)` as the audit potential. Explain the affected
support: unchanged terms cancel in a before/after difference. Introduce the
future local-factor form only as a short bridge, with factor support explicit.

**Example:** a transfer touches two cells; an unchanged third term cancels.
Show equal total resource but different potential.

**Bridge:** a potential tells us about states; its marginal describes a nearby
directional change.

**Boundary:** the sum is not a dense fitted global Gaussian or runtime global
ranking service. Physical mass/resource conservation and potential change
remain different objects. Threshold/hinge and other domain potentials remain
valid alternatives; do not teach their full L/U machinery here.

### 13. Marginal field: what a small change means

**Question:** What does the local slope say about a small transfer?

Introduce a derivative as a local rate of change, then

\[
\mu_i=\frac{x_i-x_i^\star}{\sigma_i^2},\qquad
S_e=-e_i+e_j,\qquad f_e=-\mu^T S_e=\mu_i-\mu_j.
\]

Define the transpose/dot product through a two-cell calculation. For a
dimensionless `V`, `mu` and `f` have inverse-stock units. The sign reports a
local directional effect, not an order to move.

**Example:** positive, negative and zero initial force. Do not turn the example
into `J = M[f-theta]_+` as an intrinsic EBU law.

**Bridge:** real actions move a finite quantity, so their final effect can differ
from the initial slope's linear prediction.

**Boundary:** a constitutive law or actor-selection algorithm is an additional
model, not supplied by taking the derivative.

### Movement D: finite action and exact accounting

### 14. A real action is finite

**Question:** Why can a seemingly favorable direction become harmful if taken
too far?

Build a complete finite endpoint from a physically feasible increment before
evaluating it. For the quadratic model define the diagonal curvature matrix
`H = diag(1/sigma_i^2)` and derive

\[
E(\delta x)=-\mu(x)^T\delta x-\frac12\delta x^T H\delta x.
\]

For an edge quantity `q`, derive

\[
E_e(q)=qf_e-\frac{q^2}{2}
\left(\frac1{\sigma_i^2}+\frac1{\sigma_j^2}\right).
\]

**Example:** a hand-calculated restorative transfer and an overshoot. At the
reference, a nonzero state increment has strictly negative field value because
`H` is positive definite. A nonempty group whose net increment is zero is not
a counterexample to that statement.

**Bridge:** the exact endpoint relation, not the initial slope alone, is the
central finite EBU account.

**Boundary:** fixture arithmetic is illustration; it is not an executed
scientific trajectory, proof of recovery, or a chosen study parameter set.

### 15. The exact EBU equation

**Question:** What precisely is being calculated for an accepted physical action?

State the general form in words before notation: the declared potential before
the action, less the potential after it, less any separately declared residual
physical process burden not already counted.

\[
\Delta E_a=V_A(z)-V_A(T_a(z))-C_a.
\]

`z` is the declared frozen action baseline; when a study includes preceding
external drive, name that event order rather than silently crediting drive to
an actor. `A` is complete affected potential support. For the ideal lossless
Gaussian example, `C_a = 0`; this is a scope choice, not proof real processes
have no cost.

Explain positive, zero and negative signs; local-to-global cancellation;
conservation versus burden; and the difference between an audit ledger and a
valued potential factor. Avoid reopening the unresolved lossy cost classification
as if the introductory book had adopted it.

**Example:** finish one two-cell account including units, admissibility and
the exact finite value.

**Bridge:** what changes when more than one action touches the same state?

**Boundary:** EBU is exact relative to the declared model and data, not an
omniscient measurement of all physical, moral or social consequences.

### 16. One action, several actions and the path between states

**Question:** Why does a simultaneous group need one coherent before/after
account?

Use `delta_x_G = sum_a delta_x_a` only when the action map really is fixed and
additive. Define `E_G = V(x) - V(x + delta_x_G)`. Show that shared-coordinate
curvature can make this differ from the sum of same-baseline singleton values.

Explain the fundamental-theorem identity

\[
E_G=-\int_0^1\nabla V(x+\lambda\delta x_G)^T\delta x_G\,d\lambda.
\]

If per-action attribution is introduced, label the common-path convention and
show its closure separately from settlement/ownership. The line may be a
model-realised constant-rate path only if the declared dynamics specify that;
otherwise it is an endpoint interpolation used for accounting.

**Example:** two transfers sharing a destination; contrast disjoint support.
No full Boolean subset enumeration is needed for this introductory lesson.

**Bridge:** several useful exact identities follow, but none chooses an actor's
policy or proves the long-run outcome.

**Boundary:** naming a path decomposition does not remove its relation to known
Aumann-Shapley mathematics, nor prove fairness, causal entitlement, incentive
compatibility or permission to spend an owner's balance. Detailed theory is II/VI.

### 17. What follows mathematically, and what does not

**Question:** Which claims are consequences of definitions and which require
additional evidence?

Present a small theorem ledger with explicit assumptions:

- affected-support cancellation;
- exact quadratic finite expansion;
- endpoint/path identity;
- sequential telescoping;
- complete-state closed-cycle field sum zero under a fixed potential, and net
  sum `-sum C_a <= 0` when nonnegative burdens are included;
- common-path closure under declared fixed additive increments, without
  claiming institutional or causal meaning.

Keep changing potential versions, intervening drive, omitted coordinates and
nonadditive action maps visible as ways an unstated application can fail.

A short prospective bridge may define earned/spent nonnegative capacity and
external injection, but leave full ownership, affordability, stochastic dynamics
and proof obligations to V/VI. The identity `V + sum B - J = constant` depends
on matching event and receipt definitions and is not a recovery theorem.

**Example:** a closed two-action loop; explain why externally driven histories
require their own ledger.

**Bridge:** the equation gives a clear research programme, not a completed economy.

**Boundary:** on a closed finite nonnegative resource simplex with fixed positive
scales, `V` is already bounded. Boundedness alone is not evidence that affordability
improves regulation. Stationarity, concentration near a reference and recovery
are stronger, distinct questions.

### 18. The equation as a beginning, not a promise

**Question:** What can the reader now say precisely, and what remains open?

Return to the clinic example with the conceptual layers now separated. Explain
the next books' proof, implementation, evidence, dynamics, interaction, routes,
coordination and institutional responsibilities. End with the finite EBU
equation and its assumptions, not an asserted simulation victory.

Present the new prospective experiment in one short, status-labeled passage:
independent physical forcing; physically feasible EBU-blind candidates; exact
valuation; declared capacity affordability; random actor choice; exact execution;
settlement and audit. No predicted success is written as an observation.

**Closing boundary:** EBU does not by itself establish an optimal reference,
universal welfare scale, automatic recovery, a fair capacity institution or a
complete monetary transition. Those are real research tasks, not decorative
disclaimers.

## 4. Material deliberately outside the main introductory spine

- Allee thresholds, explicit L/U/R hinge construction and P1C eligibility:
  preserved historical/domain material in II, III and V.
- Full continuous flow laws, mobility, activation thresholds, Onsager analogy,
  discrete timestep certificates and numerical tolerances: II/III/V as scoped
  technical material, not prerequisites to learning the EBU definition.
- Detailed common-path allocation, ownership, same-event netting, Mobius,
  recursive motifs and causal identification: II/VI and later policy layers.
- Gaussian Stage A/B parameter tables, seeds, trajectories, stationary-law
  claims and return-rate estimates: future V evidence, not present I.
- Institutional capacity pools, transfers, entitlements, borrowing, wealth and
  governance: IX; no simulated account rule is silently generalized to society.
- Heavy code architecture, command instructions, test counts and operational
  incidents: III and repository evidence.

A short forward reference is preferable to importing a whole later chapter.
No topic is erased from the source record because it leaves Book I.

## 5. Readability, examples and visual plan

For each important equation answer in order:

1. What question is being answered?
2. What objects are involved?
3. What does every symbol mean, with units?
4. What is calculated?
5. What does the result mean physically or operationally?
6. Why is this needed in EBU?
7. Which assumptions and limits apply?
8. What would make the expression inapplicable?

Use a concrete example where it aids understanding. Do not require code or a
simulation before a reader can understand a theorem. Figures should be only
where relationships become clearer: two-account comparison; needs/access and
physical boundary map; steady state versus equilibrium; reference displacement;
Gaussian curve and tangent; finite overshoot; affected-support cancellation;
shared-coordinate group; and the claim-status ladder.

Every visual is labeled schematic, mathematically derived, external observed
evidence with source, or EBU registered result with its exact provenance. No
generated illustrative trajectory may resemble a registered result without
clear labeling. Preserve informative negative examples.

Old introductory practice studios can be integrated into the relevant chapters
rather than repeated after the whole theory. Add a glossary and a compact symbol
table. Avoid symbols before the reader has met the underlying object.
