\chapter{The historical engine and the Onsager connection}\label{ch:legacy-engine}
The older implementation is not simply the new model with a different
potential expression. It has a particular natural drive, flux controller,
permission layer and event order. Understanding those choices is necessary
both to preserve its results and to avoid importing them accidentally.

\section{Why force, mobility and flux entered EBU}
Onsager's primary papers concern coupled irreversible processes and linear
force--rate relations under microscopic-reversibility assumptions
\cite{onsager-one,onsager-two}. The useful modelling pattern for EBU was
to separate a directional driving force from a response coefficient. The
earlier D0 design made that relationship explicit instead of treating the
potential's derivative as though it were already a physical flow.

Its local marginal came from the historical hinge potential. For an edge
with usable-delivery efficiency $\eta_e$, the directional force was
$f_e=\mu_i-\eta_e\mu_j$. The controller requested
\begin{equation}
 J_e=M_e[f_e-\theta_e]_+.
\end{equation}
Mobility $M_e$ controls responsiveness; $\theta_e$ is an edge activation
threshold. It is distinct from Allee or homeostatic stock thresholds.
Neither coefficient is an EBU balance or a source's physical stock.

This adaptation is called Onsager-type because the model declares its own
potential and directed threshold response. It does not establish that the
potential is thermodynamic free energy, or that a social network satisfies
microscopic reversibility or reciprocal transport coefficients. The reason
to use it is the transparent conditional force--flux calculation, not the
prestige of an analogy.

\section{D0's synchronous update}
The historical map freezes state, computes the natural drive $u(x)$ and
edge requests, then applies a synchronous Euler update of the form
$x^+=x+\Delta t(u+SJ)$. Frozen synchronous evaluation prevents a newly
updated neighbor from influencing another edge within the same nominal
step. Sequentially mutating an edge loop would implement a different
propagation rule.

Part II derives the continuous-time dissipation structure and its finite-step
curvature correction. The historical tests concern their registered hinge
model and certificates. Replacing the evaluator with a Gaussian formula
while retaining the old drive and selector would create a hybrid model,
not automatically implement the new programme.

\section{P1C is a permission layer with premises}
The historical P1C classifier distinguishes regenerative and other source
types and their admissibility states. The robust budget's arithmetic assumes
the applicable source classification. For a finite or irreversible stock,
the preservation policy does not become an internal-transfer permission
merely because a helper can compute a positive number.

The distinction between $R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and homeostatic $R$ also survives.
The former bounds export under a source certificate; it does not itself
deliver resource to a destination below its own reference or reserve. A
resolver's protection cannot be used to delete another controller's service
logic while claiming the same function remains.

\section{What is retained and what is not imported}
Keep historical D0/P1C, quote code and evidence immutable. Reuse reviewed
ideas such as complete support, frozen identities and exact finite
evaluation. Do not import Allee drive, gradient-best selection, service-first
comparators or finite-source export prohibitions into the Gaussian random
actor process without a separate scientific declaration.

Likewise, retaining the Onsager explanation does not make it the active
actor policy. It remains a mathematically meaningful controller alternative
and an important part of the project's history. The new process asks a
different question about random affordable actions.

\section*{Chapter recap}
The historical engine supplies mechanisms, proofs and evidence at a known
scope. Its force--mobility--flux architecture should be explained, not hidden;
its controller should not be silently reused under a different study name.
