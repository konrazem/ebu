\chapter{Capacity accounts and random affordable action}\label{ch:capacity}
The new research question is not whether a controller can be written to
move downhill. The Onsager-type example already shows how a particular
response law can have a descent property. The proposed question is what
happens when actors choose without ranking EBU, but their choices are
restricted by a separately declared capacity account.

\section{The policy, stated conditionally}
Let $B_i\geq0$ be persistent experimental capacity for owner $i$, initially
zero. It is not physical stock, installed pumping capacity or a real-world
monetary entitlement. Suppose an explicitly adopted owner/path policy would
assign group changes $\Delta B_i$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\Delta B_i=E_G.
\end{equation}
The proposed affordability condition is
\begin{equation}
 B_i+\Delta B_i\geq0\quad\hbox{for every affected owner }i.
\end{equation}
The intended policy permits same-event signed netting within one owner,
not across all owners. It introduces no loan, refill or global pool.
Its adoption is a modelling choice still requiring the programme's declared
authority, not a consequence of Chapter 42.

\section{Why positive group value is insufficient}
Consider a candidate whose owner changes are $(3,-1)$ and whose aggregate
value is two. With zero balances, it is unaffordable for the second owner.
Positive total value does not cover that owner's debit unless a different
policy explicitly permits pooling. Conversely, if one owner holds both
child actions, same-owner netting would produce $+2$ and pass. Ownership
can therefore change the feasible stochastic process without changing the
physical endpoint or group field value.

With positive existing capacity, a negative-valued action may be affordable.
That is not an accounting error. It is one intended reason this policy is
not a monotone potential-descent controller. Whether spending later enables
useful action or merely produces cycles is a research question.

\section{The external-audit invariant}
Let $J$ accumulate $D_{\mathrm{ext},t}=V(z_t)-V(x_t)$ and suppose
the adopted cost-free updates are exact. Then
\begin{align}
 J_{t+1}&=J_t+D_{\mathrm{ext},t},\\
 \sum_iB_{i,t+1}&=\sum_iB_{i,t}+V(z_t)-V(x_{t+1}).
\end{align}
Adding terms yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
 V(x_{t+1})+\sum_iB_{i,t+1}-J_{t+1}
 =V(x_t)+\sum_iB_{i,t}-J_t=K.
\end{equation}
At reference with zero genesis and zero audit, $K=0$. Forcing does not
directly fund an actor. Its signed potential change enters $J$ so that
the full account can be reconstructed.

After a single shock, suppose $J=4$ and $K=0$. If an actor reduces potential
to one, total capacity becomes three. Later an affordable negative action
could raise potential to two while lowering total capacity to two. The
identity permits this. It gives a constraint, not a ranking of choices.

\section{What can be proved about the reference}
At $x=x^*$, any nonzero net increment has
$E_G=-\tfrac12\delta_G^TH\delta_G<0$. At zero balances, owner-wise
affordability would require every $\Delta B_i\geq0$, making their sum
nonnegative. Thus no nonzero-net action can be affordable there under the
stated cost-free policy. A cancelling group may have zero aggregate change;
its owner-level feasibility still depends on the declared attribution/map.

This is a conditional statement about zero genesis at the reference. After
a shock and earned capacity, the account can permit uphill movement.
Therefore the zero-genesis observation does not establish attraction from
disturbed states or long-run recovery.

\section{Random choice is not mathematical neutrality}
``Choose uniformly'' is incomplete until the sample space is fixed. Uniform
choice among edges differs from uniform choice among quantities, groups or
owners. If one physical option appears twice under different identifiers,
sampling entries gives it twice the probability. A finite menu is also not
the same as the continuum of all physically possible quantities.

The programme intends EBU-blind candidate construction and unweighted choice
among affordable physically feasible options, with a physical-random control
that lacks the affordability filter. Exact menu, duplicate, no-action and
group-assembly semantics remain load-bearing decisions. They must not be
chosen after inspecting which version makes EBU look better.

\section*{Chapter recap}
Capacity and external audit can satisfy an exact conditional invariant.
That invariant does not make the policy fair, decentralized, monotone or
successful. Those are separate statements about the declared process.
