\chapter{From a calculation to an EBU system}\label{ch:system-design}

A useful EBU system connects precise physical information to the decisions that maintain life and service. Its purpose is not to maximise the number of receipts. It is to make restoration, shared dependence and resource consequences visible enough to support better choices. We now assemble the theory around that purpose.

\section{What the account adds to an economy}
Money coordinates exchange, finances equipment and redistributes claims. A payment alone does not describe the physical conditions left behind by the activity it finances. EBU adds a signed account of those conditions under a declared field and boundary.

A leaking water line makes the difference concrete. Its repair uses labour, materials and energy now, then changes the physical capability of the route. A commercial invoice describes payment for the work. The physical account describes the represented change of condition, the resource consequences of the repair and the later delivery records. Where the potential contains a justified capacity or condition coordinate, its present improvement can be valued directly; otherwise future benefits remain attached to future outcomes or to an explicitly named forecast.

This makes maintenance legible alongside production. A community can see why preventing loss matters even when it creates less immediate sales activity. It can also see the burden of constructing the improvement, rather than treating a desirable project as physically free.

\section{Why not simply use monetary prices?}
Money provides a common unit for prices, a means of exchange and a way to carry purchasing power through time. Those functions make specialization and exchange possible without requiring every pair of participants to want one another's goods at the same moment.\cite{intro-money-functions} Contracts and financing can also make a long-lived water repair possible before its service benefits have accumulated. An EBU account should acknowledge these strengths.

The question it adds is different: what happened to the represented physical state when the action occurred? A purchase price can carry useful information about scarcity, opportunity, bargaining and institutional rules. It is not, by definition, the before-minus-after difference of a specified physical potential. The same activity can therefore have a monetary price and an EBU consequence without the two numbers being interchangeable.

The economic motivation includes externalities: effects on others that are not included in the relevant transaction price can separate private cost from broader social cost.\cite{intro-externality} This is a conditional economic problem, not a claim that every price omits every environmental consequence. Taxes, regulations, liability, contracts and public provision can change what private decisions take into account.

Existing environmental accounting also goes well beyond a list of payments. The United Nations System of Environmental-Economic Accounting brings physical and monetary information about environmental flows and assets into an integrated statistical framework.\cite{seea-framework} EBU should be compared with that work as well as with a simple cash ledger. Its contribution proposed here is the particular potential-difference architecture, explicit coalition decomposition and separation of response, registration and history; the book does not establish superiority over existing systems.

A plausible future arrangement could use money for exchange and financing, an EBU-style account for declared physical consequences, and ordinary legal and public processes for rights and allocation. A public authority might use the physical account to explain a maintenance decision without making EBU transferable or spendable at all. None of the finite theorems requires replacing money.

\section{One purchase can have several physical consequences}
Consider a workshop buying an inexpensive cleaning input for the water system. Its financial record includes the purchase and payment. The physical account may need to include extraction of the input, useful cleaning, energy use, residual contamination, disposal capacity and material still in transit. The example assigns no numerical EBU: those values would require a defined field and observed or modelled endpoints.

The useful question is whether the declared boundary follows the relevant consequences. If a cleaner tank is obtained by moving the contamination into an unrepresented sink, the local improvement is incomplete as a claim about the wider system. Once that sink is part of the state and its burden part of the potential, its change enters the same finite difference whether or not a separate invoice exists for it.

EBU can miss the consequence too. Omitting the sink, ignoring a future-relevant pending state or assigning it no justified sensitivity leaves the account incomplete. The architecture makes those choices inspectable; it cannot make an omitted coordinate reappear through algebra.

The societal opportunity is more precise than promising to eliminate externalities. A fuller physical description can help people see where a low private cost has displaced a burden, compare ways to meet the same service requirement, and decide whether a contract or public rule should change. Monetary cost remains relevant to whether the alternative can be financed.

\section{From a physical event to a historical entry}
The architecture can now be stated as a sequence of responsibilities. The physical state says what is present. The current field says which potential and units govern the comparison. A registered action identifies the actor event and its declared boundary. Its response supplies the complete endpoint, or the group endpoints needed for a coalition comparison. Finite valuation produces the EBU amount. A chosen attribution and institutional rule determine any actor entry, which retains its original event and field identity.

\begin{lawbox}{The account has a physical layer and an event layer}
Physical state and current field determine a potential comparison. Registration identifies which actor event is being accounted for. Joint physical value is established before its allocation. The resulting historical entry retains the comparison under which it was made.

Field evolution alone is not a registered action. No registered action means no actor transaction. Current conditions price new actions; they do not automatically reprice historical entries.
\end{lawbox}

The distinction matters during a delivery. Water dispatched yesterday may arrive today, changing the current state. A registered dispatch-and-delivery event can encompass that interval under its specified outcome rule. If the model instead registers distinct physical stages, each stage has its own complete boundary and chronology. Either way, repeated observation of the same arrival does not create repeated credit.

Nor should a more favourable reserve target manufacture an actor contribution. The new target changes the field; it does not show that someone moved water. The physical history may contain the field revision and natural evolution even when the actor ledger has no new entry. This lets the system remain informative between human decisions without turning every fluctuation into a payment.

\section{The physical state includes commitments already made}
A system that records only usable stock can miss water in transit, repair work in progress or a reserve already committed to another service. Pending coordinates make these commitments part of the present description.

The feedback theory explains why this matters. Eliminating a pending stock produces memory and an initial-condition term. Ignoring both can make two identical tank readings appear to require the same response even though one tank has a large delivery approaching and the other does not.

An EBU service interface can therefore show current availability, pending delivery and the horizon of its quote together. This can reduce repeated orders, unnecessary pumping and avoidable overshoot. The resulting efficiency supports continuity of service because corrective effort is directed to an actual remaining need.

\section{Joint projects receive one physical total}
Water delivery, electrical repair and refrigeration can depend on one another. Separate invoices can describe the work, but isolated EBU quotes need not sum to the joint value. The coalition landscape and Möbius hierarchy explain the difference.

The total is evaluated once from the complete group endpoint. Its coefficients show which dependencies are already explained by single actions, which belong to pairs and which require a larger context. The recursion makes the explanation readable: a third action changes a pair relationship, and the third-order term isolates that change.

Where common linear response and quadratic valuation apply, the hierarchy stops at pairs exactly. That simplification can make a large service account less expensive to maintain. Where a nonlinear resolver or plant creates higher-order dependence, the richer model has a specific job: explain a physical effect that the simpler account cannot represent.

This supports coordination without creating an extra reward merely for grouping names together. Shared effects are inside the total, and a separate attribution rule determines how the institution recognises contributors.

\section{History supports trust and learning}
A complete signed history telescopes. Splitting an action does not remove its curvature; reversing a complete state change cancels its fixed-field value; external consumption or disturbance has its own line. These laws make the account coherent over time.

The same history can reveal recurring burdens. Repeated electricity use for a fixed delivered service can direct attention to equipment condition, route choice or operating conditions. The record retains those circumstances so that a change in the field or load is distinguishable from a change in the machine.

Model revisions remain visible too. A new reserve target is a field change. A new feedback gain is a response change. A corrected observation is a correction to knowledge of an event. Their separation allows the system to improve while preserving the meaning of earlier contributions.

For participants, this is a practical form of trust: the record explains how a number was obtained, what changed later and which rule governed any institutional consequence.

\section{A useful incentive preserves essential service}
An institution can use physical information to support maintenance, finance shared repairs or alter future action capacity. It can reward represented restoration while retaining negative entries for activities that consume reserves. The physical sign stays honest; the access rule states how necessary service is protected.

A remote clinic may have no low-burden route today. Denying its users care would not improve the physical opportunity set. An institution can instead identify that route as a priority for public investment, while supporting current service through an explicit rule. The account then helps create a better feasible option rather than blaming people for the options they lack.

Similarly, an efficient household device may be unaffordable to the household that would benefit most. A collective repair or financing programme changes the feasible set. EBU can describe the represented physical improvement and preserve its provenance; the institution supplies the means to act.

The constructive relationship is therefore between information and opportunity. Better physical accounts become socially useful when they help people maintain function with fewer avoidable burdens and when access to those improvements is part of the design.

\section{Efficiency has a physical explanation}
Each major law has supplied a particular efficiency feature. Local cancellation avoids recalculating unchanged factors. Telescoping reconciles many events without inventing new value at each subdivision. Möbius inversion removes double counting of shared effects. Degree ceilings eliminate unnecessary higher-order terms. Feedback memory keeps pending commitments visible. Complete boundaries prevent a local improvement from hiding a shifted physical burden.

These are specific consequences of the theory. Their combination offers a system that can spend less effort on redundant calculation and correction, while giving more attention to maintenance, shared bottlenecks and useful service.

Efficiency does not mean maximizing one scalar without constraints. A reserve has value because it supports future function; a deadline matters because care is time-sensitive; redundancy can be useful because a single route can fail. The state, service constraints and response model give those requirements their explicit place.

\section{A common account does not erase different resources}
Water, energy, habitat and institutional capacity have different physical and social meanings. Several EBU fields can be displayed as a vector, preserving each one's scale and boundary. A scalar combination adds a conversion or weighting rule whose meaning must be stated.

The canonical bridge provides a precise normalization in its physical scope. It does not automatically turn every dimensionless field into an interchangeable currency. A useful institution can still coordinate several accounts, treating an essential resource constraint as a constraint rather than allowing an easy gain elsewhere to cancel it numerically.

This preserves the strengths of a common mathematical language without erasing the distinctions that make life-support functions non-substitutable.

\section{What these capabilities could mean for society}
The following uses are prospective interpretations of the architecture. They describe why its structure may be useful if the chosen physical models and institutional arrangements work in their intended domains. They are neither measured outcomes nor a claim that one potential has already been calibrated for every resource.

\subsection{Restoration and depletion become different questions from expenditure}
Return to the watershed supplying the town. Degradation lowers its ability to support the declared function; restoration aims to recover that condition. A monetary account can describe what the restoration costs. The physical account asks how the represented state actually changed. Expenditure on an unsuccessful intervention and expenditure on successful restoration can be similar while their physical consequences differ.

A complete signed history also keeps earlier degradation visible. Exact restoration under one fixed field reverses the earlier potential change. This helps distinguish maintenance of a functioning system from a story that counts only the repair and forgets the damage. Genuine external wear can justify recurring restoration, so the account must preserve where each change came from instead of treating recurrence itself as wrongdoing.

For public decisions, this offers a way to discuss both affordability and consequence. A community may need a repair even when its financial return is low, and may reject an expensive intervention that fails to restore the relevant function. The account supplies the physical comparison; public priorities and finance determine which feasible repair is undertaken.

\subsection{Infrastructure can be evaluated as a working combination}
Storage, delivery and reliable energy are useful together. Improving an empty store without a supply route may achieve little; increasing delivery without safe storage may merely shift the bottleneck. The coalition table asks about each alternative and the joint endpoint under one baseline. Möbius terms explain which part of the result requires the combination.

The same conceptual question can be asked of transport, housing and a grid connection, provided a suitable physical state and boundary are declared. The joint effect may be favourable, unfavourable or additive. No sign is guaranteed by the fact that the project sounds cooperative.

This helps a public discussion distinguish ``three good projects'' from ``a good combined system.'' Higher-order recursion can show why a third component changes the relationship between the first two. The benefit is more informed coordination and a clearer explanation of dependencies. Who pays and who receives any shared gain remains a separate institutional question.

\subsection{Energy and water decisions can include pending consequences}
A storage device changes when energy becomes available; it does not create energy by shifting the time of use. Likewise, a water order changes pending stock before it changes water available at the clinic. The response model describes those stages, and the carrier account retains conversion and loss.

The feedback lesson is useful in both settings: a controller can repeatedly correct a shortage while previous correction is still on its way. Explicit pending state, or its correctly derived memory representation, distinguishes an uncovered need from one already being addressed. This can guide a choice of controller and reduce unnecessary repeated intervention.

Stability alone does not decide whether the service is satisfactory. A system can settle at a persistent shortage; a well-valued final state can also have been reached after an unacceptable interruption. A useful service description therefore retains endpoint value, timing and access as distinct requirements. The theory explains how to compare them without disguising one as another.

\subsection{Shared sinks make displaced burdens visible}
A town can improve its own immediate environment while exporting waste elsewhere. A boundary that follows the relevant material into its sink can represent that relocation. The valued-sink edge formula makes the arithmetic explicit: the receiver's benefit and the source, pending and sink consequences enter the complete transition.

This can help discussions about pollution, resource depletion and supply chains begin from the same physical description. It does not imply that every effect is measurable or that every harm is substitutable. Where a constraint protects an essential ecological function, a gain in another coordinate need not authorize violating it. Permission and valuation remain separate.

The efficiency sought here is maintaining the required function with fewer avoidable burdens across the represented system. It is not simply improving one visible score by moving its cost out of sight.

\subsection{Long-lived assets connect present action to future options}
A reserve, a functioning pipe or a restored soil condition can matter because it changes which future services remain possible. If that capability is part of a justified present state and potential, the current improvement can be represented there. If it is only a forecast of later outcomes, the forecast remains explicitly conditional on its response model and horizon.

This distinction prevents counting a promised future restoration as though it had already occurred. It also prevents ignoring every long-lived benefit merely because it is not consumed today. The theoretical requirement is to say which present condition has changed, how it affects future possibilities and what valuation is being assigned to that condition.

An intergenerational decision additionally needs views about rights, acceptable risks and obligations to people who cannot participate now. EBU does not derive those views. It can help preserve a legible record of depletion, maintenance, pending commitments and changed capacity on which those decisions can draw.

\section{The institutional questions the mathematics leaves open}
A physical scalar does not choose fairness, political legitimacy, ownership or distributional justice. An interaction coefficient does not by itself identify causal responsibility or credit entitlement. An aggregate capacity invariant does not supply a market design, a tax rule, a monetary policy or a guarantee of financial stability. Human preferences, social welfare aggregation and legal enforcement require their own concepts and institutions.

These are substantial responsibilities, not details to be filled in by an unexplained weight in $V$. An institution must decide who may revise the field, challenge observations, inspect a group comparison and appeal an allocation. It must also decide which rights and essential services constrain the use of the account.

The theory remains useful precisely because those decisions need an intelligible physical starting point. It prevents later choices from beginning with a total that double-counts a joint outcome, omits an in-transit commitment, confuses a changed ruler with restoration or treats a stable shortage as success. People can then disagree about policy while knowing which physical comparison is actually at issue.

This is the constructive role of the accounting layer: a common description of represented consequences that can support better institutional judgement. It supplies neither a constitution nor a command to maximize one number.

\section{The system remains explainable to its participants}
A person should be able to understand the route from a service to its record: what changed, which boundary was used, why the sign has its value and what the institution does with it. The explanations developed in this book are part of that design. A formula hidden behind an unexplained score would lose much of their value.

The physical account, the response model, attribution and access rules can be reviewed at their own level. A dispute about a sensor does not require rejecting the theorem. A dispute about fairness does not require altering the endpoint arithmetic. This makes correction possible without making the whole system opaque.

The final chapter gathers what the theory has established and returns to the opening question: which conditions must an economy continue to maintain so that its success today does not consume the basis of tomorrow's life?
