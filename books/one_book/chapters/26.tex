\chapter{When the yardstick changes}\label{ch:moving-field}

A useful account must be able to learn without rewriting what people physically did. When references or scales change, the potential changes even at a fixed state. We derive the extra field-change term, distinguish it from a change of controller and show how a versioned history can remain coherent through both.


\section{Learning about the world is not the same as changing it}
A community can improve a water reserve by moving water. It can also learn that its previous reserve target was unsuitable. The first changes the physical state; the second changes the field used to interpret that state. Both can be important, but only one is the physical action described by the original receipt.

Let $V(x,\theta)$ be a family of potentials, with $\theta$ identifying references, scales and other declared field parameters. A fixed-field action still has $E_\theta=V(x_0,\theta)-V(x_1,\theta)$. Once $\theta$ changes, an account spanning both versions needs to show that change explicitly.

\section{The full differential has two components}
Assume $V$ is $C^1$ along a regular path $(x(t),\theta(t))$. The chain rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-differential}
 dV=\nabla_xV\cdot dx+\partial_\theta V\cdot d\theta.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-integral}
 V(x_1,\theta_1)-V(x_0,\theta_0)
 =\int\nabla_xV\cdot dx+\int\partial_\theta V\cdot d\theta.
\end{equation}
The total is an exact state-function difference on the enlarged state-and-parameter space. The two component integrals are generally path dependent separately. One cannot discard the parameter component and retain a universal endpoint theorem for the physical-action component across changing fields.

For example, with $V(x,\theta)=\theta x$, the physical component is $\theta\,dx$ and the parameter component is $x\,d\theta$. Moving $x$ first and changing $\theta$ second allocates different amounts to the two components than doing them in reverse order. Both sums give the same final minus initial product.

\section{A finite version makes the ordering explicit}
For a transition from $(x,\theta)$ to $(y,\theta')$, insert the intermediate value $V(y,\theta)$:
\begin{align}\label{eq:field-split}
 V(x,\theta)-V(y,\theta')
 ={}&[V(x,\theta)-V(y,\theta)]\nonumber\\
   &+[V(y,\theta)-V(y,\theta')].
\end{align}
The first bracket values the physical change under the original field. The second records revaluation at the final physical state. An alternative ordering evaluates the physical change under the new field and revalues at the initial state. Both are valid decompositions with different meanings. A protocol must choose and record its ordering.

At $(14,6)$, changing both Gaussian scales from two to four changes potential from four to one without moving water. That three-unit difference is a field-change entry. An actor who was idle during the revision has not performed a three-unit restoration.

\section{A closed physical loop can exploit an untracked scale change}
Let $W(0)=0$, $W(1)=1$, and define fields $V_A=2W$, $V_B=W$. An action $1\to0$ under A gives $+2$. A later reverse action $0\to1$ under B gives $-1$. Returning the field to A completes a loop in both physical state and field version, but the two action entries alone sum to $+1$.

The missing field-change entries reconcile the loop. No material was created and the fixed-field theorem was not violated: its premise of one field did not hold for the action-only sum. Treating those heterogeneous entries as a persistent spendable claim without a bridge would create an accounting opportunity from changes in the ruler.

This example identifies a concrete institutional risk without deciding a universal remedy. A canonical capacity reference, an explicit conversion rule or separated accounts may each be considered in a future design. Each needs a justified meaning and its own analysis. The book does not adopt one by naming the problem.

\section{Historical receipts retain their original meaning}
An original receipt retains its physical endpoints, field version and signed value. A later field can evaluate the same event retrospectively, but that new comparison has a different ruler. Preserving both makes learning visible instead of turning revised interpretation into a claim that the past event changed.

A controller revision is another kind of change. Altering mobility, residence time or feedback gain changes the response map and therefore future endpoints. It does not directly revalue the same fixed state when $V$ is unchanged. Changing the potential weight changes valuation; if the controller uses that weight, it may also change response. Both effects must then remain explicit.

The native tank makes the distinction concrete. Its roots depend on $\nu=2wM$ and $\tau$. Keeping $\nu$ fixed while changing $w$ and compensating $M$ can preserve the dynamics while changing the native burden scale. Observing unchanged oscillations therefore does not establish an unchanged accounting denomination.

For EBU this is an important trust feature. The system can improve control and revise its representation while participants can still reconstruct why a historical contribution received its value. A service operator gains a clearer basis for comparing equipment or scheduling changes, and a community avoids windfalls created solely by an unrecorded change in scale.

\section{A persistent entry and a changing action price}
Suppose a restoration is registered in 2030. Its record identifies the endpoints, the 2030 field and the value computed under that field. By 2040 the reserve target, physical surroundings or state description may have changed. A new restoration is valued under those current conditions. The historical 2030 entry remains the entry that was made; a retrospective calculation under the 2040 field is a separate comparison.

This persistence has a concrete purpose. Participants can reconstruct what an earlier rule recognised without having every change in scientific understanding rewrite their entire history. An error correction can still be recorded explicitly, linked to the affected event. Non-repricing is therefore compatible with correction and learning.

Nor does a stable denomination imply a constant action price. Even with one fixed quadratic field, a litre transferred from a full tank into a shortage has a different value from the same litre transferred after that shortage has disappeared. The unit can remain unchanged while the state-dependent difference changes. Across fields, retaining one denomination additionally requires a justified calibration or conversion; a common label alone is insufficient.

The analogy with monetary prices is limited but useful: knowing the unit in which a price is expressed does not fix the price of every activity. EBU's proposed physical denomination addresses the ruler. State, boundary, field and response determine a new action's consequence. This distinction does not establish monetary price stability or eliminate inflation; those belong to a different institutional analysis.

\section{A common coefficient across fields remains a hypothesis}
The canonical benchmark gave $J_{\mathrm{rel}}=V$ under a particular energy normalization. For more general fields, one might investigate
\begin{equation}
 J_{{\mathrm{rel}},\theta}(x)\overset{?}{=}
 \beta_{\mathrm{bridge}}V_\theta(x)
\end{equation}
with one coefficient across the specified fields. The question is stronger than agreement within one fitted field. It requires independently defined potentials, compatible density measures and a justified common denomination.

A curvature comparison $K_\theta\overset{?}{=}\beta_{\mathrm{bridge}}H_\theta$ can be part of that programme, with accessible tangent directions treated correctly. Local curvature agreement alone does not establish a global identity or a universal conversion among resources. Nor may the potential branch be defined from the probability branch and then presented as independent evidence of agreement.

\section{Why a fixed yardstick is not the final social answer}
Knowledge and physical conditions change. Drought can alter reserve requirements; technology can alter the relation between energy use and service; a fuller boundary can make a previously omitted consequence visible. A permanent freeze would keep arithmetic simple at the cost of a less useful representation.

The differential and finite split provide the alternative. Preserve physical-action entries under their declared field, record revaluation separately, and make any conversion of persistent institutional claims explicit. This allows the account to remain both adaptable and inspectable.

The benefit is a form of continuity that people can use: maintenance history remains legible while the system learns. Better definitions can guide future choices without erasing prior work or disguising changed judgement as fresh restoration. The next chapter keeps the field fixed and asks how imperfect input knowledge affects an exact interaction calculation.
