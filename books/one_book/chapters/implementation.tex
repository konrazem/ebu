\chapter{Making the account reproducible}\label{ch:implementation}

\section{The program must calculate the object the theorem names}
An exact equation can be implemented incorrectly by selecting the wrong baseline, omitting a factor, using requested rather than accepted quantities, or giving a selector information it was not meant to see. More decimal places do not correct those errors. The implementation needs records and interfaces that preserve the scientific meaning of every operand.

The older EBU books devoted substantial attention to these boundaries. The central lessons remain relevant, even though their historical engines, random-affordable proposals and execution statuses are not the active authority for a new system. We retain those lessons here without importing old runtime assumptions or claiming that the present book has executed a scientific model.

\section{Typed records prevent plausible-looking category errors}
Physical stocks, reference parameters, route capacity, signed historical contributions and spendable institutional balances should be separate objects. A cached potential is a derived value, not another physical carrier. A numerical field called ``budget'' is insufficient if it can mean either litres available at a source or EBU an owner may spend.

For one accepted event, the essential record connects the predecessor state, field version, action identity, requested and accepted quantities, complete successor, affected factors and measurement evidence. If a group is involved, retain its children and the declared comparison or attribution semantics. An evaluator should be able to reproduce the value without changing the world.

This design can make errors easier to discover. A quantity with units of stock per hour should not be inserted where a finite stock amount is expected. A later field version should not silently replace the one attached to a receipt. Refusing such substitutions is more reliable than trying to infer the author's intention from a convenient number.

\section{Separate permission, evaluation and choice}
A physical checker determines whether the complete proposed transformation satisfies the declared constraints. An evaluator computes its value. A selector chooses among eligible alternatives under an explicit policy. An execution mechanism applies exactly the authorized transformation. A recorder preserves what occurred and its evidence.

The boundaries matter because the components answer different questions. A physical checker that silently selects the most favorable quantity is also a controller. An evaluator that changes stock while computing a quote is also an executor. A logger that edits inputs until an invariant closes is changing the scientific event.

A future system can combine these components operationally, but their responsibilities and permitted information must remain inspectable. Local valuation does not establish local selection: a group assembler may still read a global menu. A global auditor can observe outcomes without making those observations available to an actor.

\section{A small arithmetic audit can expose a large semantic error}
The four-unit transfer from $(18,2)$ to $(14,6)$ has exact value twelve. Endpoint evaluation, the finite quadratic and the changing-field integral all agree. Returning sixteen suggests that the initial tangent replaced the finite account. Returning fourteen for two two-unit children suggests that independent counterfactual values replaced the group total.

A useful check uses independently derived expected values, not two calls through the same helper. It also includes negative actions, cancellation, unequal scales and non-unit durations. A test that only examines beneficial examples may miss a reversed sign or a hidden rule deleting adverse lines.

For interaction, reconstruct the table from its coefficients and compare with the original entries. Check the quadratic triple null and a constructed nonzero cubic or nonlinear-response case. These are arithmetic checks of mathematical objects. They are not evidence that a deployed system improves service or that an experimental process has been run.

\section{Randomness requires more than a seed label}
If a later registered policy uses random selection, the sample space must be explicit. Uniform choice among edges differs from uniform choice among quantities, groups or owners. Duplicate menu entries change probabilities. A no-action option changes time occupation even when its immediate field value is zero.

Separate environmental and actor randomness when the design calls for independent or paired exposure. Otherwise extra rejection draws in one arm can shift its later environmental sequence. Even common raw environmental draws do not guarantee common applied disturbances: a proposal can be feasible in one evolving state and infeasible in another. The protocol must record which form of pairing it intends and how state-dependent feasibility is handled.

These are scientific choices, not incidental implementation details. They determine the transition process being studied. A future EBU comparison should preserve them before outcome inspection rather than quietly selecting the version that makes an intervention appear more successful.

\section{What the implementation must preserve across interruption}
A reproducible restart may need physical state, field version, pending event identity, attribution or capacity records, external audit and random-stream coordinates together. Restoring only the stock can leave a different future because the accounts or random position changed.

An invocation, a scientific run and a finalized result are different objects. A program can fail before any state changes, or finish calculations before its evidence packet is durably complete. A study needs an explicit operational contract for these cases. The book does not grant a new attempt by calling it recovery.

Hashes help identify the exact bytes of that contract, implementation and result. They do not establish that a sensor is honest or a model adequate. Their role is to make substitution visible and allow an independent reviewer to know which claim belongs to which artifact.

\section{Conformance, evidence and adoption}
A software test can establish that an implementation follows its specification on the covered cases. A mathematical proof can establish a property for every object satisfying its assumptions. A registered experiment can provide evidence about a physical or simulated regime. Institutional adoption requires further judgments about access, harms and legitimacy.

Historical EBU experiments remain attached to their own source versions and protocols. They should not be relabeled as evidence for the new Möbius-generator hierarchy or a new capacity institution. Useful historical lessons include the distinction between a soft penalty and a hard permission, preservation and delivered service, performance and efficacy, and valid null results versus invalid comparisons.

For the present edition, the E1a execution pause remains in force in the controlling record. No trajectory, random stream, calibration or campaign is advanced by writing or building this book. Its examples are constructed mathematical objects, and the next chapter describes possible studies without preregistering or authorizing them.

\section{Why this engineering discipline can matter to society}
A physical account intended to influence access or investment must be challengeable. People should be able to ask which measurement changed, which rule applied, why a group was refused, and whether a correction preserved the original evidence. Typed records, independent arithmetic and explicit information boundaries make those questions answerable.

These features could reduce arbitrary decisions and improve trust if paired with effective governance. They are not a substitute for it. A perfectly reproducible system can still implement an unjust rule; a transparent record at least makes the rule and its consequences easier to examine.
