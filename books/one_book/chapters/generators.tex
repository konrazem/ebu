\chapter{A generator supplies the movement}\label{ch:generators}

A generator supplies the endpoint produced by a declared law of change. Once that endpoint exists on the physical domain, it enters the same finite EBU and coalition formulas as an immediate action. This interface connects accounting to duration, background movement and ordered execution without requiring a new definition of value.


\section{From a map of value to a rule of change}
The previous chapter separated the gradient from the rule that operates a pump. We now give a general interface for a declared motion law. Once a law and a duration determine an endpoint for each control setting, that endpoint can enter the same coalition table. The potential definition and Möbius transform need not be reinvented.

A generator therefore sits beside the valuation chain as a source of physical response. It is not the foundation from which EBU must always be derived. Measured finite transitions, fixed increments and jumps can all have endpoint EBU without being solutions of the particular differential equation used here.

\section{A well-posed flow is needed first}
Let a fixed control vector $z$ specify a vector field $b_z$. Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 \dot x=b_z(x),\qquad x(0)=x_0,
 \qquad x_z({T_f})=\Phi_z^{T_f}(x_0).
\end{equation}
For each control used in the coalition table, require a unique solution through the entire declared duration ${T_f}$, within the admissible state domain. Continuity and local Lipschitz dependence on state are familiar sufficient local conditions; existence up to the chosen horizon and domain invariance still need to be established.

These requirements prevent the endpoint from being ambiguous. The equation $\dot x=\sqrt{|x|}$ starting at zero has nonunique solutions: the state can remain zero for an arbitrary waiting time before departing. The equation $\dot x=x^2$ starting at one has solution $1/(1-t)$ and no finite endpoint at or beyond $t=1$. Writing a generator symbol does not repair either problem.

\section{The generator-to-coalition theorem}
\begin{theorem}{Flow, value and interaction}\label{thm:flow-mobius}
Suppose every required control $z=\chi_S$ has a unique admissible endpoint $\Phi_z^{T_f}(x_0)$, and $V$ is finite there. Set
\begin{equation}
 E_{T_f}(S)=V(x_0)-V(\Phi_{\chi_S}^{T_f}(x_0)).
\end{equation}
Then for every nonempty $T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow-mobius}
 m_{E_{T_f}}(T)=-\Delta_T\bigl[V(\Phi_z^{T_f}(x_0))\bigr]_{z=0}.
\end{equation}
If in addition $V$ and the trajectories satisfy the path theorem,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow-path}
 E_{T_f}(S)=-\int_0^{T_f}
 \nabla V(x_S(t))^{\mathsf T}b_{\chi_S}(x_S(t))\,dt.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The flow supplies a complete endpoint map. Apply the coalition definition and the cancellation of the common constant $V(x_0)$ in every nonempty difference. Along each regular solution, the chain rule gives $dV/dt=\nabla V^{\mathsf T}b_z$; its integral gives the path expression.
\end{proof}

The first statement requires endpoints, not a smooth interpolation across all control values. Control derivatives used later need stronger parameter regularity. Keeping those levels separate makes the theorem usable for a broad range of physically declared response models.

\section{No control can still mean natural movement}
An empty coalition need not leave a dynamical system at rest. If $b_0\ne0$, the no-action endpoint $x_\varnothing({T_f})$ can differ from $x_0$. Then the raw table has
\begin{equation}
 E_{T_f}(\varnothing)=V(x_0)-V(x_\varnothing({T_f}))\ne0.
\end{equation}
It measures the whole interval, including natural drift. To compare controls against that same no-action evolution, define the centered table
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-centered}
 E_{T_f}^{\mathrm{rel}}(S)=V(x_\varnothing({T_f}))-V(x_S({T_f})).
\end{equation}
Now $E_{T_f}^{\mathrm{rel}}(\varnothing)=0$. The raw and relative tables differ by a constant, so all their nonempty Möbius coefficients agree. Their total values and interpretations differ by the baseline drift.

The relative table isolates the action comparison within the declared dynamics. The raw table retains the complete interval change. EBU can use both without crediting passive recovery to an idle actor: the background line and the action line remain distinct, and together explain the physical history.

\section{Historical force, flux and opposition}
The historical EBU force--response chain can be read through this interface. A potential supplies directional information, mobility and thresholds determine requested flux, and complete physical balances determine the state velocity. A scalar quantity called opposition in a particular proposal is one input to that construction; it is not by itself the full vector field $b_z$.

The complete generator includes source withdrawal, pending stock, arrival, consumption and every represented boundary flow. The arrival direction must also include a valued sink when losses are present, as derived in Chapter~\ref{ch:field}. A controller signal based on eventual completed delivery need not equal the derivative of immediate dispatch.

This explicit construction makes response models interchangeable in a useful sense. Different route or controller laws can supply different endpoints while the EBU definition remains the same. Their assumptions stay visible, so a difference in outcome can be traced to the response instead of being hidden in a redefinition of value.

\section{Ordered actions ask a different question}
A flow under simultaneous controls produces one endpoint. Performing A and then B can produce another, and reversing their order can produce a third. These are ordered words, not interchangeable rows of an unordered subset table.

For a concrete smooth example in the plane, let $X=\partial_x$ and $Y=x\partial_y$, starting from $(0,0)$. Applying X for time ${T_f}$ then Y for time ${T_f}$ reaches $({T_f},{T_f}^2)$. Reversing the order reaches $({T_f},0)$. Applying $X+Y$ simultaneously for time ${T_f}$ reaches $({T_f},{T_f}^2/2)$. All statements follow by direct integration of these declared equations. Each sequential protocol lasts $2T_f$, while the simultaneous protocol here lasts $T_f$; the example isolates the stated order and timing conventions, rather than claiming a comparison at equal total duration.

The order difference is related at small times to the Lie bracket $[X,Y]=DY\,X-DX\,Y$. For observables, $[L_X,L_Y]=L_{[X,Y]}$, where $L_Xf=Df[X]$. Appendix~\ref{app:generators} gives the convention and expansion. The difference between two orders is not itself an unordered Möbius coefficient; one must first define a coalition protocol for every subset.

\section{Why the interface matters for EBU}
The generator interface gives the account a disciplined time dimension. A repair can alter what a delivery achieves after an hour; pending material can change the endpoint after a day. Fixing the horizon lets these consequences be compared consistently. A failure of endpoint uniqueness belongs to the response model, while an incorrect potential difference belongs to valuation.

The practical feature is a common account across different physical mechanisms. A community can compare a delivery, a repair and a scheduling change through their complete outcomes while retaining their different timescales and resource boundaries. This supports coordinated maintenance and reduces the temptation to treat an immediate stock reading as the whole future.
