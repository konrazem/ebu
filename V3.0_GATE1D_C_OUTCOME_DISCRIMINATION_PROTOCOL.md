## 3. Why O14 could not discriminate, and what this design changes

The committed post-O14 diagnosis proved that under buffered, demand-capped
bounded service the window service functional is a **plateau**: while
`x_j(t) + δ_j(t) ≥ dem_j(t)` at every window tick (with
`δ_j(t) = dt·η·q_j(t)` the delivered amount), cumulative service equals the
window demand — a world constant. Every O14 feasible world sat on that
plateau for every deficit-seeking arm, so the primary discriminator carried
zero policy information.

Gate 1D-C restores sensitivity **without changing the service semantics**,
using only the three non-semantic levers named by the disposition (§7.4
items 1, 2, 5):

1. **A flow-revealing destination** (DC1/DC3): a destination with zero
   initial stock whose demand rate strictly exceeds the maximum possible
   delivery rate. Under the *unchanged* rule `service = min(dem, x + δ)`
   this pins the stock at zero and makes service **exactly equal to
   delivered flow** — the plateau collapses to a point and the outcome
   functional becomes *linear* in the delivery schedule.
2. **Capacity below multiplexed demand** (DC2): every single out-edge's
   maximum service rate is below the total demand rate, so temporal
   multiplexing — the mechanism that erased O14's capability cost — is
   *provably insufficient* for every one-action-per-tick arm, while the
   full-capability arm A provably serves everything.
3. **Registered demand timing** (DC3): pulsed demand makes service depend
   on *pre-positioned* stock. Deficit-driven rules can bank (their
   thresholds exceed the pulse); the stock-blind rule S provably cannot
   (its indicator reads the current tick's demand rate, which is zero
   off-pulse).

Perishable stock, flow-only service semantics, or any other semantic change
was **not needed and is not introduced**: the sensitivity requirement was
met entirely within `service_v30.bounded_step` verbatim.

## 4. Shared analytical machinery (exact, from the committed formulas)

All decision quantities below use the committed definitions:
`μ(x) = −2α[L−x]₊ + 2β[x−U]₊ − 2χ[R−x]₊`; `f_e = μ_i − η_e μ_j`;
`J_e = M_e[f_e − θ]₊`; quote
`Δe(q) = v_i(z_i) + v_j(z_j) − v_i(z_i − dt·q) − v_j(z_j + dt·η q) − c₁q`
with `c₁ = 0.1·dt·(1−η)` and `z = x + dt·u` (demand-stripped drive).

**Source flat band (exact).** Every world uses the logistic source
`ρ = 0.6, K = 20, R_eff = 8, χ = 1, α = 1, β = 0.5, L = 5, U = 25`,
`x₀ = 12`. Because `L = 5 < R_eff = 8 ≤ x_s ≤ K = 20 < U = 25` on the whole
P1C-protected reachable domain, **μ_src = 0 and v_src = 0 exactly** there:
every force and every quote is a function of the target destination's stock
alone. (P1C holds `x_s ≥ R_eff` within the corrected `1e-9`-scale
tolerance; a sub-tolerance excursion perturbs any force by at most
`2(α+χ)·10⁻⁹`-scale, absorbed by every margin below.)

**Budget floor (exact).** `Q_max = (x_s − 8)/dt + g(x_s) ≥ g(8) = 2.88`
for every reachable `x_s ∈ [8, 20]` (the `(x−8)/dt` term dominates the
logistic variation). Every restricted single request in every world is
below 2.88, so **restricted arms are never budget-capped** and menu
`q_acc = frac·J_e` exactly.

**Flow-revealing pinning (Lemma P, exact induction).** If `x_j(0) = 0` and
`dt·d_j > dt·η·J_j^max` (maximum one-tick delivery), then for every arm and
every tick: `avail_j = δ_j ≤ dt·η·J_j^max < dt·d_j = dem_j`, hence
`service_j = avail_j` and `x_j' = 0`. The destination stays at zero
forever, its force is **constant**, and its window service equals
`dt·η·Σq_j(t)` — exactly linear in the allocation.

**Threshold floor invariant (Lemma T).** For a two-destination world where
dst1 is pinned (constant force `F1`), arm B serves dst2 exactly when
`f₂(x₂) > F1`, i.e. when `x₂ < x̄_B = L₂ − F1/(2η₂α₂)`. If the delivery
rate at the threshold exceeds the demand rate
(`η₂J₂(x̄_B) > d₂`), then by induction `x₂` never falls below
`x̄_B − dt·d₂` once it enters the band, and dst2's service is **full at
every tick** (its available stock always covers the tick demand). The same
invariant applies to arm D with its own threshold `x̄_D` (the root of
`quote₂(J₂(x); x) = quote₁(J₁)`, computed by bisection over the released
quote function at frozen states).

**Capacity-cap lemma (Lemma C).** A one-action-per-tick arm delivers at
most `dt·cap_max` service-units per tick, `cap_e = η_e M_e (f_e^max − θ)`
with `f_e^max = 2η_e α_j L_j` (destination stock ≥ 0). A destination's
stock can never exceed `x_dead_j + one delivery quantum` (above
`x_dead_j = L_j − θ/(2η_e α_j)` its edge is inactive). Hence window
restricted service ≤ `W·dt·cap_max + Σ_j (max(x_j(0), x_dead_j) + dt·cap_j)`
— schedule-independent.

**Isolated reserve-dust lemma (Lemma R).** When an arm's aggregate draw
holds its source at the reserve boundary, floating-point rounding of the
P1C update can land `x_s` one ULP below `R_eff`, making the *next* tick
State R (zero budget, zero delivery) even though exact arithmetic forbids
it. Such zero-budget ticks are **isolated**: during an R tick the source
exports nothing and regenerates `dt·g(R_eff)` =
`0.7320793085917641 / 1.3177427554651753` (many orders above ULP scale),
and the following tick's budget `(x_s − R)/dt + g(x_s)` is
**5.83177131360896 / 5.866035751568388**, above the maximal aggregate
request 4.302 by 1.52977131360896 / 1.564035751568388, forcing `σ = 1`.
The actual 150-tick post-burn-in window needs an endpoint correction: if its
last tick is State R, that tick's forced `σ = 1` successor lies outside the
window. Zero-budget ticks are still non-consecutive, so the worst window has
75 R ticks, 74 in-window forced successors and one other State-P tick with
`σ ≥ σ_lb = 2.88/4.302 = 0.6694560669456066`. Thus the exact
boundary-safe lower sum of arm-A scale factors is
`74 + σ_lb = 74.6694560669456`, not `W/2 = 75`.

For PC3, arm B's invariant band is
`[3−dt·0.5, 3+dt·(r₂,max−0.5)]`, with
`r₂,max = 0.6855794611082868 / 0.7185230299949162`. Stock balance, with
an additional one-tick endpoint allowance, gives at least
`N_B = (W−1)·0.5/r₂,max − 1 = 107.66719939300044 /
102.68491598735132` dst2 top-up ticks. The rigorous boundary-corrected
service gaps are 18.538915 / 28.228544. The registered F4 floors are the
more conservative closed-form bounds
`(N_B−W/2)·dt·η₁J₁ − dt·(r₂,max+0.5)` =
**18.427051868293074 / 28.012116339140597**; these remain below the rigorous
bounds by 0.111863 / 0.216427. This subtraction is part of the static
certificate, not a separate F4 slack interval.

Restricted arms do not approach the reserve boundary: their maximal
sustained draw 2.506 has the stable high logistic equilibrium
`x_s = 14.057914078275521`, approached from the initial `x_s = 12`.
DC2's arm A keeps `ΣJ ≤ 2.71 < 2.88 = g(R_eff)`, so only DC1/DC3 arm A
needs Lemma R.

## 6. Instrument-sensitivity certificate

For each primary world, from the running-buffer identity
`service_j(t) = min(dem_j(t), x_j(t) + δ_j(t))`,
`x_j(t+1) = x_j(t) + δ_j(t) − service_j(t)`:

1. **The service functional is not constant** over feasible delivery
   schedules. DC1/DC3: dst1 service is *linear* in the schedule (Lemma P).
   DC2: restricted totals are capacity-limited while A's are
   demand-limited (Lemma C).
2. **Two admissible schedules differ measurably.** DC1: always-serve-dst1
   vs the B-band schedule differ in window dst2 service by the full window
   dst2 demand (19.06 cons / 34.32 near) minus at most the 3.5 buffer.
   DC2: the admissible a-only and b-only band schedules differ exactly by
   `W·dt·(d_b−d_a)` = 14.391250119927083 / 25.90425021586875 after
   burn-in (the non-selected initial buffers exhaust before tick 50). DC3:
   banking vs never-banking differ by ≥ 5.5 on window pulses.
3. **Margins.** Certified lower bounds (plan `positive_control`): DC1
   S-vs-B `15.5646 / 30.8162`; DC1 A-vs-B `18.4271 / 28.0121`
   (reserve-dust-robust, Lemma R); DC2 A-vs-restricted `7.4766 / 25.3845`;
   DC3 S-vs-B `5.5 / 5.5`. Each exceeds the frozen numerical tolerance
   `1e-9·(1+|v|)` by > 10⁶× and the Gate 1D absolute predicate threshold
   (1.0) by ≥ 5× (declared safety margin rule, frozen in the plan).
4. **The window does not begin inside a saturated plateau.** DC1/DC3 dst1
   is availability-limited at *every* tick of *every* arm; DC2's window
   restricted service is provably below window demand
   (`W·dt·cap_max + x_T0^ub < W·dt·Σd` at both dts).
5. **Temporal multiplexing cannot erase the result.** DC1's A-vs-B bound
   already grants B its best multiplexed case (B must still spend at least
   107.667199 / 102.684916 window ticks on guaranteed dst2 top-ups while the
   flow-revealing dst1 banks nothing) *and* uses Lemma R's endpoint-safe
   `74 + σ_lb` arm-A scale-factor floor. Arm A's dst2 full service survives
   even worst-case alternation (equilibrium `x₂ = 1.9024691358024688`, with
   its floor above one tick of demand at both dts). DC2's cap bound is per-tick and
   schedule-independent. DC3's S has no admissible pattern (informational
   failure), while B's banking is proven sufficient.
6. **Physical feasibility and reserve protection remain possible.** P1C
   keeps every source at or above `R_eff = 8` (corrected tolerance);
   restricted requests are never budget-capped; DC2 is fully A-feasible
   with σ ≡ 1; DC1/DC3's dst1 structural scarcity is registered, honest,
   and identical across arms.

## 8. Arms, capability discipline, and comparisons

baseline), **B** restricted matched non-EBU force rule (primary baseline),

Paired analyses: A-vs-B (capability, PC3/PC4), B-vs-C (F1), **B-vs-D
(primary, C3)** — Gate 1D predicate verbatim on totals *plus* the
registered per-destination channel (discriminator_v2 channel iii) —
S-vs-B/S-vs-D (attribution, PC1/PC2), and conservative vs near-certificate
sensitivity (H7). PC3/PC4 certify discriminator_v2's primary channels in
DC1/DC2. DC3's guaranteed sensitivity is the secondary per-destination PC2
channel; PC2 is not silently relabelled as a primary D-vs-B channel. The
F13-symmetric falsifier F3 fires only if **no** world-timestep pair moves
**any primary discriminator_v2** channel beyond tolerance. Its study-level
antecedent is unreachable if the DC1/DC2 certified bounds hold, so its firing
indicts the analysis itself; H2 records DC3's secondary guarantee separately.

## 9. Registered analysis predictions (falsifiable; the D verdict is not forced)

The threshold model of §5 *predicts* (it does not guarantee) that in
DC1/DC3 both B and D fully serve dst2 at different standing buffers
(`x̄_B = 3.0` vs `x̄_D ≈ 1.30`), so D would top up later, in fewer ticks,
and show **higher** total service than B through the linear dst1 channel —
i.e. the model predicts the under-service branch of the alignment predicate
does *not* fire in DC1/DC3. This prediction is registered **as analysis, on
the O14 boundary-lemma pattern**: its failure in-run is a reportable
scientific finding, not a defect. For **DC2 no prediction is registered**:
the scarce-allocation verdict is analytically unconstrained in both
directions, and that open question — with an instrument that now provably
responds to whatever happens — is the O11 payload of this gate.
