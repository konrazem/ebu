## 1. Editorial decision and its limits

Book I becomes a substantially restructured introduction: the life-facing
economic problem; evidence about access, inequality and environmental pressures;
homeostasis, functional balance and equilibrium; physical state and actions;
declared local reference geometry; potential and marginal field; exact finite
EBU; and the mathematics that follows under stated assumptions.

The Local Gaussian potential is a declared model, not a universal natural law.
EBU evaluates physical actions; a separately specified actor policy chooses them.
The new prospective random-affordable experiment is not an EBU-maximizing or
service-first controller experiment.

Allee thresholds, lower/upper-band machinery, regenerative source eligibility,
P1C details and numerical timestep/error certificates leave Book I's main
introductory narrative. Their existing mathematics and historical results are
not invalidated or deleted. Parts II, III and the later domain-specific dynamics
volume retain appropriate ownership. Honest uncertainty and applicability
limits do not disappear merely because introductory technical detail moves.

Parts II and III are preserved, with a targeted future compatibility map rather
than a wholesale regeneration requirement. All existing PDFs remain unchanged.
Current work changes planning and repository navigation only.

Definitions, algebraic identities and reviewed conditional proofs can be taught
before a new scientific trajectory exists. They do not need an experimental
success gate. Claims of recovery, stationary behavior, social benefit or a
working economic institution do require their corresponding evidence. This
distinction supersedes the old blanket framework-alpha/trilogy regeneration
sequence for the present Book I project.

The complete teaching brief is in
[BOOK_I_INTRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md](BOOK_I_INTRODUCTION_BLUEPRINT.md). A prompt for
a later, separately authorized authoring task is in
[BOOK_I_GENERATION_HANDOVER.md](BOOK_I_GENERATION_HANDOVER.md). Neither starts
that task.

## 6. Claims, editorial checks and publication gates

Every major passage must distinguish: Definition; Algebraic identity; Theorem
under assumptions; Model-dependent derivation; Tested implementation property;
Observed registered result; Research hypothesis; Institutional choice; Analogy;
Open problem. A committed document can itself contain candidates and open
problems; commitment does not erase its internal status labels.

The Book I motivation source register is
[BOOK_I_MOTIVATION_SOURCE_REGISTER.md](BOOK_I_MOTIVATION_SOURCE_REGISTER.md).
Use its claim-specific sources and dated figures, not absolute assertions that
money has no life connection, that all scarcity has a single cause, or that an
economy contains no feedback institutions.

Mathematical gate for the new introductory exposition:

1. exact declared state/action/potential domain and units;
2. all assumptions of the identity/proof stated;
3. inherited statements traced to exact committed sources;
4. new Gaussian arguments labeled as new model-dependent derivations until
   reviewed and committed at that status;
5. no convergence/economic conclusion inferred from the equation alone.

This gate does not require Stage A/B success. The theory book can explain an
unsuccessful future mechanism honestly; its mathematics is not contingent on
producing favorable trajectories.

Authoring gate, separate from the mathematical gate:

- explicit later authorization to write Book I;
- verified original source or authorized new-source mode;
- preserved PDF/source hashes and recoverable baseline;
- section-level retained/moved/rewritten ledger;
- source/citation checks, readable examples and formula review;
- separate authorization for rendering/generation and output paths;
- Parts II/III byte identity unless separately authorized.

Old page budgets (including the 27-42-page Part I addition) are historical
planning estimates, not constraints for a major restructure. Current PDF page
counts, new chapter/word estimates, measured rendered pages and total series
estimates must remain separate. Do not compress explanations or remove caveats
to meet an inherited total.
