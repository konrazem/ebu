## 2. Interpretation boundary

What the existing result establishes: P1C prevented the known
reserve-destruction channel; no source crossed its reserve or Allee threshold;
no profitable persistent reserve-harm exploit was found under the registered
predicate; and the quote-maximizing actor nevertheless served less and left a
worse represented world than the full P1C baseline.

This is **not** proof that the token is misaligned. It is **not** dismissible
as acceptable numerical error. It is registered as:

> **a candidate service-alignment failure requiring a fair, bounded,
> certificate-compliant comparison.**

> ### Preservation succeeded, but preservation alone did not demonstrate useful service.

## 3. Candidate causes, registered separately (no cause selected here)

- **C1 — capability mismatch.** Arm A could execute many accepted flows per
  source per tick; the quote actor was restricted to one action per source per
  micro-step. Reproduced magnitude: arm C took **0.425×** arm A's actions.
- **C2 — quote-coverage limitation.** Only **3.25 %** of accepted arm-B flow
  was quotable under the first-model restriction (mean 624.2 accepted flows per
  layout recorded `unquotable_multi_edge`).
- **C3 — genuine objective misalignment.** Even at identical action capacity,
  maximizing exact local EBU may prefer locally profitable work over
  demand-serving work.
- **C4 — unbounded consumer dynamics.** Standing demand drove consumer cells
  below zero; **all 36** Gate-1C runs left the physical domain.
- **C5 — uncertified timestep.** `dt = 1` against a certificate of ≈ 0.438,
  `r_dt ≈ 2.283 > 1`.
- **C6 — predicate blindness.** The no-action comparator does not classify
  "positive EBU but worse service than a capability-matched P1C controller" as
  an exploit or an alignment failure (mean tail harm −1887.8 confirms the blind
  spot).
- **C7 — interaction.** The gap may result from more than one of C1–C6.

**No cause may be selected without the controlled study registered below.**

## 4. Bounded physical service semantics (design only)

A consumer must never consume stock that does not physically exist. Registered
update order:

1. freeze the current physical state;
2. calculate on-site external input and regeneration;
3. calculate local transport requests;
4. apply P1C source preservation budgets;
5. apply accepted transport simultaneously;
6. calculate physically available destination stock;
7. serve demand only up to physically available stock;
8. record unmet demand explicitly;
9. leave all stocks non-negative;
10. calculate global evaluation metrics only after local decisions and physical
    updates.

For destination `i`:

```
available_i = stock after natural input, regeneration and accepted (post-loss) transport
service_i   = min(demand_i, available_i)
unmet_i     = demand_i − service_i
x_i'        = available_i − service_i  ≥ 0
```

Requirements: requested demand is not automatically delivered service; unmet
demand is never hidden; no phantom stock; no negative consumer state; no silent
clipping that erases accounting information; transport losses remain explicit;
service uses the post-loss delivered quantity; the physical ledger must close.

> **This bounded service wrapper is outside the present V2.8 D0 theorem unless
> a separate constrained/saturated theorem is proved.** Passing a test for it
> will be numerical validation, never proof. (It is the same *class* of object
> as P1K/P1C — V2.8 §11 excludes saturation and projection.)

## 7. Service-alignment predicate (frozen before execution)

A candidate **profitable service-alignment failure** requires all of:

1. cumulative signed EBU **> 1.0** burden units;
2. delivered service **persistently lower** than the capability-matched
   baseline (arm B) — relative difference ≥ **5 %** *and* absolute ≥ **1.0**
   stock unit — and/or unmet demand **persistently higher** by the same
   thresholds;
3. **no sufficient physical preservation improvement** explaining the
   reduction.

Frozen: EBU threshold 1.0; service and unmet thresholds 5 % relative and 1.0
absolute; **persistence window 20 ticks** with the condition holding at *every*
tick of the final window (never a single-tick blip); run length 200 ticks;
burn-in 25 % (50 ticks); paired comparison at identical world, parameters,
timestep, menu and capacity, tick-by-tick D vs B; baseline = arm B; tolerance
`1e-9·(1+|value|)`. **Preservation-justification rule:** a reduction is *not* an
alignment failure if arm D strictly avoided a reserve crossing that arm B
incurred, **or** arm D's minimum source stock exceeds arm B's by ≥ `δ_R = 0.5`
stock units in a world where arm B crossed `R_eff`; otherwise the reduction
counts. Burden and viability are corroborating diagnostics only — the predicate
fires on delivered service and unmet demand.

Reported **separately**: reserve-destruction exploit; profitable
service-alignment failure; action-throughput limitation; safe rationing;
physical infeasibility; numerical/domain failure.

Prohibitions: a service reduction caused **solely** by lower action capacity
than the comparator must not be labelled EBU misalignment; positive EBU must
never be called useful service without checking physical service; no threshold
may change after results are observed.

## 8. Outcome classes and deterministic precedence

Classes: preserve-and-serve; preserve-but-under-serve; safe rationing caused by
physical scarcity; distributive or policy under-service despite feasible
capacity; destructive service; physical impossibility; systemic collapse;
numerical or domain failure; unclassified.

**Precedence (first match wins):**

1. numerical or domain failure
2. systemic collapse
3. destructive service
4. physical impossibility
5. distributive or policy under-service despite feasible capacity
6. safe rationing caused by physical scarcity
7. preserve but under-serve
8. preserve and serve
9. unclassified

Invariants this ordering enforces: physical/domain invalidity cannot be hidden
by a good service mean; source survival alone cannot imply success; positive
EBU alone cannot imply useful work; "did not crash" cannot imply stability;
full service obtained through reserve destruction can never be classified
preserve-and-serve.

## 9. Error classes kept separate

`ε_num` numerical floating-point tolerance (a rounding budget only); `ε_x`
measurement uncertainty in local stock; `ε_u` uncertainty in local drive,
demand, leakage or regeneration; `τ` observation-to-execution latency; `δ_R`
ecological reserve or model margin; **structural uncertainty** — a physical
variable or delayed effect missing from the field.

**A single arbitrary "EBU error threshold" is forbidden**, and a large
tolerance must never be used to hide the Gate-1C service difference.

For the primary Gate-1D experiment, frozen: `τ = 0`; exact deterministic local
observations; no sensor noise; `ε_x = ε_u = 0`; existing numerical tolerance
only; `δ_R` reported separately and never merged into a tolerance. This
idealized study isolates service alignment from communication and measurement
error.

> **Gate 1D makes no real-world latency or robustness claim.**

## 10. Registered (not implemented) Gate 1E: robust quote programme

A future robust quote epoch should bind: observation tick/timestamp;
deterministic local-state commitment; maximum permitted quote age `τ_max`;
`ε_x`; `ε_u`; `δ_R`; equation version; parameter version; expiration
conditions. A quote should expire when `τ > τ_max`, its committed local state
becomes stale, measured local change exceeds its uncertainty envelope, source
configuration changes, reserve certification changes, or the equation/parameter
version changes.

Registered robust physical allowance (already in the V2.9 lineage):

```
Q_max^rob = [ (x − ε_x) + Δt(u − ε_u) − R_eff ]₊ / Δt
```

For bounded adverse drift during latency: `x_safe = x − ε_x − L_x·τ`, with `L_x`
a declared local upper bound on adverse state change. **`L_x` is not assumed
always to exist.**

### 10.1 Interval-valued EBU quote (future candidate)

`Δe ∈ [Δe₋, Δe₊]`, obtained by evaluating the **exact** local transaction
equation over the declared local uncertainty set. If `Δe₋ > 0` the action is
robustly positive; if `Δe₊ < 0` robustly negative; if zero lies inside, the
sign is uncertain. **Settlement semantics are not chosen in Gate 1D.**
Candidate future responses: requote, wait for better measurement, reduce the
accepted quantity, escrow, zero/neutral settlement — **none adopted**.

Uncertain transactions must **not** be silently rounded to zero, and every
transaction must not be shrunk toward zero, without proving the effect on:
telescoping; damage–repair non-profitability; split invariance;
natural-regeneration exclusion; asymmetric credit/debit behaviour; quote
epochs; actor incentives. These are **Gate-1E proof obligations**.

All future uncertainty margins must be physically or empirically justified,
frozen before confirmatory execution, reported separately, sensitivity-tested,
and never post-result tuned. **Structural uncertainty cannot be solved by
increasing `ε`**: if a delayed physical consequence is absent from the present
field, the required additional local state coordinate must be identified (this
is open problem **O1**, the capacity coordinate of the foundation §7.4).

## 11. Hypotheses and falsifiers

**Hypotheses (registered, not assumed true).** H1 observational quote stays
physically identical to its paired baseline. H2 a substantial part of the
Gate-1C gap disappears under identical action capacity. H3 if the gap remains
under matched capacity, the exact local quote has a genuine service-alignment
problem in the tested world. H4 bounded service removes negative-stock domain
exits without hiding unmet demand. H5 a certified timestep removes the
numerical-stability confounder. H6 P1C continues to prevent reserve and Allee
crossings. H7 positive EBU and good service are correlated only in appropriate
feasible worlds, not guaranteed universally. H8 the serving-baseline predicate
detects harmful under-service missed by the Gate-1C no-action predicate. H9
full versus restricted P1C isolates how much of the earlier gap was action
throughput. H10 zero-latency results are a necessary ideal baseline but
establish no robustness under real latency.

**Falsifiers F1–F15** (all reported, never tuned away): F1 observational
quoting changes the physical trajectory. F2 any stock becomes negative under
bounded service. F3 unmet demand is hidden or counted as delivered service. F4
matched baseline and quote-greedy arms have different action menus or
capacities. F5 a paired timestep exceeds the registered certificate. F6 P1C
crosses a reserve while its assumptions hold. F7 quote-greedy earns positive
EBU while persistently under-serving relative to the matched feasible baseline.
F8 a result is labelled aligned merely because no source died. F9 a predicate or
threshold changes after results are observed. F10 any registered run is
dropped. F11 global service, burden, viability, future state or result
information enters the production actor decision. F12 the study cannot
distinguish physical scarcity from policy under-service. F13 a domain exit is
treated as an ordinary valid outcome. F14 a large error tolerance is used to
erase a systematic service difference. F15 a zero-latency result is presented
as proof of real-world robustness.

## 14. Actor-economy pause

**Gate 2 remains paused.** If exact-quote maximization rewards persistent
under-service under a fair, bounded, certificate-compliant comparison, then
attaching survival and access to that signal could amplify the misalignment:
an actor whose health depends on service would be coupled to a signal that, on
the present evidence, may reward serving less. Nothing in Gate 1D implements
health, needs, wallets, death, personal debt, prices, or transfers.

Gate 2 may proceed only after the registered Gate-1D study determines whether
the Gate-1C surprise was a **capability artifact (C1)**, a **quote-coverage
artifact (C2)**, **genuine objective misalignment (C3)**, an
**unbounded-dynamics artifact (C4)**, an **uncertified-timestep artifact
(C5)**, a **predicate artifact (C6)**, or an **interaction (C7)**.
