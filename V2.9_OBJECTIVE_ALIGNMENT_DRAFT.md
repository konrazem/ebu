## 1. Plain-language statement of purpose

The Energy Balance Project's first responsibility is **not** to build a
controller that satisfies all demand or makes every cell comfortable. Its first
responsibility is to provide an **honest physical sustainability signal**.

The intended hierarchy of purposes (author's clarification, restated as the
design target — **not** a proved property of any current model):

1. **preserve** the regenerative and planetary foundation;
2. **calculate and report** the true sustainable allowance;
3. **record ecological debt** whenever consumption exceeds that allowance;
4. **minimise EBU burden per useful result**;
5. **award EBU one-to-one** for *verified net restoration* of the corresponding
   ecological account;
6. leave allocation, emergency exceptions, prices, fairness, rationing, and
   responsibility to a later **regulatory/economic layer**.

EBU is intended to become a **physically grounded sustainability certificate and
coordination unit**, carrying the intended societal incentive

```
EBU intensity  c_EBU  =  verified net physical burden  /  useful result Y.        (1.1)
```

Society is then motivated to obtain the same or a better useful result with
fewer EBU (lower `c_EBU`, higher efficiency `ε_EBU = 1/c_EBU`). Actors may earn
EBU through **verified net restoration** of homeostatic or regenerative
capacity.

> **Hypothesis 1.1 (societal incentive — behavioural, NOT proved).** Charging
> `c_EBU` per useful result and crediting verified restoration one-to-one causes
> agents to prefer lower-burden ways of achieving the same result and to invest
> in restoration. This is an economic/behavioural conjecture about a system that
> does not yet exist; it is **not** a consequence of the V2.8 theorem, and this
> note offers no proof of it.

Two non-negotiable guardrails frame everything below:

- **G1 (no laundering by price).** A high EBU price alone is *not* a hard
  ecological guarantee. An action beyond a non-negotiable planetary boundary
  must receive **no** sustainability certificate, an **UNSUSTAINABLE/INFEASIBLE**
  status, or an **explicit ecological-debt entry** if an external regulatory
  layer authorises an exception. Cost is never permission for irreversible harm.
- **G2 (no laundering by transfer).** Actor-to-actor token transfers may
  redistribute *responsibility*; they do **not** reduce *planetary debt*.
  Planetary debt falls **only** through verified physical restoration or
  verified recovery enabled by reduced demand.

The system must never exploit a source, serve demand, and call the result
sustainable. "Preserve AND serve" is a *joint* success criterion **only where
both are physically feasible**; where they are not, an honest outcome is
preservation with reported unmet demand, or service under an explicitly
authorised exception with recorded ecological debt (§12).

## 2. The quantities, separated by kind

The single most common error this note guards against is conflating a **physical
state**, a **diagnostic**, a **certificate**, an **accounting record**, and an
**economic claim**. They obey different laws and have different truth conditions.

| Symbol | Name | Kind | Units | Source in current code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `x_i` | physical stock | physical state | stock | `d0_v29` state vector; `energy_balance` `x` |
| `L_i` | homeostatic/service lower level | parameter (**service target, NOT a hard boundary**) | stock | `Cell.L` |
| `U_i`, `K_i` | upper viable threshold / capacity | parameter | stock | `Cell.U`, `Cell.K` |
| `R_i` | declared regenerative/ecological reserve | parameter (candidate boundary) | stock | `Cell.R` (used by `χ[R−x]₊²`) |
| `A_i` | Allee threshold | parameter | stock | `Cell.A` |
| `x_{r,i}` | driven reserve root (unstable middle root of `G=0`) | **diagnostic**, state/drive-dependent | stock | V2.7 Thm 4.2 (not in engine) |
| `g_i(x_i)` | local regeneration | physical law term | stock/time | `d0_v29._regen`, `energy_balance.regen_at` |
| `u_i(x)` | local natural drive `s+g−d−λ−κx` | physical law term | stock/time | `d0_v29.natural_drive` |
| `q_request` | requested withdrawal (export) | request | stock/time | not represented as a first-class object yet |
| `q_i^safe` | max certified safe withdrawal | **certificate** (designer/edge-local) | stock/time | **does not exist** in any engine |
| `O_r` | ecological overdraw on account `r` | diagnostic → accounting input | resource-`r` units/time | does not exist |
| `D_r` | resource-specific ecological debt | **accounting record** (planetary) | resource-`r` units | does not exist |
| `Δ_EBU` | EBU debit | accounting record (actor) | EBU | V2.5 ledger is the nearest analogue |
| `Ĉ_r` | verified restoration credit | accounting record | EBU (≙ resource-`r` units) | does not exist |
| `w_a` | actor EBU balance | accounting record (actor) | EBU | V2.5 `EBULedger` (nearest analogue) |
| `Y` | useful result | **economic measurement** (exogenous) | result units | does not exist |
| `c_EBU` | EBU intensity `= burden/Y` | economic claim/diagnostic | EBU / result | does not exist |
| `ε_EBU` | EBU efficiency `= Y/burden = 1/c_EBU` | economic claim/diagnostic | result / EBU | does not exist |

**Reading of the table.** Only `x_i`, `g_i`, `u_i` and the parameters are
physical/law objects that the V2.8 mathematics governs. `x_{r,i}` is a
*diagnostic* of the drive. `q_i^safe`, the debt vector `D`, the credit `Ĉ`, and
every EBU quantity are **new objects** that no current engine computes; `Y` and
`c_EBU` are **economic measurements** external to the physics. The V2.5/V2.6
ledgers are the nearest existing analogue of the actor-account layer, but §7/§10
explain why they do **not** already implement the restoration semantics.

## 3. The safe ecological set

### 3.1 Candidate definition and why it is not adopted blindly

> **Candidate design 3.1 (safe set).**
> ```
> S = { x : x_i ≥ R_i^eff  for every non-substitutable critical source i }.       (3.1)
> ```
> with an **effective** reserve `R_i^eff` (below), and with the *service* target
> `L_i` deliberately **excluded** from `S` (crossing `L_i` is a homeostatic/
> service failure, not necessarily an ecological-boundary breach — §11, D6).

`S` restricts only the cells declared *non-substitutable and critical*. A cell
whose depletion is reversible and substitutable may leave a "comfort" band
without leaving `S`; a cell carrying an irreversible planetary function may have
`R_i^eff` set at or above its current stock, so that *any* net withdrawal exits
`S`.

### 3.2 Is `R_i` constant, or state/drive dependent?

The V2.4/V2.8 code uses a **constant** `R_i` (`Cell.R`), and the reserve penalty
is `χ_i[R_i − x_i]₊²`. But V2.7 Theorem 4.2 shows the *true* persistence boundary
for a driven Allee source is the **unstable middle root** `x_{r,i}` of

```
G_i(x) = ρ_i x (1 − x/K_i)(x/A_i − 1) + s_i − d_i − λ_i − κ_i x − h_i = 0,          (3.2)
```

which **moves with the drive**: `∂x_r/∂h = ∂x_r/∂d = ∂x_r/∂λ = 1/G'(x_r) > 0`,
`∂x_r/∂s = −1/G'(x_r) < 0` (V2.7 (4.2)). Export (harvest `h`, or transport *out*)
**raises** the reserve; supply **lowers** it. Therefore a constant `R_i` is at
best a **surrogate** for the drive-dependent `x_{r,i}`.

> **Candidate design 3.2 (effective reserve).**
> ```
> R_i^eff = max( R_i^declared ,  sup_{drive ∈ envelope} x_{r,i}(drive) )  +  δ_i,   (3.3)
> ```
> `δ_i ≥ 0` an uncertainty margin. This makes explicit the V2.7 **Conjecture C-2**
> requirement `δ_i ≥ x_{r,i} − A_i` over the operating envelope: only then does a
> constant declared reserve dominate the true driven root.

> **Open problem 3.3.** Establish, for the discrete synchronous D0 law with a
> realistic edge-flow envelope, a computable local bound on `sup x_{r,i}` from
> *local* drive/edge data only, so that `R_i^eff` can be certified without a
> global solve. Unresolved.

### 3.3 Per-source-type analysis (this is where a single `S` breaks down)

- **External renewable flow** (`s_i > 0`, no stock dynamics; the "resource" is
  the flow, not a stock). There is no stock to fall below `R`; the binding
  quantity is the *flow*: sustainable export ≤ arriving flow. Over-withdrawal
  draws down whatever buffer exists and is **debt**, not a reserve crossing.
  `S` as a stock condition is the wrong object here; a **flow** condition
  `q_i ≤ s_i` is the right one.
- **Logistic regenerative stock** (`g = ρx(1−x/K)`). `S` is meaningful with
  `R_i^eff` above the unstable root `x_−(q)` of the driven map (V2.7 Thm 3.1–3.3).
  Existence of an equilibrium (`q ≤ ρK/4`) is **not** sustainability: the basin
  condition `x > x_−(q)` and the flip condition `ρ√D < 2` are also required
  (V2.7 Cor 3.3).
- **Allee regenerative stock.** The protected boundary is the driven middle root
  `x_{r,i}` (§3.2), not `A_i`. `S` needs `R_i^eff ≥ x_{r,i}`; below `x_{r,i}` the
  cell is in the collapse basin and regeneration is *negative* — physical stock
  may remain while the source is already doomed.
- **Finite / non-regenerating stock** (`g = 0`). **`S` is not forward-invariant
  under any positive export.** `x_{n+1} = x_n − dt·q` is monotone decreasing for
  `q > 0`; there is no sustainable positive withdrawal. Honest statement:
  extraction **is** depletion even while stock remains above `R`; the correct
  bookkeeping is a depletion schedule and per-unit ecological debt, not a safe
  rate (§4.4, §6).
- **Irreversible / non-substitutable ecological state.** `R_i^eff` is set at (or
  above) the current stock; the safe withdrawal is **zero** and any net use is
  irreversible debt (`D_r^irr`, §6). No price and no transfer changes this (G1,
  G2).

> **Conclusion 3.4.** A *single* stock-based safe set `S` is adequate only for
> regenerative stocks with a well-chosen `R_i^eff`. External flows need a flow
> condition; finite and irreversible resources need a **depletion/debt** account,
> because for them "safe stock level" is not a sustainability concept at all.
> This is a **structural** reason the objective needs the debt layer of §6, not
> only a reserve set.

## 4. Candidate local safe-withdrawal certificate `q_i^safe`

Goal: a quantity an edge/source process may compute from **local** data only
(its own stock and declared parameters, declared local inflows/outflows, `dt`) —
never the whole world, never a global future rollout — bounding the withdrawal
(export) rate `q_i` it may certify as safe.

We must distinguish four *different* bounds; conflating them is the classic
error (V2.7 §3 "existence is not sustainability").

### 4.1 One-tick reserve-preservation bound (local, derivable)

> **Candidate design 4.1 (one-tick bound).** For source `i` with local
> regeneration `g_i`, declared reserve `R_i^eff`, and step `dt`, requiring the
> synchronous D0 update to satisfy `x_{n+1} ≥ R_i^eff` under export `q_i` and
> otherwise-declared local drive `u_i^rest = s_i − d_i − λ_i − κ_i x_i` gives
> ```
> x_i + dt ( g_i(x_i) + u_i^rest − q_i ) ≥ R_i^eff
>  ⟺  q_i ≤ q_i^{1tick}(x_i) := g_i(x_i) + u_i^rest + (x_i − R_i^eff)/dt.           (4.1)
> ```
> Clamp `q_i^{1tick} := max(0, ·)`. **Dimensionally:** every term is stock/time
> (`(x−R)/dt` is stock/time). This uses only on-site data and `dt` — it is
> **local by construction** (it is exactly the reserve-margin form of the D0
> update solved for `q`).

**Limitation.** (4.1) prevents crossing `R^eff` on *this* tick only. Applied
repeatedly it can hold `x` **at** `R^eff` forever, which is unsafe if `R^eff`
is at or below the unstable root: a one-tick-safe schedule can still steer a
logistic/Allee source onto its collapse boundary. One-tick preservation ⇏
long-run feasibility.

### 4.2 Long-run regenerative-feasibility condition (local *form*, non-local *guarantee*)

> **Candidate design 4.2 (logistic sustainable export).** A constant export `q_i`
> from an isolated logistic source is sustainable only if (V2.7 Thm 3.1–3.3, Cor
> 3.3)
> ```
> (i)  q_i < g_max := ρ_i K_i / 4      (strict; = is the fold, marginally stable)
> (ii) operating point is the stable root x_+(q_i) = (K_i/2)(1 + √(1 − 4q_i/(ρ_i K_i)))
> (iii) flip margin  ρ_i √(1 − 4q_i/(ρ_i K_i)) < 2.                                 (4.2)
> ```
> A conservative local export certificate is then
> ```
> q_i^{long} = (1 − m_i) · g_max = (1 − m_i) ρ_i K_i / 4,   0 < m_i < 1,            (4.3)
> ```
> with margin `m_i` chosen so the operating point stays a safe distance above
> `x_−(q_i)` and inside the flip-stable region.

**The role and limitation of `g_max = ρK/4`.** It is the *maximum sustainable
isolated yield* and an **upper** bound on any sustainable export — but harvesting
*at* `g_max` sits exactly at the fold where `x_− = x_+ = K/2` (marginally
stable), so `g_max` is a ceiling to stay strictly below, never a target. It is
also **isolated**: with transport-in (supply) the effective reserve drops
(§3.2), and with transport-out stacked on demand the effective harvest is larger
than `q_i` alone. So `g_max` bounds a *component*, not the realised multi-edge
export.

**For an Allee source**, the analogous long-run condition is that the operating
point stay above the **driven middle root** `x_{r,i}` (§3.2), with a reserve
margin `δ_i` (V2.7 C-2). Because `x_{r,i}` depends on the very drive/export being
certified, (4.2)'s clean closed form has no exact Allee analogue.

### 4.3 Source export bound vs destination service target

These are **two different caps** and must not be merged:

- **source export bound**: `q_i ≤ min(q_i^{1tick}, q_i^{long})` — protects the
  source (§4.1–4.2), local to `i`;
- **destination service target**: the withdrawal the sink *would like*,
  `q_request` (demand `d_j` plus deficit relief), local to `j` and the edge.

The certified safe transfer on edge `(i→j)` is the **min** of what the source
may safely give and what the destination needs, adjusted for loss `η`:

> **Candidate design 4.3 (edge-local certified transfer).**
> ```
> J_e^safe = min( q_i^safe ,  q_request / η_e ) ,
> q_i^safe = max(0, min( q_i^{1tick}, q_i^{long} ) ) − (margin δ-terms).            (4.4)
> ```
> When `q_i^safe < q_request/η_e`, the gap `q_request − η_e q_i^safe` is **unmet
> demand to be reported honestly**, never silently served from reserve (this is
> the P1-vs-P1K honesty rule of Amendment 2, promoted to a *pre-service* cap
> rather than a *post-hoc* diagnosis).

### 4.4 Finite source: honesty clause

> **Definition 4.4 (finite extraction is depletion).** For `g_i ≡ 0`,
> `q_i^{long} = 0`: **no positive export is sustainable**. `q_i^{1tick}` merely
> bounds how long the buffer above `R^eff` lasts (`(x_i − R_i^eff)/dt` per tick).
> Any `q_i > 0` accrues ecological debt per unit extracted (§6), even while
> `x_i > R_i^eff`. Reporting a finite-stock withdrawal as "within safe stock" is
> exactly the dishonesty the objective forbids.

### 4.5 Can `q_safe` be local under the current model? (direct answer)

- **One-tick reserve preservation: YES, local** (4.1) — closed form in on-site
  data and `dt`.
- **Long-run logistic feasibility: local FORM (4.3), but the guarantee is
  conditional** on the operating point staying in the basin and flip-stable
  region, which multi-edge coupling and drive can violate; the *isolated* bound
  is local, the *realised* guarantee is not (§4.2).
- **Allee long-run: NOT cleanly local** — the protected root `x_{r,i}` is
  drive-dependent and implicit in the export being certified; only a conservative
  `δ_i`-margin surrogate is local, and its validity is V2.7 Conjecture C-2
  (unproved). **Open problem.**
- **Finite/irreversible: no positive local `q_safe > 0` exists** by construction
  (§4.4); the honest local object is a depletion/debt rate, not a safe rate.

> **Impossibility note 4.5.** A *single universal* local `q_i^safe` that
> certifies long-run sustainability for **all** source types under the current
> assumptions **does not exist**: the finite/irreversible case has no positive
> safe rate, and the Allee case needs a non-local (or Conjecture-C-2-dependent)
> reserve root. This is stated as a gap, not patched by invention.

## 5. Preservation mechanism — soft penalty vs hard boundary

### 5.1 The two architectures

- **A (soft barrier marginal).** Augment the marginal with a reserve term that
  grows as `x → R`. The **current** implementation (V2.4 `marg`, V2.7 (2.4), V2.8
  `χ[R−x]₊²`) uses the **quadratic** penalty `χ_i[R_i − x_i]₊²`, marginal
  `−2χ_i(R_i − x_i)₊`.
- **B (hard admissibility constraint / control-barrier).** Impose an explicit
  local constraint `x_{n+1} ≥ R^eff` (equivalently the cap `J_e ≤ J_e^safe` of
  §4.3), or a control-barrier condition enforcing forward invariance of `S`.

### 5.2 The decisive observation about architecture A as implemented

> **Observation 5.1 (the quadratic reserve penalty is soft, NOT a barrier).**
> The marginal of `χ[R−x]₊²` is `−2χ(R−x)₊`, which **vanishes at `x = R`** and is
> finite for all `x < R`. It therefore exerts *zero* restoring force exactly at
> the boundary and only a *bounded* force below it. It **cannot** guarantee
> `x ≥ R`: a sufficiently strong export or demand pull drives `x` below `R` and
> even toward `0`, with the penalty never becoming large enough to forbid it.
> A true barrier requires a marginal that **diverges** as `x → R⁺` (e.g.
> `−log(x−R)` or `1/(x−R)`).

This is consistent with the Gate-2 data: in D5 the reserve penalty `χ` made **no
difference** (§11) — not because barriers are unnecessary, but because the
quadratic `χ` term is soft *and* the fixture never stressed the reserve. The
soft penalty is a **nudge**, not a guarantee.

### 5.3 Compatibility of each architecture with the existing theory

| Mechanism | Soft quadratic A (current) | Hard/barrier B (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| V2.7 continuous Onsager flow | compatible; enters `μ^tot` smoothly (V2.7 (2.4)); **no invariance** implied | a log/reciprocal barrier keeps the *continuous* flow in `S` by Nagumo tangency **if** drive at the boundary is inward — needs proof |
| V2.8 synchronous D0 | compatible; `χ` is inside `v_i` already (D0 `penalty`) | an explicit per-edge cap `J_e ≤ J_e^safe` is a **projected/constrained** step — **outside** the D0 theorem (§10) |
| discrete timestep bound | soft penalty adds curvature `2χ` on `x<R`, already in `L_V` (V2.8 Asm 2.5); step bound unaffected in form | a finite Euler step can **jump across** even a divergent barrier: discrete invariance needs a `dt` bound *and/or* an implicit/projected step |
| simultaneous multi-edge withdrawal | soft: several edges can each pass their gate and jointly overshoot `R` | hard: joint cap needs **source-level** aggregation of all outgoing `J_e` (a conflict rule) |
| future P1C proportional scaling | n/a (P1C not implemented) | P1C's proportional source scaling is the natural place to enforce `Σ_e J_e ≤ q_i^safe`; **not implemented, not proved** |
| clipping/projection | orthogonal (`[0,K]` projection is P1K, already outside the theorem) | a reserve projection onto `S` is the same *class* of object as P1K — outside V2.8 (§10) |
| theorem scope | inside V2.8 (it is just a penalty weight) | **outside** V2.8; needs a projected-dynamics / CBF theorem |

### 5.4 What forward invariance would require

> **Open problem 5.2 (forward invariance of `S`).** To claim
> `x(0) ∈ S ⇒ x(t) ∈ S` (all `t`) for the relevant idealised model requires,
> at minimum:
> - **continuous time (Model C):** a barrier or Nagumo/CBF tangency condition —
>   at every boundary point `x_i = R_i^eff`, the net inward rate is non-negative:
>   `g_i(R_i^eff) + u_i^rest(R_i^eff) + (SJ)_i ≥ 0`. This is a condition on the
>   *drive and edge flow at the boundary*, not on the penalty shape alone;
> - **discrete D0 (`dt = 1` or certified `dt`):** additionally a step-size bound
>   preventing the Euler step from jumping across `R^eff`, **or** a projected/
>   constrained step (a per-source cap `Σ_e J_e ≤ q_i^safe`, i.e. architecture B),
>   which is **outside** the V2.8 D0 theorem;
> - **simultaneous multi-edge:** a source-level conflict/scaling rule so that the
>   *sum* of certified outgoing fluxes respects `q_i^safe` (candidate: P1C).
>
> **No forward-invariance theorem is proved in this note.** The soft quadratic
> penalty (architecture A) demonstrably does **not** provide it (Observation
> 5.1). A hard cap (architecture B) is a **candidate design** whose discrete
> guarantee is an **open problem** requiring a projected/barrier theorem distinct
> from V2.8.

## 6. Ecological debt

### 6.1 Resource-specific, non-fungible by construction

> **Definition 6.1 (ecological-debt vector).** Rather than a single fungible
> scalar,
> ```
> D = ( D_carbon, D_water, D_soil, D_biodiversity, D_finite_stock, … ),           (6.1)
> ```
> one non-negative account per **non-substitutable** ecological dimension. There
> is **no** general netting operator between accounts: a surplus in `D_water`
> does not reduce `D_biodiversity`. Substitution between two accounts is allowed
> **only** where a declared, physically justified substitution law exists, and is
> otherwise forbidden (guard against §"accidental unrestricted substitution").

### 6.2 Update structure

> **Candidate design 6.2 (debt update).** Per account `r`,
> ```
> D_r(t+1) = max( 0 ,  D_r(t) + O_r(t) + Λ_r(t) − Ĉ_r(t) ) ,                       (6.2)
> ```
> where `O_r(t) ≥ 0` is **ecological overdraw** (the certified excess of
> withdrawal over the safe allowance, e.g. `∫ (q_i − q_i^safe)_+`), `Λ_r(t) ≥ 0`
> is **attributable additional damage** (spills, habitat loss), and `Ĉ_r(t) ≥ 0`
> is **verified net restoration** in account `r` (§7). The `max(0,·)` forbids an
> account going negative (you cannot bank "ecological surplus" as spendable
> credit against future harm beyond restoring the account to baseline).
> **Dimensionally**, all terms are in resource-`r` units; `O_r` and `Ĉ_r` are
> rates × `dt` = resource units.

> **Definition 6.3 (irreversible sub-account).** Split `D_r = D_r^rev + D_r^irr`
> with `D_r^irr` **monotone non-decreasing**: `Ĉ_r` may reduce `D_r^rev` but
> **never** `D_r^irr`. Extinction, aquifer collapse, and finite-stock depletion
> feed `D_r^irr`.

### 6.3 Clarifications the debt layer must encode

- **Planetary debt vs actor responsibility.** `D_r` is a **planetary** account
  (a physical fact about the world). *Responsibility* for `D_r` may be assigned
  to actors (a separate ledger, §9-C), and may be **transferred** between actors;
  the physical `D_r` is **unchanged** by any such transfer (G2).
- **Physical debt vs EBU token balance.** `D_r` (resource units) and `w_a` (EBU)
  are different objects with different laws. A large `w_a` does not reduce any
  `D_r`.
- **Transferable responsibility vs non-transferable damage.** Who *owes* the
  restoration can move; whether the damage *happened* cannot.
- **Why one account cannot compensate another.** Non-substitutability (Def 6.1):
  restoring water does not un-extinguish a species.
- **Uncertainty recording.** Each `O_r`, `Λ_r`, `Ĉ_r` carries an interval/margin;
  debt is recorded at the **conservative** (larger-debt) end so measurement
  uncertainty never *reduces* recorded debt.
- **Why token circulation cannot reduce physical debt.** (6.2) has no term in
  `w_a`: EBU moving between wallets does not appear in the debt update. Only
  `Ĉ_r` (verified physical restoration) reduces `D_r^rev` (G2). This is the
  formal statement of the guardrail.

## 7. One-to-one restoration credit

### 7.1 The intended rule

> **Candidate design 7.1 (one-to-one credit).**
> ```
> 1 unit of verified net reduction in D_r  ↔  1 unit of EBU restoration credit
>                                             in account r (only).                 (7.1)
> ```
> Credit is **account-scoped**: restoring `D_water` yields water-account EBU
> credit, never biodiversity credit.

### 7.2 What it explicitly does **not** mean

- **not** one hour of labour = one EBU;
- **not** one dollar spent = one EBU;
- **not** that natural regeneration belongs to an actor (an actor cannot claim
  the source's own `g_i` as their restoration — see the V2.0 counterfactual
  test `energy_balance` "natural inflow/regen not credited to actor, impact=0",
  which is exactly this principle at the physics layer);
- **not** that damage followed by repair is profitable;
- **not** that repeated measurement of the same restoration creates repeated
  credit;
- **not** that unrelated restoration cancels irreversible harm.

### 7.3 What candidate credit must account for

> **Candidate design 7.2 (verified restoration credit).**
> ```
> Ĉ_r,a(t) = [ ΔD_r^{no-action baseline} − ΔD_r^{observed} ]_attributable to a
>             − burden_a(t)                       (burden the restorative act caused)
>             × 1[ persistence verified over horizon H ]                            (7.2)
> ```
> subject to:
> - **counterfactual baseline**: credit is the improvement over the *no-action*
>   trajectory, not the raw improvement (nature's own recovery is not the actor's);
> - **burden of the work**: the restorative action's own physical burden is
>   debited, so `Ĉ` is a **net** quantity;
> - **persistence/verification delay**: credit vests only after the restoration
>   persists over a horizon `H` (guards transient/vanishing "restoration");
> - **live-state telescoping**: credit computed against the live account, so a
>   sequence of claims telescopes and cannot be split for extra credit (the V2.5
>   guarded-ledger mechanism);
> - **no double credit**: each unit of `D_r` reduction is creditable once;
> - **damage–repair ≤ 0 unless genuine capacity exceeds the original baseline**:
>   harming then repairing yields `Ĉ ≤ 0` net; positive credit requires ending
>   **above** the pre-damage baseline capacity;
> - **attribution among cooperating actors**: `Σ_a Ĉ_r,a ≤` total verified
>   reduction (no over-attribution across a coalition);
> - **account compatibility**: credit only in the account actually restored.

### 7.4 Relationship to V2.5 / V2.6 (do not overclaim)

The V2.5 guarded ledger already implements **live-state telescoping**, debiting
transport loss and irreversible extraction, and closes several enumerated gaming
attacks (round-trips, splitting, claiming regeneration, reserve sacrifice); with
`selection="physics"` the physics trajectory is identical regardless of ledger
mode (observational separation). **However:**

> **Open problem 7.3.** V2.6's adversarial study found **1 of 12** randomized
> layouts to be a *confirmed profitable persistent-harm guarded exploit*
> (seed 0: +260 guarded EBU while all sources die, viability → 0%; kept as a
> regression fixture, ledger reported **unchanged**). Therefore the guarded
> ledger is **known to be exploitable on at least one topology**, and it does
> **not** yet implement the *resource-specific debt* (§6) or the *counterfactual/
> persistence/irreversibility* structure of (7.2). The one-to-one restoration
> credit is a **new requirement** that V2.5/V2.6 **do not** already satisfy.

## 8. EBU motivation and efficiency

### 8.1 Definitions

> **Definition 8.1.**
> ```
> c_EBU = verified net EBU burden / useful result Y     (EBU per result)          (8.1)
> ε_EBU = Y / verified net EBU burden = 1 / c_EBU        (result per EBU)          (8.2)
> ```
> "verified net EBU burden" is the debit side implied by §6/§7 (overdraw priced
> into EBU, minus verified restoration), **not** money or labour.

### 8.2 Compact-parking example

Two designs deliver the **same** useful result `Y` = *N cars parked per day*:

- **Design 1** — a sprawling surface lot: large land footprint (soil/biodiversity
  overdraw), long internal driving (transport burden, leakage), much material.
- **Design 2** — a compact stacked structure on a smaller footprint: less land,
  shorter transport, more reuse of vertical space.

If both park `N` cars, `Y` is equal, but Design 2's **verified net burden** is
lower, so `c_EBU(2) < c_EBU(1)` and `ε_EBU(2) > ε_EBU(1)`. The intended incentive
(Hypothesis 1.1) is that agents prefer Design 2 because it achieves the same
result for fewer EBU — motivating compactness, less land/material, less
transport/idling, reuse, maintenance (lower leakage), better infrastructure,
substitution, restoration work, and shared capacity/coordination.

### 8.3 Failure modes (documented, not dismissed)

`c_EBU` is a metric, and **optimising a metric is not optimising the planet**:

- **burden shifting** — moving burden to an unmeasured account or region;
- **rebound (Jevons)** — cheaper-per-result use expands total use;
- **measurement gaming** — tuning to the estimator of `Y` or burden;
- **omitted dimensions** — an ecological account not in `D` is free to wreck;
- **monopoly/issuance capture** — controlling EBU issuance controls the signal;
- **inequality** — efficient-on-average can be unjust in distribution;
- **cost-as-permission** — treating a high `c_EBU` as a licence for irreversible
  harm (**forbidden by G1**);
- **metric optimisation** — improving `c_EBU` while `D_r^irr` still rises.

> **Hypothesis 8.2 (behavioural, NOT a theorem).** The `c_EBU` incentive produces
> the desired conservation behaviour. This is an economic conjecture about a
> not-yet-built system; it is **not** implied by the V2.8 theorem and is subject
> to every failure mode above. It must be studied empirically, not assumed.

## 9. Information-storage architecture (semantic, not technological)

Three **distinct** stores with distinct writers, readers, and truth conditions.
(No database/blockchain technology is chosen here — this gate specifies
*semantics* only.)

**Store A — Local physical field / source state.** Per cell: stock `x_i`,
reserve `R_i^eff`, regeneration law `g_i`, local drive `u_i`, uncertainty,
on-site marginal `μ_i`, local flows. *Writer:* the physical update (D0/engine).
*Readers:* the **local decision process** may read only its own and neighbour
endpoints' state + declared parameters + edge constants (the V2.9 §4 information
boundary, enforced in `d0_v29`/`test_v29` group 9). *Evaluation-only:* global `V`,
viability, served demand — computed after decisions, never a decision input.

**Store B — Planetary / resource ledger.** Per account `r`: debt `D_r`
(`D_r^rev`, `D_r^irr`), verified restoration `Ĉ_r`, overdraw/damage provenance,
timestamps, uncertainty. *Writer:* an **evaluation/verification** process, from
certified overdraw and *verified* restoration (never from token movement).
*Readers:* auditors and the regulatory layer. **A wallet transfer never writes
Store B** (formal statement of G2).

**Store C — Actor accounts.** Per actor `a`: EBU debit, EBU credit, assigned
**responsibility** for debt accounts, transfers, claims. *Writer:* the EBU
accounting layer (V2.5-style). *Readers:* actors and the regulatory layer.
Transfers move responsibility and EBU between actors; they **do not** touch
Store A (physics) or the physical `D_r` in Store B.

**Reconciliation.**
- Store A total stock change reconciles via the V2.8 **stock/loss ledger**
  (Thm 8.1): `1ᵀΔx = dt[1ᵀu − Σ(1−η)J]` — a closed physical identity.
- Store B `D_r` changes only by (6.2): certified overdraw/damage in, verified
  restoration out.
- Store C EBU is **conserved under transfer** (zero-sum between wallets) and
  changes globally only by issuance (restoration credit) or debit (overdraw
  pricing) — and issuance is gated on Store B verification.
- **Invariant (candidate):** `Σ_a w_a` may rise only when some `D_r^rev` falls by
  a verified restoration; no wallet operation changes any `x_i` or physical
  `D_r`.

*Auditable* (must be): every `D_r` entry's provenance, every restoration
verification, every issuance. *Distributed/local* (may be): Store A per cell,
Store C per actor. Store B is the shared truth that must reconcile globally.

## 10. Exact relationship to V2.8

| **V2.8 already provides (proved, at its scope)** | **V2.8 does NOT provide** |
|---|---|
| local separable state functional `V(x)=Σ v_i` (Def 2.1) | a hard reserve **guarantee** (`x ≥ R`) — no invariance theorem |
| local marginals `μ_i = v_i'` (Def 2.1) | `q_i^safe` for every source type (only §4.1 one-tick is local & closed) |
| loss-aware force `f_e = μ_i − η μ_j` (Def 2.3) | ecological-debt semantics `D_r` (new object) |
| Onsager flux `J_e = M[f_e−θ]₊` (Def 2.3) | actor attribution / responsibility |
| synchronous discrete update D0 (Def 3.2) | one-to-one EBU **restoration** credit |
| dissipation term `Σ(J²/M+θJ)` and identity (Lemma 4.2) | wallets / token storage / exchange |
| finite-step remainder `R_n=(L_V dt²/2)‖u+SJ‖²` (Thm 4.4) | societal motivation (`c_EBU` behaviour) |
| sufficient timestep bounds (Thm 5.1/5.2/5.5/5.6, Rmk 5.4) | fairness / allocation |
| stock/loss ledger `1ᵀΔx = dt[1ᵀu−Σ(1−η)J]` (Thm 8.1) | adversarial security of the token layer |
| one-tick locality under D0 (Thm 9.1) | any proof for the production **DE** engine family (Asm 3.4) |

### 10.1 Does the preservation proposal extend, constrain, or replace V2.8?

- The **soft reserve penalty** (architecture A) is *already inside* V2.8: it is
  just a penalty weight `χ` in `v_i`, so it **extends nothing** and, by
  Observation 5.1, **guarantees nothing**.
- The **hard safe-withdrawal cap / barrier** (architecture B, `q_i^safe`) is a
  **constraint** on the D0 law: it restricts the admissible flux. As a
  *projected/constrained* step it falls **outside** the V2.8 D0 theorem exactly
  as clipping/P1K does (V2.8 §11 exclusions). It therefore **requires a different
  theorem** — a projected-dynamical-system or control-barrier result — not a
  corollary of V2.8.

> **Statement 10.1.** The preservation objective **constrains** D0 and
> **requires new mathematics** (a projected/barrier invariance theorem); it is
> **not** an extension that V2.8 already underwrites. The debt/credit/EBU layers
> are **entirely new objects** V2.8 does not touch. **V2.8 does not prove a hard
> preservation guarantee, and this note proves none.**

## 11. Reinterpreting the Gate-2 surprises (no historical result modified)

These reinterpretations read the **frozen** Gate-2 summary/trace; they change no
result, label, or file.

**D5 (Allee reserve-pressure comparison) — non-discriminating, confirmed.** Both
`χ=1` and `χ=0` served 150/150, stayed admissible, and produced **zero** reserve
and Allee crossings; final `V ≈ 11.9992` in both. Checking the trace: the source
began at `x₀ = 6.5` (already **below** `R = 8`, so no *downward* `R`-crossing was
even possible) and **rose monotonically** to `19.33`, with source-min `= 6.697`,
never approaching `A = 5`. The demand pull was too weak to drag the growing Allee
source toward its reserve, so the reserve penalty was never exercised where it
matters. **Conclusion (unchanged): the fixture is non-discriminating; it gives no
evidence for or against the reserve penalty.** This is precisely why §5's soft-vs-
hard question cannot be settled by D5 and needs the new fixture D9 (§13).

**D6 (supply shock) — below-`L` service fragility, NOT a reserve breach.** P1
served 200/200 valid demand and stayed admissible, yet final viability is `0.5`
because the **sink** equilibrates at `≈ 3.985 < L = 5`. Crucially, D6's source
cell has **no reserve and no regeneration** (`source="none"`, supply via
`s_extra`, `κ`-leak): source-min `= 4.63`, far from any boundary. So D6's
viability drop is a **homeostatic/service** shortfall on the *sink* (below its
service target `L`), **not** an ecological-reserve crossing. **This is exactly
the distinction the objective demands (§1): `L` is a service target, `R` is the
hard boundary.** To classify D6's trajectory ecologically one needs data D6 does
not carry: a *reserve* on the served resource and a *safe-allowance* comparison.
Under the proposed classes (§12), D6/P1 is **SAFE_WITH_UNMET_(SERVICE)_TARGET**
(the sink misses its comfort band) but **not** debt-creating (no reserve
crossed) — a genuinely different outcome from "collapse".

**D8 (oversized-step control) — control fired; hypothesis clause was wrong.** The
tick-1 overshoot reproduced exactly (`V: 2 → 3.92`, ratio `1.96`). The registered
*monotone-growth* clause failed because the single directed edge's **one-sided
positive-part flux** `[f_e − θ]₊` goes **inactive** once the gradient flips sign
after the overshoot — a `[·]₊` truncation artifact, not a modelling error. The
registered falsifier (tick-1 overshoot) was the real control and it fired.
Lesson for future design: overshoot controls on a **single** directed edge
should either use a bidirectional pair or state the transient (tick-1) claim, not
a monotone-growth claim.

**D4 (logistic feasible demand) — classifier terminology, not a physics problem.**
The frozen §17.4 classifier labelled a **bounded, converging driven approach**
(`V: 0 → 4.578`, admissible throughout, 150/150 served, viability 1.0) as
`accumulation`, via its `τ > 0.05`-increasing branch. The trajectory is not
physically accumulating; it rises monotonically toward a bounded driven
equilibrium. **Preserve the historical label** (no result changes). *Proposed
future terminology:* separate **`accumulation` (unbounded)** from **`driven-rise-
to-bounded-equilibrium`**, using the state-boundedness flag and a tail-slope→0
test rather than a quarter-mean trend on a monotone rise.

## 12. Proposed future outcome classes (for a later preregistration)

Non-overlapping; evaluated in order, first match wins. Defined precisely enough
to preregister, **not** adopted now.

> **Candidate design 12.1.**
> - **SAFE_AND_SERVED** — `x ∈ S` for all ticks (no `R^eff` crossing on any
>   non-substitutable source) **and** post-burn-in valid served demand ≥ target
>   **and** no ecological overdraw (`O_r ≡ 0`).
> - **SAFE_WITH_UNMET_DEMAND** — `x ∈ S` throughout, `O_r ≡ 0`, but valid served
>   demand < target: honest rationing, preservation prioritised.
> - **AUTHORIZED_DEBT_AND_SERVED** — demand served under an **explicit external
>   authorisation**, with `O_r > 0` **recorded** as ecological debt (`D_r`
>   increases); never labelled sustainable.
> - **UNSUSTAINABLE_OVERDRAW** — `O_r > 0` **without** authorisation (or a soft
>   reserve crossed): overdraw occurred and is flagged; **no** sustainability
>   certificate.
> - **PHYSICALLY_INFEASIBLE** — no admissible trajectory serves the demand within
>   `S` given the drive/topology (e.g. demand > sustainable capacity along all
>   routes): reported as the correct physical outcome (V2.9 H8).
> - **IRREVERSIBLE_DAMAGE** — any `D_r^irr` increase (finite-stock depletion,
>   collapse-basin entry, extinction): dominates and is recorded permanently.
> - **UNKNOWN_OR_UNCERTIFIED** — insufficient certified information (e.g. `q_safe`
>   not computable for a source type, §4.5): explicitly *not* folded into any
>   success class.

Precedence note: **IRREVERSIBLE_DAMAGE** and **PHYSICALLY_INFEASIBLE** are
checked before the "served" classes so that service can never mask irreversible
harm or infeasibility (the anti-laundering ordering, cf. G1).

## 13. Proposed D9 and D10 (conceptual design; no parameters chosen by simulation)

> **No exploratory simulation is run in this gate.** Candidate ranges below are
> derived analytically from V2.7/V2.8; final numerical choices are **unresolved**
> and must be fixed by a future preregistration amendment, not by tuning.

### 13.1 D9 — regenerative reserve stress (must actually reach the reserve)

**Purpose:** unlike D5 (which never stressed the reserve), D9 must drive an Allee
source *toward* its driven reserve root `x_{r}` so that reserve-aware (`χ>0`) and
reserve-blind (`χ=0`) behaviour **diverge**.

**Analytic design conditions** (derived, not simulated):
- Allee source with a **demand strong enough** that the reserve-blind operating
  point sits **below** `A`: from V2.7 Thm 4.2, choose demand/export so the driven
  middle root `x_r` (root of `G(x)=0`, (3.2)) satisfies `x_r > A`, i.e. the pull
  actively raises the reserve into a regime the soft penalty must resist.
- **Initial stock in the recovery basin but near it:** `A < x₀` only modestly
  above `x_r`, so the *difference* between `χ>0` (which should hold `x` up) and
  `χ=0` (which should let it fall through `x_r` into the collapse basin) is
  observable.
- **`χ` strong enough to matter but still soft:** because the quadratic penalty
  is soft (Observation 5.1), D9 should **also** include a hard-cap arm
  (architecture B / a `q_safe` clamp) as a *third* configuration, to test whether
  only the hard cap — not the soft penalty — actually preserves the source.
- **Certified `dt`** below the Gershgorin bound (Rmk 5.4), as in Gate 2.

**Registered discriminator (candidate):** reserve/Allee downward crossings and
source survival must **differ** across `{χ=0, χ>0, hard-cap}`; if they do not,
D9 is (like D5) non-discriminating and says nothing — that outcome must be
reportable, not tuned away.

**Unresolved:** exact `ρ, K, A, δ, d, η, θ, χ` and `dt` — to be derived from the
`G(x)=0` root condition at preregistration, not from a parameter search.

### 13.2 D10 — service-versus-preservation map (deterministic parameter map)

**Purpose:** map, over a deterministic grid of parameters, which regime each
world falls into, to expose the boundaries between safe service, safe rationing,
debt-service, and collapse.

**Candidate axes** (each a small, analytically motivated deterministic set — no
random seeds):
- **demand / safe regenerative capacity** `d / g_max = d/(ρK/4)` — the single
  most informative axis; crosses 1 at the feasibility fold (V2.7 Thm 3.1);
- **efficiency `η`** — governs loss and the loss-aware force (V2.8 Def 2.3);
- **threshold `θ`** — activation gate;
- **reserve margin `δ`** (hence `R^eff = A+δ`) and/or **barrier strength `χ`** —
  soft-vs-hard preservation strength;
- **regeneration rate `ρ`** — flip-stability axis (V2.7 Thm 3.2, `ρ√D<2`);
- **timestep `dt` / `r_dt`** — below vs at the certified bound.

**Map should classify each cell** (using §12 classes) into:
- **safe service** (SAFE_AND_SERVED);
- **safe rationing / unmet demand** (SAFE_WITH_UNMET_DEMAND);
- **service with ecological debt** (AUTHORIZED_DEBT_AND_SERVED /
  UNSUSTAINABLE_OVERDRAW);
- **infeasible demand** (PHYSICALLY_INFEASIBLE);
- **reserve collapse** (IRREVERSIBLE_DAMAGE).

**Candidate analytic ranges** (illustrative, **unresolved**): `d/g_max ∈
{0.25, 0.5, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1}` straddling the fold; `η ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0}`;
`δ` spanning `x_r − A` from below to above (V2.7 C-2). Final values derived at
preregistration from the fold/flip/root conditions, **never** selected by
exploratory runs.

## 14. Required conclusions (answered explicitly)

**1. Are we still on the original EBU path?** **Yes.** The objective — a
physically grounded sustainability certificate that preserves the foundation,
reports the true allowance, records debt on overdraw, minimises burden per
result, and credits verified restoration one-to-one — is coherent and consistent
with V2.7/V2.8. But most of it is **not yet built or proved**: V2.8 is one brick
(the honest per-tick physical/dissipation accounting), not the building.

**2. What part of the original idea is already represented by V2.8?** The
**honest physical substrate**: a local state functional and marginals, the
loss-aware Onsager force, the synchronous D0 update, the dissipation identity and
finite-step remainder, sufficient timestep bounds, the **stock/loss ledger**
(the seed of resource conservation), and one-tick locality. Also, from V2.5, a
guarded actor ledger with live-state telescoping and loss/extraction debits — the
nearest analogue of the actor layer.

**3. What exact mathematical object is still missing?** Several, all new: (a) a
**local safe-withdrawal certificate** `q_i^safe` valid across source types (only
the one-tick reserve bound §4.1 is currently local and closed); (b) a
**forward-invariance theorem** for a safe set `S` under a *constrained/barrier*
D0 law (architecture B) — outside V2.8; (c) the **resource-specific ecological
debt** vector `D_r` with irreversible sub-accounts and its update law; (d) the
**verified one-to-one restoration credit** with counterfactual/persistence/no-
double-count structure; (e) the **EBU intensity** economic layer and its
behavioural theory. None of (a)–(e) is proved here.

**4. Can `q_safe` be local under the current model?** **Partly.** One-tick reserve
preservation is local and closed-form (§4.1). Long-run logistic feasibility has a
local *form* but a non-local *guarantee* under multi-edge drive (§4.2). Allee
long-run is **not** cleanly local (drive-dependent root `x_r`, V2.7 C-2 unproved).
Finite/irreversible sources have **no** positive local safe rate — extraction is
depletion (§4.4). A **single universal** local `q_safe` **does not exist** under
current assumptions (Impossibility note 4.5).

**5. What must be proved before claiming that unit actors always preserve the
regenerative foundation?** A **forward-invariance theorem** `x(0)∈S ⇒ x(t)∈S`
for the realistic model, which requires: a boundary tangency/CBF condition in
continuous time; a discrete step-size bound **or** a projected/constrained step
(architecture B, a per-source cap `Σ_e J_e ≤ q_i^safe`) for D0; and a source-level
conflict rule for simultaneous multi-edge withdrawal (candidate P1C). The
**current soft quadratic penalty provably does not** provide this (Observation
5.1). This is an **open problem**; no such theorem exists yet.

**6. How can ecological debt be socially assigned without pretending token
transfer restores the planet?** Keep three **separate** stores (§9): physical
field (A), planetary resource ledger (B), actor accounts (C). *Responsibility*
for a debt account lives in C and is transferable between actors; the physical
`D_r` lives in B and is written **only** by certified overdraw/damage and
**verified restoration** — never by any wallet operation (G2, encoded as the
absence of any `w_a` term in the debt update (6.2)). Transfers reallocate who
owes; only physical restoration (or verified recovery from reduced demand)
reduces `D_r^rev`; `D_r^irr` never falls.

**7. What would falsify the proposed sustainability certificate?** The
certificate is falsified if any of the following is exhibited: **(F-A)** a
trajectory it labels SAFE_AND_SERVED that in fact crosses a non-substitutable
`R^eff` or increases some `D_r^irr`; **(F-B)** a `q_i^safe` certificate that,
applied repeatedly under admissible local drive, still drives a source below its
reserve (one-tick safety masking long-run collapse); **(F-C)** a wallet-only
sequence of transfers that reduces a recorded physical `D_r` (violating G2);
**(F-D)** a damage-then-repair sequence yielding net positive restoration credit
without ending above the pre-damage baseline (violating §7.3); **(F-E)** a
high-`c_EBU` action granted a sustainability certificate despite breaching a
planetary boundary (violating G1); **(F-F)** an ecological account reduced by
crediting restoration in a *different*, non-substitutable account. A certificate
architecture that cannot be falsified by construction (e.g. because it never
emits UNSUSTAINABLE/INFEASIBLE) is itself rejected.
