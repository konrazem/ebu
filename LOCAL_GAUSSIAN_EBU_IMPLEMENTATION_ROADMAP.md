## 5. Foundation checks and their honest execution classes

Static/pure checks may inspect syntax, schemas, units, fixed-state arithmetic
and symbolic/algebraic identities. Importing an arbitrary runner is not a
static check. A test calling a state transition is a transition test even if
it has only one tick and synthetic data. Label those classes separately.

Required later foundation properties include:

- Gaussian finite expansion and marginal, affected-factor cancellation;
- group value versus independent singleton over-counting;
- common-path closure and explicit interpretation refusal;
- at-reference nonzero-net-increment sign, including cancelling-group case;
- conservation, nonnegative physical state and joint constraint handling;
- quantity/rate conversion at non-unit timesteps where rates are used;
- signed per-owner aggregation and zero-genesis/no-overdraft behavior;
- no forcing credit, signed audit and `V + sum(B) - J` closure;
- zero/empty menus, duplicate groups, infeasible subsets and replay refusal;
- independent addressed streams and no future-information reads;
- control parity for physical rules, with exposure differences reported;
- potential-family isolation and no hidden historical P1C/selector import.

Möbius is a later optional diagnostic. If tested, all relevant subsets share a
baseline/protocol; the quadratic higher-order-zero claim additionally needs
fixed increments across those subsets. It is not a settlement mechanism.

## 6. What Stage A and B would ask

Stage A asks about response after one declared conservative disturbance,
with forcing then off. It must allow nonrecovery, spending/deviation cycles,
trapping and refusal to be findings. A finite local action menu can prevent
exact reference reachability; do not reinterpret that as a universal law.

Stage B asks about a continuously driven physical state under a declared
stochastic process. Define whether stationarity concerns `x`, `V`, balance
distribution, the full `(x,B,J)` process, or only specified observables.
The latter accounts may drift even if physical observables stabilize.

Both arms live on a bounded finite-mass simplex under the proposed domain.
Therefore “bounded versus unbounded” is not a discriminating benefit claim.
Recovery, deviation occupancy, service only if actually modeled, refusal,
cycling, distribution and forcing exposure require their own metrics.
