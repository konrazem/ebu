# Demand-driven EBU — book revision plan

**No book is edited by this task and none may be regenerated yet.** This plan
records what the corrected economy requires of the explanatory books, so that a
later authorized revision has a written target rather than an improvised one.

Companion plans: `RESTORING_TENDENCY_BOOK_REVISION_PLAN.md` (which this one
extends, not replaces) and `EBU_FUTURE_BOOKS_STRUCTURE.md` (the eight-part
architecture this material slots into).

**The governing rule, carried over unchanged:** a worked example may be written
only from a recorded result, never from an anticipated one. Since the
demand-driven ledger is empty, every worked example below is currently
*specified* and none may yet be *written with outcomes*.

---

## 1. The correction the books must carry

The single most consequential change is one sentence, and it belongs early,
prominently, and before any actor appears:

> An actor does not choose from everything physically possible. It chooses only
> among plans that answer a demand that actually exists.

Every existing passage that describes an actor selecting among "feasible
transfers" is describing the stress model, not the economy. Those passages are
not wrong about what they describe, and the honest revision keeps them while
relabelling what they are: a stress environment that was studied first because
it isolates the mechanism from the economy, not the economy itself.

## 2. Conceptual sequence to be added

Ordered by dependency, not by chapter number. Chapter placement is a later
editorial decision.

### 2.1 Two kinds of demand

`E` and `P` are introduced together and contrasted immediately, because each is
easiest to understand against the other.

- `P` is read off the physical state. It is not stored, scheduled or
  remembered. A shortfall *is* a need; the need persists exactly as long as the
  shortfall; it disappears the moment the shortfall does. The pedagogical
  image is a field, not a queue.
- `E` arrives from outside. It has no obligation to correspond to any physical
  shortfall — this is the point readers most often get wrong, and it deserves
  its own short section titled for it: **why economic demand need not be a
  physical deficit**. Society wanting sand at `C` is not a statement about
  whether `C` is below its reference.
- The asymmetry — `P` cannot be rejected, `E` can — must be presented as
  **ontology, not ethics**. A shortfall cannot be voted out of existence
  because it is written in the state. An economic request is a new optional
  obligation under consideration. Any phrasing that reads as "physical need
  matters more than economic need" is a misstatement and must be caught in
  review.

### 2.2 A surplus is not a demand

Short, and worth its own heading. It is the step that lets one service test
cover both classes: what you lack is need, what you hold spare is supply.

### 2.3 Arrival is not execution

A demand arriving changes nothing physical. It moves no stock, changes no `V`,
issues no capacity. Only executing physical service changes anything. This is
the conceptual precondition for the capacity-source table in §2.11, and
readers who skip it will misread that table.

### 2.4 One service test for both classes

`delta_G[c] >= q`. Derive that the `P` case reads `x_c + delta_G[c] >= x*_c`,
and draw out the three consequences explicitly: no double credit is even
expressible; there is no partial service; overshoot is permitted, and permitted
*deliberately*, because forbidding it would leave a hostile actor unable to act
at all.

### 2.5 Can this plan actually happen right now?

In plain language, with the source-funded rule given a worked counterexample:
a relay in which `A` sends what `C` is sending it in the same instant looks
fine at the endpoints and is refused, because endpoint arithmetic silently
assumes intermediate physics nobody declared.

### 2.6 Demand provenance, and why no plan exists without a demand

The irredundancy rule is the book's answer to "what stops an actor buying
itself a favour". Present it as the structural guard it is: if an action could
be dropped and the obligation would still be met, the plan containing it is not
offered. Then the converse, which is the satisfying half: in a plan that
survives, *every* action is necessary for something, and that something is its
provenance.

The concrete image: at the reference, in the small sand/water world, eighteen
transfers are physically possible and every single one is offered to nobody.

### 2.7 Random admission, and why it is deliberately arbitrary

Readers will expect a price, a priority or a queue, and their absence needs
defending rather than merely stating. The defence is that any of the three
would confound the mechanism under study: the book cannot ask "what does EBU
contribute?" while a price system is also allocating. Admission is therefore
uniform over inclusion-maximal compatible subsets, blind to EBU, capacity and
the potential, and optimizing nothing.

Include the caution that uniform choice over *subsets* is not uniform over
*demands*.

### 2.8 Scarcity is decided before EBU

The canonical example, and the one the author named: **1000 tonnes of sand
requested where 560 exist.** The request is valid. It is not truncated to 560,
not regenerated smaller, and not topped up. There is no complete physical plan,
so it is rejected on physics before any EBU number is computed. Three outcomes
are then distinguished by name — physically unserviceable, admitted but
unaffordable, served — and the book should hold them apart carefully, because
collapsing them is the most natural reading error in the whole model.

This example is designated the canonical explanatory case for the
demand-driven chapters, to be written out in full once the model is accepted.

### 2.9 Unaffordability is not rejection

A demand can be admitted, physically serviceable, and simply not payable. It is
not erased. It waits, in the sense that it remains an obligation — not in the
sense that anything is queuing it.

### 2.10 Simultaneous obligations, and coupling

Both classes live in one unordered set. Introduce the dependency graph through
the sand-versus-water picture: an impossible sand demand must not freeze an
unrelated water restoration. Then the subtler edge, which is the one worth the
page: **accounts are held by nodes**, so two demands on different resources can
be coupled purely because the same node would pay for both.

### 2.11 Capacity circulation, and closed-cycle no issuance

The two worked fixtures, both exact and both already computed:

- **No net issuance.** `100 -> 80 -> 88 -> 95 -> 100`. An economic action costs
  20; restorations earn 8, 7 and 5; the state returns exactly; the total
  returns exactly. Capacity has moved from economic consumption to physical
  restoration, and between actors. Nothing was minted.
- **Irreversible loss.** `100 -> 80 -> 97`. Two units are wasted, the reachable
  floor is 3, so only 17 can ever be earned back, and the shortfall in the
  ledger is exactly the unrecoverable potential.

Then the theorem in one line — over a closed cycle with no injection,
`delta B_total = 0` — followed by the capacity-source table, whose third row is
the one readers should leave remembering: **a demand is a reason to act, never
a receipt.**

### 2.12 External natural injection

Nature moves stock, serves no demand, and pays nobody. Only verified actor
receipts change capacity. This is where the two legitimate sources of aggregate
capacity get their physical interpretation.

### 2.13 Losses

Loss never destroys quantity; it moves it somewhere it cannot return from. The
book must carry the distinction between a sink **inside** `V`, whose
accumulation EBU charges for, and an **audit-only** sink outside `V`, which
exists so conservation closes and which generates no demand — there is no
reference for it to fall below.

Then the honest consequence, which is not a footnote: in a closed world,
irreversible loss makes the reference permanently unreachable, so "restoring
tendency" in a lossy world has to be defined against the reachable floor. That
definition does not exist yet and the book must not pretend it does.

### 2.14 Restoring tendency in the demand-driven economy

The definitions carry over unchanged: `dV_actor = -E`, `dV_total`, `D_A`,
`D_T`, with `H95`/`H99` as occupancy diagnostics and never as viability
boundaries. What does **not** carry over is any behavioural conclusion. This
section states the question and stops.

## 3. Corrections that must be carried into any revision

1. **Relabel, do not delete, the stress-model results.** They are valid about
   the model they ran. `LEGACY_ARBITRARY_ACTION_STRESS_MODEL_RECLASSIFICATION.md`
   is the authority for which claims may be cited for what.
2. **Do not carry the endpoint-saturation conclusion across.** The theorem is
   preserved; its hypothesis — forcing amplitude equal to the action quantum,
   exact reversal uniquely maximal — is not satisfied where plans are
   multi-action and overshoot is permitted. Any text asserting that an aligned
   actor trivially achieves perfect occupancy must be scoped to the old world.
3. **Do not describe the hostile actor as free to do arbitrary damage.** In the
   corrected economy it must answer the obligation completely and may only
   choose the most damaging way of doing so.
4. **Keep the three inactions distinct.** No active demand, no complete plan,
   and nothing affordable are different situations, and the books have
   historically had only one word for all three.
5. **No Foster–Lyapunov language.** The three reasons it is unavailable are
   unchanged and the corrected model repairs none of them.

## 4. Sequencing

1. Accept the demand-driven model. (Not done; this task delivers the model and
   its conformance, not its acceptance.)
2. Write §2.1–§2.10 — these are definitional and need no results.
3. Write §2.11–§2.13 — these rest on theorems and exact fixtures that already
   exist, and may be written now in the sense that their content is settled.
4. Leave §2.14 as a stated question until a registered demand-driven study
   exists.
5. Only then write canonical worked examples with outcomes, from the case
   library ledger.

## 5. What must not be written

- Any statement about whether the demand-driven economy regulates, is safe
  under a hostile actor, or beats its comparator. No such result exists.
- Any number taken from the rehearsal. It is non-confirmatory.
- Any worked example whose outcome was anticipated rather than recorded.
- Any real-world calibration. Every dimension in these worlds is synthetic, and
  the sand and water are names, not quantities of anything.
